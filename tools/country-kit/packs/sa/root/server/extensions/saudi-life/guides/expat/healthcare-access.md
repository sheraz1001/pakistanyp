# Healthcare Access for Expats

SaudiYP is not a government service; complete official procedures only on the official platforms linked below.

Most expat residents in Saudi Arabia access healthcare through **private health insurance** provided by their employer, regulated by the Council of Health Insurance (CHI). The Ministry of Health (MOH) runs public facilities and the **Sehhaty** digital platform, which is open to residents and their dependants.

## Who this applies to

- **Private-sector employees:** CHI rules make it mandatory for employers to provide a health insurance policy covering all workers **and their dependants**, including during probation.
- **Dependants** are defined by CHI as a spouse, sons up to age 25 and unmarried daughters (plus orphans fostered by the family).
- **Domestic workers:** health insurance has also been made mandatory for domestic workers under CHI rules.
- **Visitors** are covered by visitor/tourist insurance policies, not by an employer's policy.

## What you need

- Your Iqama and your insurance card or policy details from your employer.
- An **Absher account** to register on Sehhaty, per MOH.
- For dependants: confirm with HR that each family member is added to your policy.

## Steps

1. **Confirm your cover:** ask HR for your policy class, network of providers and any co-payment. Cover for non-Saudis starts from the date of service transfer or entry into the Kingdom, provided this happens within ten working days of entry ([CHI](https://www.chi.gov.sa)).
2. **For routine care,** visit a provider in your insurer's network and show your Iqama/insurance details.
3. **Use Sehhaty:** register with your Absher credentials in the official app, add dependants to the same health centre, book appointments, and access virtual clinic services and vaccinations.
4. **Call MOH 937** for medical advice, appointments, complaints or help with Sehhaty.
5. **If a claim is refused,** raise it with the insurer first, then file a complaint with CHI.

## Fees and timing

- **Sehhaty appointment booking:** MOH lists it as free of charge ([MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/eservices/sehhaty/pages/appointment-booking-on-sehhaty.aspx)).
- **Insurance premiums** are paid by the employer for workers and eligible dependants. Under the health insurance law, an employer who fails to insure or pay premiums must pay the outstanding premiums plus a fine ([CHI](https://www.chi.gov.sa/en/aboutchi/Pages/FAQ.aspx)).
- **Co-payments and deductibles** depend on your policy; check the policy schedule.
- The **Essential Benefits Package** published by CHI sets minimum cover, including examinations, diagnosis, treatment, medicines, preventive care such as vaccinations, and hospitalisation including surgery and delivery.

## Common questions

**My employer hasn't added my wife and children. Is that allowed?**
CHI requires the employer's policy to cover workers and their eligible dependants. Raise it with HR and, if unresolved, with CHI.

**Can I use government hospitals?**
Sehhaty serves all citizens and residents for booking at MOH primary health centres. For hospital care, check what your insurance covers and where.

**Does my insurance continue when I change jobs?**
Cover under the old employer's policy ends when you transfer to a new employer; the new employer must insure you.

**What about emergencies?**
In an emergency, go to the nearest emergency department or call the emergency services. Check your policy for emergency cover terms.
