# Getting a Mobile SIM

SaudiYP is not a government service; complete official procedures only on the official platforms linked below.

Mobile services in Saudi Arabia are regulated by the Communications, Space and Technology Commission (CST, formerly CITC). Every SIM, whether prepaid, postpaid or data-only, must be registered to a verified identity.

## Who this applies to

- Residents with an Iqama (and visitors with a passport and visa) who want a Saudi mobile or data SIM from a licensed operator.
- Anyone who wants to check which numbers are registered under their ID.

## What you need

- Your Iqama (or passport and entry visa for visitors).
- **Biometric (fingerprint) verification** at the point of sale. CST has required fingerprint or Absher-based verification for prepaid, postpaid and data SIMs since January 2016.
- If you cannot provide fingerprints, CST's **SIM authentication code** service provides an alternative route; the code is valid for four hours and can be used to issue new or additional SIMs.

## Steps

1. **Choose a licensed operator** and a plan (prepaid or postpaid; physical SIM or eSIM where offered).
2. **Visit an operator store or authorised outlet,** or use the operator's official app where it supports digital identity verification.
3. **Verify your identity** with your Iqama and fingerprint (or the CST authentication code).
4. **Activate the line** and keep the contract or receipt.
5. **Check your registered numbers** regularly with CST's free "My Numbers" (Arqami) service, which shows the numbers and data SIMs registered under your ID and their verification status. Report any number you do not recognise to the operator.
6. **When leaving the Kingdom,** cancel or transfer lines registered in your name.

## Fees and timing

- CST has stated that the fingerprint verification service is **free** ([CST](https://www.cst.gov.sa)).
- The "My Numbers" service is free.
- SIM, plan and eSIM prices are set by each operator and shown in their official channels.
- Unverified SIMs are subject to suspension and then disconnection, according to CST announcements.

## Common questions

**How many SIMs can I have in my name?**
CST has not set a figure that we could confirm on its public pages. Operators apply CST's rules at the point of sale; use "My Numbers" to see how many you have.

**Someone registered a number under my Iqama. What should I do?**
Check with "My Numbers", then contact the operator to cancel it and file a complaint with CST if the operator does not resolve it.

**Can I use my home-country SIM?**
International roaming works under your home operator's terms. For a local number, you need a Saudi SIM registered to your identity.

**Do I need a Saudi number for government services?**
Absher, banks and many services send verification codes to a Saudi mobile number registered in your name, so keep your number updated on those platforms.
