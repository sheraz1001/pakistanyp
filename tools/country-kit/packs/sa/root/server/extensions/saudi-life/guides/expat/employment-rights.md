# Employment Rights for Expat Workers

SaudiYP is not a government service; complete official procedures only on the official platforms linked below.

Private-sector employment in Saudi Arabia is governed by the Labor Law, supervised by the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD). Amendments to the law came into force on 19 February 2025, revising 38 articles ([MHRSD](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa)). This guide summarises the main rights; it is not legal advice.

## Who this applies to

- Expat employees of private-sector companies with a work permit and Iqama.
- **Domestic workers** (drivers, housekeepers and similar) are covered by separate regulations and the Musaned platform, not by every rule below.

## What you need

- Access to your individual **Qiwa** account to view your employment contract.
- Copies of your payslips or bank statements showing salary payments.

## Steps

1. **Check your contract on Qiwa.** Employers create and document contracts electronically; you can approve, reject or request changes from your Qiwa individual account. Once both parties agree, the contract is considered documented with MHRSD.
2. **Know the core rules** (see below) and keep records of hours, leave and pay.
3. **Raise issues with your employer first,** in writing where possible.
4. **If unresolved, file a labour complaint** through MHRSD's services. Disputes go to a friendly-settlement stage before reaching the labour courts; MHRSD reports that most complaints are resolved at that stage.

## Key rights (summary of official sources)

- **Probation:** may not exceed 90 days, extendable by written agreement up to a maximum of 180 days ([MHRSD amendments](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2025-03/Amendments%20to%20Labor%20Law%20Articles.pdf)).
- **Notice for indefinite contracts:** for monthly-paid workers, at least 30 days if the employee ends the contract and 60 days if the employer does; 30 days for other workers ([MHRSD amendments](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2025-03/Amendments%20to%20Labor%20Law%20Articles.pdf)).
- **Working hours:** normally up to 8 hours a day or 48 hours a week; reduced to 6 hours a day / 36 a week for Muslim workers in Ramadan ([Labor Law](https://www.hrsd.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2023-02/Labor.pdf)).
- **Annual leave:** at least 21 days paid, rising to at least 30 days after five consecutive years with the same employer.
- **Wages:** private-sector salaries are paid electronically and monitored under the Wage Protection Program.
- **Fees:** the employer bears Iqama, work-permit and renewal fees.
- **End-of-service award:** half a month's wage for each of the first five years and one month's wage for each later year, based on the last wage; partial years are pro-rated. On resignation, the award is reduced (one-third after 2–5 years, two-thirds after 5–10 years, full after 10 years) ([MHRSD Labor Education](https://laboreducation.hrsd.gov.sa/en/labor-education/317)). No award is due if the contract ends during probation.

## Fees and timing

- Filing a labour complaint through MHRSD's electronic services is shown on the platform; check the service page for any fee and current timelines.
- MHRSD lists a processing time for its friendly-settlement service on the service page.

## Common questions

**Does my employer have to provide health insurance?**
Yes. Council of Health Insurance rules require employers to insure workers and their eligible dependants, including during probation.

**My salary is late. What can I do?**
Raise it with HR, then file a complaint with MHRSD. Wage payments are tracked through the Wage Protection Program.

**Is my end-of-service calculated on basic or total salary?**
MHRSD's Labor Education FAQ explains which wage is used; check it for your situation and contract.

**Do the 2025 amendments apply to my existing contract?**
The amendments took effect on 19 February 2025. How they apply to a specific contract should be checked with MHRSD.
