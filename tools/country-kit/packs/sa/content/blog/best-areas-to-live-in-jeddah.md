The best areas to live in Jeddah for most families are in the north and north-centre of the city: Al Rawdah, Al Zahra, Al Shati, Al Salamah, Al Naeem and Al Muhammadiyah. People who want the sea and space often choose Obhur, further north on the Red Sea creek. Al Hamra and Al Andalus put you close to the Corniche and central offices, while Al Safa, Al Marwah and Al Bawadi offer more affordable rents.

Jeddah, the largest city in the [Makkah region](/makkah), is a long coastal city, so the real question is how far north or south you can live from your work and schools. This guide compares the main districts by lifestyle, budget and commute, and explains what to check before you sign a lease.

:::takeaways
- North-central districts such as Al Rawdah, Al Zahra and Al Shati are the most popular all-round choice for families.
- Obhur offers waterfront living and larger homes, but it is a long drive from the south and centre.
- Al Hamra and Al Andalus suit professionals who want to be near the Corniche and central offices.
- Al Safa, Al Marwah, Al Bawadi and Al Rehab usually offer better value.
- As of mid-2026, the five-year rent freeze applies to Riyadh only, so Jeddah rents still follow the market.
:::

## How to choose the best area to live in Jeddah

Jeddah stretches along the Red Sea, and the main roads, such as King Abdulaziz Road, Madinah Road and Prince Mohammed bin Abdulaziz Road (Tahlia), run north to south. Commute times depend on how many of those north–south kilometres you cover at rush hour. Humidity is high for much of the year, so good air conditioning and parking near the door matter more than you might expect.

## North-central Jeddah: Al Rawdah, Al Zahra, Al Shati and Al Salamah

This band of districts is where many families and professionals settle. It is well served by supermarkets, clinics, international and private [schools](/categories/schools), malls and restaurants, and it sits between the centre and the north.

### What each district offers

- **Al Rawdah:** a central, popular residential district with good access to Tahlia Street and major roads.
- **Al Zahra:** established villas and apartments, close to schools and shopping.
- **Al Shati:** close to the northern Corniche and the sea, with some of the city's more upscale housing.
- **Al Salamah and Al Naeem:** residential districts with a mix of apartments and villas, popular with families.
- **Al Muhammadiyah:** further north, with newer buildings and access to the northern parts of the city.

Listing data from mid-2026 suggests asking rents in these districts are well above the city median. Expect to pay more for newer buildings and sea views, but for many families these remain the best areas to live in Jeddah.

> [!TIP]
> Humidity is hard on buildings. Look for signs of damp, check AC drainage and ask when the units were last serviced. Ask the landlord to put any repair promises in the contract.

## Obhur: waterfront living in north Jeddah

Obhur (also written Abhur) sits around the creek at the northern end of the city. It is known for beaches, marinas, water sports and larger villas, and it is popular with families who want space and the sea at weekends.

The trade-off is distance. Obhur is a long drive from central and southern Jeddah, and services are more spread out than in the centre. It works best if you work in the north, near the airport, or can avoid peak-hour traffic. If you enjoy the sea, look at [water sports and marine services](/categories/water-sports-marine) in the area.

## Central Jeddah: Al Hamra, Al Andalus and Al Khalidiyah

Al Hamra and Al Andalus lie near the central Corniche, with many offices, consulates, hotels and apartment towers. Al Khalidiyah sits slightly inland with established villas and shops.

These districts suit professionals and couples who want a short commute and the seafront within easy reach. Apartments are more common than villas, and buildings range from older blocks to new towers. Parking can be tight in busy streets, so ask whether a space is included.

Further south, Al Balad, Jeddah's historic old town, has been on the UNESCO World Heritage List since 2014. It is a great place to visit, with traditional coral-stone houses and souks, but most residents choose more modern districts for daily life.

## Value districts: Al Safa, Al Marwah, Al Bawadi and Al Rehab

If budget is the priority, several established districts offer good value without being far from the centre:

- **Al Safa and Al Marwah:** residential districts in the east of the city, with schools, mosques and local shops.
- **Al Bawadi and Al Faisaliyah:** central-east districts with a wide range of older and mid-range apartments.
- **Al Rehab:** popular with families looking for value. Nearby Al Nahda, by contrast, has larger and more expensive homes.

Mid-2026 listing data shows that a sizeable share of Jeddah apartment listings fall under SAR 30,000 a year, mostly in districts like these. Older buildings can need more maintenance, so inspect carefully.

## Renting in Jeddah: rules and checks

Rental contracts in Saudi Arabia should be registered on Ejar, the official rental platform. Rent is usually paid annually or in instalments. Unlike Riyadh, Jeddah is not covered by the five-year rent freeze as of mid-2026, although the Real Estate General Authority has said the framework can be extended to other cities with government approval. Read our [explainer on the Riyadh rent freeze](/news/riyadh-rent-freeze-what-tenants-landlords-need-to-know) to understand how it could apply.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not pay rent or a deposit until the contract is registered on Ejar. Check that the landlord's name and the property details match the title deed before you sign.

:::checklist
- Inspect AC units, drainage and signs of damp
- Confirm parking, water and maintenance costs
- Check the drive to work and school at rush hour
- Agree the rent, increases and payment schedule in writing
- Register the contract on Ejar before paying
- Photograph the property's condition on move-in day
- Book [movers](/categories/moving-storage) once the contract is registered
:::

The [expat guide to renting accommodation](/expat-guide/renting-accommodation) explains deposits, contracts and Ejar step by step. You can also search [residential rentals](/categories/residential-rentals) and [compounds and furnished apartments](/categories/compounds-furnished-apartments) across the city.

:::faq
### Which is the best area to live in Jeddah for families?
Al Rawdah, Al Zahra, Al Shati, Al Salamah and Al Naeem are popular with families because they offer good schools, shops and clinics close to home. Obhur suits families who want a larger home near the sea and do not mind a longer commute. Families on a tighter budget often choose Al Safa, Al Marwah or Al Rehab.

### Where do expats live in Jeddah?
Many expatriates live in north-central districts such as Al Rawdah, Al Zahra and Al Shati, in Obhur, or in residential compounds across the north of the city. Professionals working in the centre often choose Al Hamra or Al Andalus near the Corniche. The best choice depends mainly on the location of the office and the children's school.

### What is the cheapest area to live in Jeddah?
Districts such as Al Bawadi, Al Faisaliyah, Al Safa and parts of the south and east generally have lower rents. Listing data from mid-2026 shows a significant share of Jeddah apartments advertised under SAR 30,000 a year, mostly in such districts. Lower rents often mean older buildings, so inspect the property carefully before signing.

### Is Obhur a good place to live?
Obhur is excellent if you value the sea, beaches, marinas and larger homes. It is popular with families and water-sports enthusiasts. The main downside is distance: it is far from the centre and the south, so daily commutes can be long. It works best if you work in north Jeddah or have flexible hours.

### How much is rent in Jeddah?
Rents vary widely by district and building. Listing data from mid-2026 put the city-wide median asking rent for apartments at around SAR 36,000 a year, with districts such as Al Rawdah and Al Nahda well above that and Al Bawadi below it. These are asking prices, so negotiate and compare before you commit.

### Does the rent freeze apply in Jeddah?
Not as of mid-2026. The five-year freeze on rent increases that started on 25 September 2025 covers properties inside Riyadh's urban boundary. The Real Estate General Authority can extend the framework to other cities with the approval of the Council of Economic and Development Affairs, so check for official announcements before you sign a long contract.

### Is Al Balad a good place to live?
Al Balad is Jeddah's historic centre and a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2014. It is fascinating to visit for its coral-stone houses, souks and restoration projects. Most residents, especially families, prefer modern districts with newer buildings, parking and nearby schools, so Al Balad is better treated as a place to explore than a place to rent.

### Is Jeddah more relaxed than Riyadh?
Many residents describe Jeddah as more relaxed, thanks to its seafront, its long history as a trading port and its mix of communities. The climate is different too: Jeddah is humid, while Riyadh is drier with hotter summers. Both cities offer good services, so the choice usually comes down to job location.

### How do I find a good apartment in Jeddah?
Shortlist two or three districts based on your commute, then work with a licensed broker who knows them. Visit at rush hour, check AC units and signs of damp, confirm what the rent includes, and register the contract on Ejar. Search verified agents and rental listings on SaudiYP to compare options.

### Is it better to rent a villa or an apartment in Jeddah?
Apartments are easier to find, cheaper to cool and often include parking and building maintenance. Villas offer more space and privacy, which suits larger families, but cost more to rent, cool and maintain. In humid Jeddah, the condition of air conditioning and drainage matters more than the property type, so inspect carefully either way.
:::
