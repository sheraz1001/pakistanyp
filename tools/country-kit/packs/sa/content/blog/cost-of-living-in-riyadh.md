The cost of living in Riyadh is driven by three things: housing, schooling and, for expatriates, government fees for family members. Everyday costs such as fuel, groceries and public transport are moderate by Gulf standards. Rent is the largest line for most households, and international school fees can match it for families.

As a rough guide, a single professional can live comfortably on a fraction of what a family of four with two children in international schools needs. This guide breaks down each cost with verified figures where they exist, and indicative ranges where they do not, so you can build your own budget.

:::takeaways
- Rent increases in Riyadh are frozen for five years from 25 September 2025, for new and existing contracts within the city's urban boundary.
- Average apartment rents rose sharply in 2024–25. Figures attributed to JLL put the average at around SAR 30,800 a year in Q2 2025.
- Residential electricity costs 18 halalas per kWh up to 6,000 kWh a month, and 30 halalas above that, at the time of writing.
- Expatriates pay a SAR 400 monthly fee for each dependent on their iqama.
- International school fees typically range from about SAR 25,000 to over SAR 150,000 a year (indicative).
:::

## Housing: the biggest part of the cost of living in Riyadh

Riyadh rents climbed quickly as the city grew. Market reports attributed to JLL put average apartment rents at about SAR 30,832 a year in Q2 2025, up nearly 20% year on year, and average villa rents at about SAR 88,715 a year. Treat these as city-wide averages. North Riyadh districts such as Al Malqa, Hittin and Al Narjis cost well above them, while older districts in the south and east cost less.

### The Riyadh rent freeze

On 25 September 2025, Saudi Arabia froze rent increases for residential and commercial properties inside Riyadh's urban boundary for five years. The Real Estate General Authority (REGA) oversees the rules. Landlords cannot raise rents on existing or new contracts during the freeze, and contracts must be registered on the [Ejar platform](https://www.ejar.sa). Reported penalties for breaking the rules reach up to 12 months' rent.

## Utilities: electricity, water, internet and mobile

Electricity is the bill that changes most with the seasons. Riyadh summers are long and hot, and air conditioning can multiply a bill between June and September.

- **Electricity:** residential tariffs set by the regulator are 18 halalas per kWh for the first 6,000 kWh a month and 30 halalas above that, at the time of writing. Reports in 2026 describe a planned increase, so check the current tariff on the [Saudi Electricity Company website](https://www.se.com.sa). Estimate your bill with our [electricity bill calculator](/calculators/electricity-bill).
- **Water:** billed by the National Water Company on a tiered basis. Most apartments pay modest amounts, while villas with gardens pay more.
- **Internet and mobile:** home fibre and 5G packages are widely available. Plans are often priced monthly and include 15% VAT.

> [!NOTE]
> VAT at 15% applies to most goods and services, including utilities, restaurant bills and school fees at private institutions. Always check whether a quoted price includes VAT.

## Transport: fuel, cars and the Riyadh Metro

Fuel is cheap compared with most countries. Aramco's published price at the time of writing is SAR 2.18 per litre for Gasoline 91 and SAR 2.33 for Gasoline 95, and Aramco reviews prices periodically. A car is still useful in Riyadh, but insurance, maintenance and parking add up.

The Riyadh Metro opened in phases from 1 December 2024 and covers six lines and about 176 km. A standard two-hour ticket costs SAR 4, with three-day, seven-day and 30-day passes available through the Darb app. Read our [Riyadh Metro guide to lines and fares](/news/riyadh-metro-lines-fares-guide) to see whether it covers your commute.

## School fees and childcare in Riyadh

For families, schooling is often the second-largest cost after rent. Saudi public schools are free for Saudi nationals. Most expatriate families choose private international or community schools.

- **Budget international and community schools:** often about SAR 25,000–45,000 a year for primary grades (indicative).
- **Mid-range British, American and IB schools:** often about SAR 55,000–90,000 a year (indicative).
- **Premium schools:** about SAR 90,000 to over SAR 150,000 for senior grades (indicative).

On top of tuition, budget for registration fees, uniforms, books, transport and exam fees. Plan with our [school budget calculator](/calculators/school-budget), check the [school admission guide for expat children](/expat-guide/school-admission-expat-children), and browse [schools in Riyadh](/categories/schools).

## Government fees for expatriate families

Expatriates pay a monthly fee of SAR 400 for each dependent sponsored on their iqama. For a family with a spouse and two children, that is SAR 1,200 a month, or SAR 14,400 a year, usually paid in advance when the iqama is renewed. Some employers cover it; many do not.

Budget also for iqama renewal, exit and re-entry visas when you travel, and medical insurance if your employer does not include family cover. Our guide to [family residency and dependents](/expat-guide/family-residency-dependents) explains the process.

:::checklist
- Annual rent and payment schedule agreed and registered on Ejar
- Summer electricity bill estimated, not just a winter month
- School tuition plus registration, transport and books
- Dependent fees for each family member (SAR 400 a month each)
- Car costs: fuel, insurance, maintenance and registration
- Health insurance for the whole family
- Annual flights home and exit and re-entry visas
:::

## How to keep the cost of living in Riyadh under control

- **Pick your district around your commute and school run.** Riyadh traffic is heavy at peak times, and a cheaper flat with a long drive can cost you in fuel and time.
- **Negotiate before you sign.** With rents frozen, the starting rent you agree is what you will pay for years, so it matters more than ever.
- **Service your AC before summer.** A clean, well-maintained unit uses less power.
- **Shop at local markets.** Fruit, vegetables, dates and meat are often cheaper at traditional markets than in large supermarkets.

## Final word

The cost of living in Riyadh is manageable for most professionals, but families need to plan for rent, schooling and dependent fees together. :::faq
### What is the average cost of living in Riyadh per month?
It depends heavily on household size. A single professional renting a modest apartment may spend roughly SAR 6,000–10,000 a month, while a family of four with children in international schools can spend SAR 15,000–25,000 or more (indicative). Rent and school fees make up most of the difference. Build your own estimate with a cost of living calculator.

### Is Riyadh expensive to live in?
Riyadh is moderate for everyday costs such as fuel, public transport and groceries, but housing has become expensive. Rents rose sharply in 2024 and 2025, which led the government to freeze rent increases from September 2025. For expatriate families, international school fees and the SAR 400 monthly dependent fee also add significantly to costs.

### How much is rent in Riyadh?
Market reports attributed to JLL put average apartment rents at about SAR 30,800 a year and villas at about SAR 88,700 a year in Q2 2025. New buildings in north Riyadh districts cost more, while older districts in the south and east cost less. Rents are usually paid annually or in instalments and must be registered on Ejar.

### What is the Riyadh rent freeze?
From 25 September 2025, landlords cannot increase rents on residential or commercial properties inside Riyadh's urban boundary for five years. The freeze covers existing and new contracts and is overseen by the Real Estate General Authority. Reported penalties reach up to 12 months' rent. The authority can extend similar rules to other cities.

### How much is the electricity bill in Riyadh?
Residential electricity is charged at 18 halalas per kWh for the first 6,000 kWh a month and 30 halalas above that, at the time of writing. Bills are low in winter but can rise steeply in summer, when air conditioning runs most of the day. A planned tariff change has been reported, so check the Saudi Electricity Company for current rates.

### How much are international school fees in Riyadh?
Indicative annual tuition ranges from about SAR 25,000–45,000 at budget international and community schools to over SAR 150,000 for senior grades at premium schools. VAT, registration, transport, uniforms and exam fees are usually extra. Fees change every year, so always ask the school for its current published fee schedule.

### How much is the dependent fee in Saudi Arabia?
Expatriates pay SAR 400 a month for each dependent sponsored on their iqama, such as a spouse or child. The fee is normally paid in advance for the iqama period when it is renewed. A family with three dependents therefore pays SAR 1,200 a month, or SAR 14,400 a year, unless the employer covers it.

### Do I need a car in Riyadh?
Many residents still rely on a car, because the city is spread out and summer heat makes walking difficult. The Riyadh Metro, which opened in phases from December 2024, now covers six lines and makes commuting without a car realistic along its routes. Ride-hailing apps and buses fill the gaps.

### Is Riyadh cheaper than Jeddah?
Costs are broadly similar, but they differ by district and property type. Riyadh rents rose faster than many other cities in recent years, which led to the rent freeze. Jeddah has its own premium coastal districts. The best approach is to compare specific districts and property types in both cities rather than city-wide averages.

### What salary do you need to live comfortably in Riyadh?
For a single person, a monthly salary of roughly SAR 10,000 or more usually allows a comfortable lifestyle, while a family with children in international schools typically needs considerably more, often SAR 25,000 or above, unless housing or schooling allowances are included (indicative). Check what your employer covers before you compare offers.
