The best areas to live in Riyadh depend on three things: where you work, where your children go to school and what you can spend. For most families, the newer northern districts such as Al Malqa, Hittin, Al Yasmin and Al Narjis are the first choice. Professionals who want to be close to offices often pick Olaya, Al Sulimaniyah or Al Wurud, and the Diplomatic Quarter suits people who want green space and a quieter, walkable setting.

If your budget is tighter, eastern districts such as Al Rawdah, Qurtubah and Ishbiliyah, and western districts such as Dhahrat Laban, offer more space for the money. Below we compare each area honestly, including commute, schools and what to check before you sign a lease.

:::takeaways
- North Riyadh (Al Malqa, Hittin, Al Yasmin, Al Narjis, Al Aqiq) is the most popular choice for families, with newer villas and apartments.
- Olaya, Al Sulimaniyah and Al Wurud put you close to central business districts and the Metro's Blue Line.
- The Diplomatic Quarter offers greenery, walking trails and a calm setting, but availability is limited.
- East and west districts usually give you more space for your budget, with longer commutes to the north.
- Rent increases in Riyadh are frozen for five years from 25 September 2025, so the rent you agree now matters.
:::

## How to choose the best area to live in Riyadh

Riyadh is large and spread out, and traffic on King Fahd Road, King Abdullah Road and the Northern Ring Road can be heavy at peak times. A home that looks cheaper on paper can cost you an hour a day in the car. Start with your fixed points, then search outward.

- Shortlist three districts | Pick districts inside your commute limit and budget.
- Visit at peak times | View the area on a weekday evening, when traffic and parking are at their worst.

## North Riyadh: Al Malqa, Hittin, Al Yasmin, Al Narjis and Al Aqiq

North Riyadh has grown fast over the past decade and is where many new villas, townhouses and apartment buildings are found. It is close to the King Abdullah Financial District (KAFD), the airport road and several international schools.

### Who it suits

- **Families** who want a modern villa or a large apartment and good school options nearby
- **Professionals** working in KAFD or in the north of the city
- **Newcomers** who want new buildings with lifts, parking and central air conditioning

### What to watch

Rents here are among the highest in the city, and some newer streets are still under construction. Al Malqa and Hittin are known for restaurants and cafés, while Al Narjis and Al Yasmin are quieter and residential. Al Aqiq sits near KAFD, which helps if you work there.

> [!TIP]
> New north Riyadh buildings vary in quality. Check water pressure, AC units, parking and the condition of shared areas, and ask who handles maintenance. A good [property management company](/categories/property-management) makes a real difference.

## Central Riyadh: Olaya, Al Sulimaniyah, Al Wurud and Al Murooj

Olaya is Riyadh's traditional business heart, home to landmark towers, malls, hotels and many offices. Al Sulimaniyah, Al Wurud and Al Murooj sit alongside it and mix apartments, older villas and shops.

These districts suit professionals and couples who want a short commute and shops, restaurants and clinics within easy reach. The Metro's Blue Line runs along Olaya Street, which makes car-free commuting along that corridor realistic. Read our [Riyadh Metro guide to lines and fares](/news/riyadh-metro-lines-fares-guide) for the routes.

The trade-off is space. Apartments are more common than large villas, buildings are often older and parking can be tight in busy streets.

## The Diplomatic Quarter: green, quiet and in demand

The Diplomatic Quarter (DQ), in the west of the city, houses most embassies and many international organisations. It is known for landscaped parks, walking and cycling trails and a calm atmosphere that is rare in Riyadh.

It suits diplomats, international staff and families who value green space and walkability. The catch is supply. Homes in the DQ are limited, demand is high and some properties are reserved for embassy staff. Expect to search for longer and decide quickly when something comes up.

## East, west and south Riyadh: more space for your budget

### East Riyadh: Al Rawdah, Qurtubah, Ishbiliyah and Al Hamra

East Riyadh is a long-established residential area with large villas, local markets and plenty of services. It is popular with Saudi families and with expatriates looking for more space for their money.

- **Good for:** families on a moderate budget who want a villa with a yard
- **Commute:** fine for jobs in the east and centre, longer for the north and KAFD
- **Character:** traditional neighbourhoods, local mosques, bakeries and grocery shops on most streets

Qurtubah and Ishbiliyah have newer housing than some older eastern districts, while Al Rawdah and Al Hamra are well established, with mature services. For many households on a moderate budget, these are among the best areas to live in Riyadh.

### West and south Riyadh: value districts with longer commutes

West Riyadh, including Dhahrat Laban, Laban and Irqah, sits near Wadi Hanifa and the Diplomatic Quarter. Irqah has some of the area's larger villas, while Dhahrat Laban and Laban generally offer more affordable rents.

South Riyadh districts such as Al Aziziyah, Al Shifa and Al Suwaidi are among the most affordable in the city. They are practical if you work in the south or in industrial areas, or if budget is your first priority. Commutes to the north can be long at peak times. ## Compounds: an alternative for expatriate families

Many expatriate families choose a residential compound. Compounds are gated communities with villas or apartments and shared facilities such as pools, gyms, play areas and sometimes shuttle buses to schools. Some include furniture, maintenance and utilities in the rent.

Compounds cost more than an equivalent home outside, and the best ones can have waiting lists. Search [compounds and furnished apartments in Riyadh](/categories/compounds-furnished-apartments) to compare facilities and ask about availability.

## Before you sign: a checklist for renting in Riyadh

> [!WARNING]
> Never pay a deposit or rent without a contract registered on Ejar, the official rental platform. An unregistered contract leaves you with little protection if there is a dispute, and it is now central to how the rent freeze is enforced.

:::checklist
- Visit the district at rush hour and at night
- Test the AC units, water pressure and hot water
- Check who pays for maintenance, water and parking
- Confirm the rent and payment schedule in the Ejar contract
- Ask about the distance to your children's [schools](/categories/schools)
- Photograph the property's condition before you move in
- Get written quotes from two or three moving companies
:::

:::faq
### Which is the best area to live in Riyadh for families?
North Riyadh districts such as Al Malqa, Hittin, Al Yasmin and Al Narjis are the most popular with families, thanks to newer villas, larger apartments and nearby international schools. They are also among the most expensive. Families on a moderate budget often choose eastern districts such as Al Rawdah, Qurtubah or Ishbiliyah, which offer more space for the money.

### Where do most expats live in Riyadh?
Many expatriates live in north Riyadh, in central districts such as Olaya and Al Sulimaniyah, in the Diplomatic Quarter, or in residential compounds spread across the city. The choice usually follows the location of the office and the children's school. Compounds remain popular with Western families for their shared facilities, security and community feel.

### What is the most affordable area in Riyadh?
South Riyadh districts such as Al Aziziyah, Al Shifa and Al Suwaidi are generally among the most affordable, along with parts of west Riyadh such as Dhahrat Laban and Laban. Some older eastern districts also offer good value. The trade-off is usually a longer commute to the north and KAFD, so factor travel time and fuel into the comparison.

### Is it better to live in north or central Riyadh?
North Riyadh suits families who want space, newer buildings and nearby schools, and people working in the north or KAFD. Central districts such as Olaya suit singles and couples who want a short commute, shops and restaurants nearby, and access to the Metro's Blue Line. Central homes are often older and smaller, with tighter parking.

### Can foreigners live in the Diplomatic Quarter?
Yes, though supply is limited. The Diplomatic Quarter houses most embassies and international organisations, and part of its housing is reserved for embassy staff. Other homes are available on the open market, but demand is high. If you want to live there, register with agents who specialise in the area and be ready to decide quickly.

### Is the Riyadh rent freeze relevant when choosing an area?
Yes. Since 25 September 2025, landlords cannot raise rents on residential properties inside Riyadh's urban boundary for five years. That means the rent you agree at the start is likely to stay in place for a long time, so negotiate carefully and make sure the contract is registered on the Ejar platform.

### Is the Riyadh Metro useful for choosing where to live?
It can be. The Metro has six lines covering about 176 km, and a standard two-hour ticket costs SAR 4. Living within walking distance of a station on your commute line can cut driving costs. The Blue Line along Olaya Street is especially useful for central offices. Check station locations before you sign.

### Are compounds worth it in Riyadh?
Compounds cost more than a similar home outside, but they often include facilities such as pools, gyms, security, maintenance and school shuttles, and sometimes furniture and utilities. For families new to Saudi Arabia, that convenience and community can be worth the premium. Compare total costs, including what is bundled into the rent, before deciding.

### How do I find a reliable estate agent in Riyadh?
Choose a licensed broker, ask for references and check that every contract will be registered on Ejar. A good agent knows the district, gives you honest commute and school advice and explains fees in writing. Search verified estate agents on SaudiYP and compare at least two before you commit to viewings.

### How far ahead should I look for a home in Riyadh?
Start looking about one to two months before your move, and earlier if you need a compound or the Diplomatic Quarter, where waiting lists are common. Many landlords want a quick decision once a property is shown. Having your iqama or employer letter, budget and preferred districts ready makes you a stronger applicant.
