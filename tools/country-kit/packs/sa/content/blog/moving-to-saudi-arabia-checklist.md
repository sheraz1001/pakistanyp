Moving to Saudi Arabia usually follows a fixed order. Your employer applies for a work visa, you complete a medical check and get your certificates attested, and you travel on the visa. After you arrive, your employer issues your iqama (residence permit), and you use it to set up Absher, a bank account, a SIM card, housing and, later, visas for your family.

The order matters because almost everything after arrival depends on the iqama. This checklist takes you through each stage, from three months before you fly to your first months in the Kingdom, with links to detailed guides and the documents to prepare.

:::takeaways
- Start attesting your degree and other certificates early; it is often the slowest step.
- Your employer sponsors your work visa and issues your iqama after arrival.
- The iqama gives you access to Absher, banking, a SIM card in your name, a lease on Ejar and family visas.
- Expatriates pay SAR 400 a month for each dependent on their iqama.
- A single exit and re-entry visa costs SAR 200 for up to two months, plus SAR 100 for each extra month.
:::

## Three months before moving to Saudi Arabia: paperwork

### Job offer and contract

Get a written job offer that states your salary, allowances (housing, transport), working hours, annual leave, medical insurance and who pays for family visas, schooling and flights. Read our guide to [employment rights in Saudi Arabia](/expat-guide/employment-rights) and the latest [Saudi Labour Law amendments](/news/saudi-labour-law-amendments-explained) so you know what the law requires.

### Certificate attestation

Most employers need your highest degree attested, and some professions need professional accreditation. Engineers register with the Saudi Council of Engineers, and healthcare workers with the Saudi Commission for Health Specialties. Attestation typically involves the issuing institution, your home country's authorities and the Saudi embassy or consulate, followed by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Saudi Arabia.

> [!TIP]
> Attest more than you think you need. Marriage and children's birth certificates are needed for family visas, and school transfer certificates for school admission. Doing it all before you leave is much easier than sending documents home later.

[Translation and attestation offices](/categories/translation-attestation) in Saudi Arabia can help with certified Arabic translations once you arrive.

### Medical check and visa

Your employer applies for your work visa. Depending on your nationality, you may need a medical examination at an approved centre in your home country, and some applicants need a police clearance certificate. Your passport should have plenty of validity left, ideally well over six months.

:::steps The moving to Saudi Arabia timeline
- 3 months before | Sign the contract and start attesting degree, marriage and birth certificates.
- 2 months before | Complete the medical check and send documents for the work visa.
- 1 month before | Receive the visa, book flights and plan temporary accommodation.
- First week | Give biometrics and get your iqama issued by your employer.
- First month | Set up Absher, a bank account, a SIM card and a lease on Ejar.
- Months 2–3 | Apply for family visas and school places, and convert your driving licence.
:::

## Your first week: iqama, Absher and Nafath

When you arrive on a work visa, your employer registers you for biometrics and issues your iqama through Muqeem, the Jawazat system for employers. The iqama is your residence permit and ID card, and it is digital as well as physical.

Next, set up your digital government accounts:

- **Absher:** the Ministry of Interior platform for iqama details, exit and re-entry visas, traffic violations and family services.
- **Nafath:** the national digital identity app, used to log in to most government and many private services.
- **Tawakkalna:** the government services app that also holds digital copies of your ID and documents.

Our guides to [iqama issuance and renewal](/expat-guide/iqama-issuance-renewal) and [government digital services](/expat-guide/government-digital-services) explain each step.

## Your first month: bank, phone and housing

### Bank account

Banks will ask for your iqama, passport and usually a salary certificate or letter from your employer. Many open accounts digitally once your iqama and Absher are active. Read the [expat guide to opening a bank account](/expat-guide/bank-account).

### Mobile SIM

You can buy a SIM on arrival with your passport and visa, but a SIM registered to your iqama is needed for most services. SIM registration uses fingerprint verification. See our guide to [getting a mobile SIM](/expat-guide/mobile-sim).

### Housing

Many newcomers stay in a hotel or furnished apartment for a few weeks while they look. Leases should be registered on Ejar, the official rental platform, and rent is usually paid annually or in instalments. In Riyadh, rent increases are frozen for five years from 25 September 2025. The [renting accommodation guide](/expat-guide/renting-accommodation) covers deposits and contracts, and our [Riyadh cost of living guide](/blogs/cost-of-living-in-riyadh) shows typical budgets.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not pay rent or a deposit for a property you have not seen, and do not sign a lease that will not be registered on Ejar. Rental scams targeting newcomers do occur, especially through informal social media adverts.

## Bringing your family to Saudi Arabia

Once your iqama is issued, you can usually apply for family visas if your profession and salary meet the requirements. Your family enters on a family visa and receives dependent iqamas.

| Item | What to know | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dependent fee | Charged monthly for each dependent on your iqama | SAR 400 per dependent per month |
| Single exit and re-entry visa | Needed every time a resident leaves and returns | SAR 200 for up to 2 months, SAR 100 per extra month |
| Multiple exit and re-entry visa | For frequent travellers | SAR 500 for 3 months, SAR 200 per extra month |
| Health insurance | Usually provided by employer for staff, check family cover | Varies by policy |
| School fees | Private and international schools charge annual tuition | Varies widely |

Fees are as widely reported at the time of writing. Check the current amounts on [Absher](https://www.absher.sa) before you pay.

Read our guide to [family residency and dependents](/expat-guide/family-residency-dependents), and start school applications early with the [school admission guide for expat children](/expat-guide/school-admission-expat-children). Popular schools can have waiting lists.

## Driving, healthcare and everyday life

- **Driving:** many nationalities can convert their home licence, while others must take lessons and a test. See the [driving licence guide](/expat-guide/driving-licence).
- **Healthcare:** private-sector employers must provide health insurance. Learn how [healthcare access](/expat-guide/healthcare-access) works.
- **Working week:** most private-sector employers work Sunday to Thursday, with Friday and Saturday as the weekend.
- **Prayer times:** many shops and services pause briefly at prayer times, so plan errands around them.

## The complete moving to Saudi Arabia checklist

:::checklist
- Signed contract with salary, allowances and benefits in writing
- Degree attested and professional accreditation started
- Marriage and children's birth certificates attested
- Medical check and police clearance, if required
- Passport with plenty of validity
- Temporary accommodation for the first weeks
- Iqama issued and Absher, Nafath and Tawakkalna set up
- Bank account and iqama-registered SIM card
- Lease registered on Ejar
- Family visa and school applications started
- Driving licence converted or lessons booked

:::faq
### What do I need to move to Saudi Arabia?
You need a job offer from a Saudi employer who will sponsor your work visa, a valid passport, attested educational certificates and, depending on your nationality, a medical check and police clearance. After arrival, your employer issues your iqama. Bringing family requires attested marriage and birth certificates and meeting the family visa conditions.

### How long does it take to get an iqama after arriving?
Your employer is responsible for issuing your iqama after you arrive on a work visa, through the Muqeem system. It requires biometric registration, a medical check where required and payment of the relevant fees. Timing depends on the employer and your profession, but many residents receive it within their first few weeks. Ask HR for an expected timeline.

### Can I bring my family to Saudi Arabia?
Usually yes, once your iqama is issued and if your profession and salary meet the requirements for a family visa. You need attested marriage and birth certificates. Each dependent on your iqama incurs a monthly fee of SAR 400, normally paid in advance when the iqama is renewed. Some employers cover this fee.

### What is the dependent fee in Saudi Arabia?
The dependent fee is SAR 400 per month for each family member sponsored on an expatriate's iqama, such as a spouse or child. A family with three dependents pays SAR 1,200 a month or SAR 14,400 a year. It is usually paid in advance for the iqama period. Check the current amount on Absher before you plan your budget.

### Which certificates need to be attested for Saudi Arabia?
Most employers require your highest educational degree to be attested, and some professions need professional accreditation, such as engineers with the Saudi Council of Engineers. For family visas you will need attested marriage and children's birth certificates. Attestation usually involves your home country's authorities, the Saudi embassy and then the Saudi Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

### How much is an exit and re-entry visa?
A single exit and re-entry visa costs SAR 200 for up to two months, plus SAR 100 for each additional month, as widely reported. A multiple exit and re-entry visa costs SAR 500 for three months, plus SAR 200 for each extra month. Your iqama and passport must have enough validity. Apply through Absher or your employer's Muqeem account.

### Can I open a bank account in Saudi Arabia without an iqama?
Most banks require an iqama to open a regular personal account, together with your passport and a letter or salary certificate from your employer. That is why getting your iqama quickly matters. Until then, many newcomers rely on cards from their home bank. Once your iqama and Absher are active, many banks allow digital account opening.

### What is the working week in Saudi Arabia?
Most private-sector employers and government offices work Sunday to Thursday, with Friday and Saturday as the weekend. Working hours depend on your contract and the Labour Law, and hours are usually reduced for Muslim employees during Ramadan. Some sectors, such as retail and hospitality, work shifts that include weekends.

### Is health insurance mandatory in Saudi Arabia?
Yes. Private-sector employers must provide health insurance for their expatriate employees, and cover for family members depends on your contract and the rules for dependents. Check exactly who is covered, which hospitals are in the network and what co-payments apply. Without valid insurance, you may struggle to renew residence documents.

### Where should I live when moving to Saudi Arabia?
Choose your city and district based on your workplace and your children's schools. In Riyadh, many expatriate families live in north Riyadh districts, central districts near Olaya or residential compounds. In Jeddah, north-central districts such as Al Rawdah and Al Zahra are popular. Stay in a furnished apartment first while you explore.
:::
