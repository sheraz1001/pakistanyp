Nitaqat is Saudi Arabia's Saudization programme. It sorts private-sector establishments into colour bands based on the share of Saudi nationals in their workforce, compared with the target for their sector and size. Your band decides whether you can issue new work visas, renew work permits and transfer expatriate staff, so it matters for every small business that employs foreign workers.

In practice, you manage Nitaqat through Qiwa, the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD) platform. Qiwa shows your current band, your sector's target and how many Saudi hires you need to move up. This guide explains how the system works for SMEs in 2026, which professions have their own quotas, what Saudi employees cost and how to build a plan that keeps you in the green.

:::takeaways
- Your band compares your Saudi share with a target set for your economic activity and establishment size.
- Your band, shown on Qiwa, ranges from Platinum through the Green bands down to Red.
- Red-band establishments lose key services, such as issuing new work visas and renewing work permits.
- Some professions, including accounting, dentistry and engineering, now have their own localisation quotas on top of the band system.
- Saudi employees must be registered with GOSI and have a documented contract to count.
:::

## What is Nitaqat and why does it matter?

Nitaqat (Arabic for "ranges" or "bands") was launched by the labour ministry in 2011. It is the main tool behind Saudization, the national policy of increasing Saudi employment in the private sector. The ministry has revised it several times, and the current version uses targets that vary by activity and size and are designed to rise gradually.

For a small business, the band is not just a score. It controls access to services you need to run the business day to day.

| Band | What it generally means | Typical effect on services |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Platinum | Well above the target | Widest access to visas and transfers |
| High Green | Above the target | Good access to services |
| Medium Green | Meets the target | Normal access |
| Low Green | Just meets the target | Normal access, little margin |
| Red | Below the target | New visas, permit renewals and many services blocked |

The exact services available in each band can change. Check the current rules on [Qiwa](https://qiwa.sa) or with the ministry.

> [!WARNING]
> Falling into Red can happen quickly in a small business. If one Saudi employee resigns from a team of eight, your rate can drop sharply overnight. Always keep a margin above your band's threshold.

## How Nitaqat is calculated for small businesses

Qiwa groups your establishment by its economic activity and the number of employees. MHRSD sets a target Saudization rate for each combination, and your actual rate is compared with it.

### Establishment size

Qiwa uses several size categories. The smallest establishments, with five or fewer workers, are treated differently from those with six or more. Establishments with 6 to 49 workers fall into the small category, where the percentage calculation starts to bite. If you are close to six employees, check how your next hire will change your obligations.

### Who counts as a Saudi employee

To count towards your rate, Saudi employees generally need to be:

- registered with GOSI under your establishment
- employed on a documented contract on Qiwa
- paid at least the wage required by the ministry for full counting, which can be higher for some professions

Some categories, such as GCC nationals or certain family members of Saudis, may count in specific ways. Check how each employee is counted on your Qiwa dashboard.

:::steps How to check your band on Qiwa
- Sign in to Qiwa | Log in to the establishment account with the owner's or manager's Nafath credentials.
- Open your establishment | Select the establishment linked to your CR.
- Review your band | See your current band, activity and size category.
- Check the target | Note the Saudization rate needed for the next band up.
- Plan hires | Use the Qiwa calculator to see how new hires change your rate.
:::

## Profession-specific Saudization quotas in 2025–26

On top of the band system, MHRSD has issued localisation decisions for specific professions. These apply when you employ a certain number of people in those roles, regardless of your overall band.

- **Accounting:** for establishments with five or more accountants, 40% of accounting roles had to be Saudised from 27 October 2025, rising by 10 percentage points a year to 70% by October 2028.
- **Dentistry:** a 45% target from July 2025, raised to 55% from 27 January 2026, with a minimum wage of SAR 9,000 for a Saudi dentist to count.
- **Engineering:** a 30% target for establishments with five or more engineers in covered roles, phased in through 2025–26, with Saudi engineers needing accreditation from the Saudi Council of Engineers and a minimum salary.

Other decisions cover sales, marketing, pharmacy and more. The ministry publishes procedural guides for each, so read the latest guide before hiring. An [accounting firm](/categories/accounting-audit) can help you track the accounting quota if you outsource finance.

- 55% | dentistry roles from 27 January 2026
- 30% | covered engineering roles, phased in 2025–26
- SAR 9,000 | minimum salary for a Saudi dentist to count
:::

## The cost of hiring Saudi employees: GOSI and salaries

GOSI, the General Organization for Social Insurance, collects contributions for pensions (annuities), occupational hazards and unemployment insurance (SANED).

- **Saudis who started before 3 July 2024:** the long-standing rates are 9.75% of the contributory wage from the employee and 11.75% from the employer, covering annuities, hazards and SANED.
- **Saudis new to the system from 3 July 2024:** under the new Social Insurance Law, the annuity rate is rising by 0.5 percentage points a year for each side, from 9.5% in July 2025 to 11% by July 2028.
- **Expatriate employees:** the employer pays 2% for occupational hazards, and the employee pays nothing.

Contributions are calculated on basic salary plus housing allowance, within GOSI's minimum and maximum limits. Use our [GOSI calculator](/calculators/gosi) and the [salary breakdown calculator](/calculators/salary-breakdown) to model costs, and confirm rates on the [GOSI website](https://www.gosi.gov.sa).

## What expatriate workers cost under the levy

Employers also pay a monthly expat levy for each foreign worker, widely reported as SAR 800 when expatriates outnumber Saudis in the establishment and SAR 700 when they do not. In December 2025, the Cabinet approved abolishing the levy for foreign workers in industrial facilities with a valid industrial licence. Check the current levy on Qiwa, as rules and exemptions change.

:::bars Indicative monthly cost to the employer, SAR 5,000 basic plus SAR 1,250 housing (SAR, indicative)
- Saudi employee, legacy GOSI rate (11.75%) | 734 | about SAR 734
- Expat employee, levy (over 50% expat) | 800 | SAR 800
- Expat employee, GOSI hazards (2%) | 125 | about SAR 125
:::

These figures show employer contributions only, not salaries. They are indicative examples, and GOSI wage limits and levy rules can change.

## A Saudization compliance plan for small businesses

Staying compliant is easier with a plan than with emergency hiring.

:::checklist
- Check your band and target on Qiwa every month
- Keep a margin of at least one Saudi hire above your threshold
- Register every Saudi employee with GOSI from day one
- Document all contracts on Qiwa
- Check profession quotas before hiring accountants, engineers or dentists
- Budget GOSI, levy and [end-of-service](/calculators/end-of-service) costs together
- Use training programmes to develop Saudi staff for skilled roles
:::

### Hiring and keeping Saudi staff

Good Saudi candidates have many options, so treat Saudization as recruitment, not paperwork. Offer clear career paths, competitive salaries and training. Government-backed programmes, such as those run by the Human Resources Development Fund (Hadaf), may support wages or training for Saudi hires. Check eligibility before you rely on them. [Professional training providers](/categories/professional-training) can help build skills in-house.

> [!TIP]
> Before you apply for a new work visa, run the Qiwa calculator to see your band after the hire. Some businesses fall into a lower band because the new expatriate employee changes the ratio.

### What is Nitaqat in Saudi Arabia?
It is the Saudization programme run by the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development. It places private-sector establishments in colour bands according to the share of Saudi nationals in their workforce, compared with a target for their economic activity and size. The band determines access to services such as work visas, permit renewals and worker transfers.

### What are the Nitaqat colour bands?
The bands range from Platinum, the highest, through High Green, Medium Green and Low Green, to Red, the lowest. Older guides also mention a Yellow band. Platinum and Green establishments keep access to most labour services, while Red establishments lose key services, such as issuing new work visas and renewing work permits for expatriate staff.

### How do I check my Nitaqat status?
Sign in to the Qiwa platform with your establishment account, using Nafath for identity verification. Select the establishment linked to your commercial registration, and the dashboard shows your current band, economic activity, size category and the Saudization rate needed for each band. Qiwa also has a calculator that shows how new hires would change your rate.

### Do Saudization rules apply to small businesses with few employees?
Yes, but the rules vary by size. Qiwa treats the smallest establishments, with five or fewer workers, differently from those with six or more, where the percentage targets apply. Even very small businesses may have obligations, and profession-specific quotas can apply. Check your establishment's requirements on Qiwa rather than assuming you are exempt.

### What happens if my company is in the Red band?
An establishment in the Red band generally cannot issue new work visas, renew work permits for expatriate workers or use many other labour services until it improves its Saudization rate. Expatriate employees may also find it easier to transfer to other employers, a process explained in our guide to [changing employers](/expat-guide/changing-employers). The fastest way out is to hire and register eligible Saudi employees.

### How much does GOSI cost for a Saudi employee?
For Saudis who joined the system before 3 July 2024, the employer contributes 11.75% and the employee 9.75% of the contributory wage. For Saudis new to the system since then, the annuity rate is rising by 0.5 percentage points a year, from 9.5% each in July 2025 to 11% by 2028, plus other branches. Confirm rates with GOSI.

### Does a Saudi employee need a minimum salary to count?
Yes. The ministry sets wage conditions for a Saudi employee to count fully, and some profession quotas set higher minimums. For example, a Saudi dentist must earn at least SAR 9,000 a month to count towards the dentistry quota. The employee must also be registered with GOSI and have a documented contract on Qiwa.

### Which professions have Saudization quotas in 2026?
MHRSD has issued quotas for many professions, including accounting, dentistry, engineering, pharmacy, sales and marketing roles. For example, accounting roles in establishments with five or more accountants needed 40% Saudis from 27 October 2025, rising to 70% by 2028. Check the ministry's latest procedural guides, as targets and dates are updated regularly.

### Is the expat levy still charged in 2026?
Yes, for most employers. The monthly levy for each foreign worker is widely reported as SAR 800 when expatriates outnumber Saudis in the establishment and SAR 700 when they do not. In December 2025, the Cabinet approved abolishing the levy for foreign workers in licensed industrial facilities. Check the current amount on Qiwa before budgeting.

### Can a Saudi owner count towards Saudization?
In some cases a Saudi owner who works full-time in the establishment and is registered with GOSI can count towards the Saudization rate, particularly in small establishments. The conditions are set by the ministry and can change. Check how the owner appears in your Qiwa dashboard, or ask a Saudization consultant to confirm before relying on it.
