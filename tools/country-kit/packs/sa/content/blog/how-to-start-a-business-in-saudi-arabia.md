If you want to know how to start a business in Saudi Arabia, the short answer is this. Choose a legal structure under the Companies Law. If any owner is foreign, register the investment with the Ministry of Investment (MISA). Then issue a commercial registration (CR) through the Ministry of Commerce, get the licences your activity and premises need, and register with ZATCA, GOSI and Qiwa before you hire or invoice.

Saudi owners can often complete the core steps online in days. Foreign-owned companies take longer, because MISA registration, attested documents and bank account opening add time. Below is each stage in order, with the official portals and indicative costs.

:::takeaways
- The Companies Law in force since 19 January 2023 allows single-owner LLCs and introduced the Simplified Joint Stock Company.
- Since the Investment Law took effect in February 2025, foreign investors register with MISA instead of applying for the old MISA licence.
- A CR no longer has an expiry date. You must confirm it every year, and the main CR fee is SAR 200 per year plus the Chamber of Commerce subscription.
- VAT registration is mandatory once taxable supplies pass SAR 375,000 a year.
- Register with GOSI and Qiwa before your first employee starts.
:::

## Step 1: Choose the right business structure in Saudi Arabia

Your structure decides who can own the business, how liability works and which documents you need. The Companies Law in force since 19 January 2023 made the system more flexible for small businesses.

The LLC is the most common choice for SMEs and foreign investors, and it can now have a single owner. The Simplified Joint Stock Company (SJSC), new in the 2023 law, suits startups that plan to raise investment. A sole establishment (*mu'assasa fardiya*) is simplest, but the owner is personally liable.

| Structure | Typical owner | Liability | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sole establishment | Saudi or GCC individual | Unlimited, personal | Small shops, trades, home services |
| LLC | One or more Saudi or foreign partners | Limited to capital | Most SMEs and foreign-owned firms |
| SJSC | One or more founders | Limited | Startups raising funding |
| JSC | Several shareholders | Limited | Large or listed businesses |
| Foreign branch | Foreign parent company | Parent is liable | Contract work for an existing company |

If unsure, a [company formation specialist](/categories/company-formation) or a [business lawyer](/categories/lawyers-law-firms) can compare structures against your plans.

## Step 2: Register with MISA if any owner is foreign

Foreign investors used to need a MISA investment licence. Under the new Investment Law, which took effect in February 2025, MISA moved to a registration model. Foreign investors now register their investment with the ministry and receive an investment registration certificate, followed by the CR and any activity licences.

MISA usually asks for parent company documents (incorporation certificate, articles, recent financial statements), passport copies of individual shareholders and managers, a board resolution or power of attorney, and your planned activities and capital. Documents issued abroad generally need to be attested by a Saudi embassy or consulate and translated into Arabic by a certified translator. You can find [translation and attestation offices](/categories/translation-attestation) in every major city.

> [!WARNING]
> MISA fees have changed with the new law. Under the old licence model, sources reported a SAR 2,000 annual licence fee plus an investor services subscription of roughly SAR 10,000 for the first year and SAR 60,000 for later years. Do not budget on these figures. Check the current fee schedule on the [Ministry of Investment portal](https://misa.gov.sa) before you apply.

Some activities remain restricted for foreign ownership or need extra approvals, so confirm yours is open before you commit.

## Step 3: Get your commercial registration (CR) from the Ministry of Commerce

The commercial registration is the legal record of your business. You apply through the [Saudi Business Center](https://business.sa), which connects the Ministry of Commerce with the other agencies you need.

### How the CR works since April 2025

The new Commercial Registration Law took effect on 3 April 2025 and changed some long-standing rules:

- A CR no longer has an expiry date. Instead, you must submit an **annual confirmation** of your details within the due date.
- The Ministry of Commerce says a CR that is not confirmed will receive a reminder, then a fine, and can then be suspended. A CR left suspended for a year without confirmation can be cancelled.

The Ministry of Commerce lists the main CR fee as SAR 200 per year, with the Chamber of Commerce subscription added on top. Chamber fees depend on your entity type and category.

:::steps Getting your CR in six steps
- Log in with Nafath | Sign in to the Saudi Business Center using Nafath, the national digital identity app.
- Reserve a trade name | Check that your Arabic (and optional English) trade name is available and reserve it.
- Pick your activities | Choose ISIC activity codes that match exactly what you will do.
- Upload documents | For companies, submit the articles of association and partner details.
- Pay the fees | Pay the CR fee and Chamber of Commerce subscription online.
- Download your CR | Print the electronic CR and note your annual confirmation date.
:::

> [!TIP]
> Choose activity codes carefully. Your CR activities decide which licences you need, which Nitaqat sector you fall into and what you can legally invoice for. Adding activities later is possible but costs time.

## Step 4: Get the municipal licence and activity licences

A CR alone does not let you open premises. Most physical businesses also need:

- **A Balady licence:** the municipal licence for your shop, office or workshop, issued once the premises meet safety requirements, including civil defence conditions.
- **A National Address:** registered with Saudi Post (SPL).
- **Sector licences:** for example from the Ministry of Health for clinics or the Ministry of Tourism for tour operators.

Your lease matters here. The premises must suit the activity and its zoning, so confirm the use before signing. Flexible [office space and business centres](/categories/office-space-business-centres) often come with a ready address.

## Step 5: Register for tax with ZATCA and set up e-invoicing

The Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA) handles zakat, income tax and VAT.

- **VAT** is charged at 15%. Registration is mandatory once your taxable supplies exceed SAR 375,000 over 12 months, and voluntary from SAR 187,500.
- **Zakat** generally applies to the Saudi and GCC share of a business, and **income tax** to the foreign share.
- **E-invoicing (Fatoora)** is mandatory for VAT-registered businesses, and ZATCA is connecting them to its platform in waves.

A [zakat, tax and VAT specialist](/categories/zakat-tax-vat) can register you, set up compliant invoicing software and file returns.

:::stats Key official thresholds (verified)
- 15% | standard VAT rate
- SAR 375,000 | mandatory VAT registration threshold
- SAR 200 | main CR fee per year (plus Chamber fees)
- 1–5 staff | Monsha'at definition of a micro enterprise
:::

## Step 6: Open a bank account and register with GOSI, Qiwa and Muqeem

Banks will ask for the CR, articles of association, National Address, the manager's ID and beneficial owner details. Account opening takes longer for foreign-owned companies, so start early.

### Registering as an employer

- **GOSI:** register the establishment with the General Organization for Social Insurance and enrol every employee. Use our [GOSI contribution calculator](/calculators/gosi) to estimate monthly costs.
- **Qiwa:** the Ministry of Human Resources platform for employment contracts, work permits and your Nitaqat (Saudization) status.
- **Muqeem:** the Jawazat system for managing foreign employees' iqamas (residence permits) and exit and re-entry visas.

For hiring, [Saudization and HR consultants](/categories/saudization-hr) can explain your Nitaqat band before you apply for visas.

:::checklist
- Trade name reserved and activities chosen
- Articles of association signed (for companies)
- MISA registration certificate (foreign owners)
- CR issued and annual confirmation date noted
- Lease and Balady licence for the premises
- National Address registered
- Corporate bank account opened
- ZATCA registration and e-invoicing set up
- GOSI, Qiwa and Muqeem accounts active
:::

## Costs: how to start a business in Saudi Arabia on a realistic budget

Budgeting is the part of how to start a business in Saudi Arabia that people most often underestimate. Government fees are only part of the picture; rent, fit-out, professional fees and visas usually cost more.

:::bars First-year setup budget for a small LLC (SAR, indicative)
- Office or shop rent | 60000 | SAR 30k–120k+
- Fit-out and furniture | 40000 | SAR 15k–80k+
- Professional and PRO fees | 15000 | SAR 5k–30k
- Accounting and e-invoicing software | 8000 | SAR 3k–15k
- CR and Chamber fees | 1500 | Around SAR 1k–2k
:::

These ranges are indicative and vary widely by city and district: rent in central [Riyadh](/cities/riyadh) is very different from a smaller city. Ask at least three providers for written quotes.

:::faq
### Can a foreigner start a business in Saudi Arabia?
Yes. Foreign investors can own many types of business outright, usually through an LLC or a branch. Since February 2025 they register the investment with the Ministry of Investment (MISA) instead of applying for the old investment licence. Some activities remain restricted or need extra approvals, so check the current list on the MISA portal.

### How long does it take to register a company in Saudi Arabia?
For a Saudi owner with documents ready, the CR can be issued online through the Saudi Business Center quickly, often within days. Foreign-owned companies usually take several weeks or longer, because MISA registration, attested parent documents, bank account opening and licences add steps.

### How much is a commercial registration in Saudi Arabia?
The Ministry of Commerce lists the main commercial registration fee as SAR 200 per year, with the Chamber of Commerce subscription added on top. Chamber fees vary by entity type. Other costs, such as trade name reservation, licences, rent and professional fees, are separate. Check the fee shown at checkout on the Saudi Business Center for your exact case.

### Does a commercial registration expire?
Not any more. Under the Commercial Registration Law in force since 3 April 2025, a CR has no expiry date. Instead, the owner must submit an annual confirmation of the registration details. If you miss it, the ministry sends a reminder, may impose a fine and can suspend the CR, and a suspended CR can eventually be cancelled.

### What is the minimum capital for an LLC in Saudi Arabia?
The 2023 Companies Law does not set a single general minimum capital for LLCs, and a one-person LLC is allowed. However, some regulated activities and sectors set their own capital requirements, and MISA or the licensing body may expect capital that matches your business plan. Confirm the requirement for your specific activity before you finalise the articles of association.

### Do I need a Balady licence?
Most businesses that operate from physical premises need a Balady licence, the municipal commercial licence issued through the Balady platform. The premises must suit the activity and meet safety requirements, including civil defence conditions. Some online or home-based activities follow different rules, so check the requirements for your activity code before you sign a lease.

### When do I need to register for VAT in Saudi Arabia?
VAT registration with ZATCA is mandatory once your taxable supplies exceed SAR 375,000 in a 12-month period, or are expected to. You can register voluntarily from SAR 187,500. The standard VAT rate is 15%. Once registered, you must issue e-invoices that meet ZATCA's Fatoora requirements and file regular returns.

### What is the difference between a sole establishment and an LLC?
A sole establishment is owned by one person and has no separate legal personality, so the owner is personally liable for its debts. An LLC is a separate company, and partners are liable only up to their capital. Under the 2023 Companies Law, an LLC can have just one owner, which makes it attractive for small businesses that want limited liability.

### Can I run a business from home in Saudi Arabia?
Some activities can be run from home, and Saudi citizens can use options such as the freelance work document for approved professions. Home-based food production and similar activities have their own municipal rules. Foreign residents working for an employer generally cannot start a side business on their work iqama. Check the rules for your activity with the relevant authority.

### Do I need to hire Saudi employees?
Once you hire staff, your establishment falls under Nitaqat, the Saudization programme managed through Qiwa. The required share of Saudi employees depends on your sector and size, and some professions have specific localisation targets. Your Nitaqat band affects whether you can obtain or renew work visas, so plan your hiring before you apply for visas.
:::
