Hail city is the capital of Hail Region and the place where most of the region's people, offices and shops are found. With 448,623 residents in the 2022 census, it holds well over half of the regional population, and it acts as the market and service centre for the farming districts and desert towns around it.

The heart of the old city is the Barzan district, where a historic market, mud-built palaces and forts stand close to the Barzan Grand Mosque. Modern Hail has grown well beyond that core, with newer commercial streets, government offices, hospitals and schools, but the Barzan market remains the best-known place to shop.

## Key facts
- **Status:** Capital of Hail Region
- **Population (2022 census, city):** 448,623 (GASTAT figures as compiled by citypopulation.de)
- **Telephone area code:** 016
- **Historic market:** Barzan market, in the Barzan heritage district
- **Landmarks:** Qishlah Palace, Barzan Palace towers and A'arif Fort
- **Weekly market:** the Friday market, a traditional-style souq
- **Chamber of commerce:** Hail Chamber

## Economy and main industries
As the regional capital, Hail's economy is built on government services, education, healthcare, retail and construction. Its businesses also serve the region's farms, which number nearly 15,000 holdings, so agricultural suppliers, machinery dealers, feed and fodder traders and transport companies are a visible part of the city's commerce.

Heritage tourism adds a smaller but growing source of business. Visitors to the UNESCO-listed rock art at Jubbah, about 90 kilometres to the north-west, usually stay in Hail city, using its hotels, restaurants, car rental firms and tour operators. Manufacturing and processing take place at Hail Industrial City, managed by MODON.

## Main business areas and markets
- **Barzan market:** described as the region's economic hub and more than a century old, selling women's and children's clothing, perfumes, cosmetics and food products
- **Barzan heritage district:** the historic centre around the Barzan Grand Mosque, where surviving towers of Barzan Palace rise to about 12 metres
- **Qishlah Palace:** a large two-storey mud-built palace with eight watchtowers, one of the city's main landmarks
- **A'arif Fort:** a historic fort and one of the heritage sites of central Hail
- **Friday market:** a traditional-style weekly souq held on the Friday weekend
- **Hail Industrial City:** MODON's industrial zone for factories and workshops

## Heritage and the visitor trade
Hail's historic buildings give the centre a distinct identity. Qishlah Palace is described as one of the oldest and largest surviving mud buildings in the Arabian Peninsula, and the Barzan area combines heritage sites with active trade, so many visitors combine sightseeing with shopping. Guides and drivers based in the city organise trips to Jubbah and the mountains around Hail.

New businesses register with the Ministry of Commerce for a Commercial Registration and obtain a municipal licence through the Balady platform. The Hail Chamber issues certificates of origin and supports local companies with training and investment events.

## Frequently asked questions
### What is the area code for Hail?
Hail uses the 016 landline code, shared with Qassim.

### Where is the main market in Hail?
The Barzan market, in the historic Barzan district next to the Barzan Grand Mosque, is the city's best-known market.

### What are the main landmarks in Hail city?
Qishlah Palace, the Barzan Palace towers and A'arif Fort are the best-known historic sites in the city centre.

### How far is Jubbah from Hail city?
Jubbah, home to the Jabal Umm Sinman rock art site, is about 90 kilometres north-west of Hail city.
