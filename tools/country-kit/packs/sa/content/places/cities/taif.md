Taif lies in the Sarawat mountains above Makkah and Jeddah, and its higher altitude and milder summers have long made it a retreat from the heat of the coast. It is the largest governorate in Makkah Region by area, and its economy mixes government services, agriculture, tourism and trade with a growing list of development projects.

The city is especially associated with roses. Farms in the Sarawat mountains in Taif Governorate harvest hundreds of millions of roses each year, which are distilled into rose water and oil and sold across the Kingdom. Alongside that traditional trade, Taif has been the focus of new investment, including a master plan for a new city area and a technology park.

## Key facts
- **Status:** Category A governorate of Makkah Region
- **Population (2022 census, Taif Governorate):** 913,374 (GASTAT)
- **Telephone area code:** 012
- **Signature product:** Taif roses, grown in the Sarawat mountains
- **Heritage market:** Souq Okaz, the historic market site on old trade and Hajj routes
- **Chamber of commerce:** Taif Chamber

## Economy and main industries
Government administration, education, healthcare and retail form the backbone of the local economy, serving the governorate's population of more than 900,000. Agriculture remains important on the surrounding slopes, and the rose industry is its best-known branch: investment in Taif roses in the Saudi market has been put at more than SAR 64 million, and the harvest supports farms, distilleries, perfume makers and gift shops.

Tourism is the second pillar. Visitors from the Kingdom's hot coastal and inland cities come for the climate and landscape, especially in summer, supporting hotels, furnished apartments, restaurants and entertainment businesses.

Investment has picked up in recent years. A Taif investment forum produced agreements worth over SAR 11 billion for projects in the governorate, and development plans cover the revitalisation of Old Taif and West Taif, the New Taif master plan, a new airport, and projects such as the Okaz market area and the Technology Oasis, a business park developed as a joint venture between King Abdulaziz City for Science and Technology and the Morganti Group.

## Main business areas and markets
- **Central Taif:** the main commercial streets, government offices and traditional markets of the city
- **Old Taif:** the historic core, the subject of a revitalisation programme
- **West Taif:** another area covered by the governorate's development plans
- **New Taif:** a master-planned urban expansion that includes the Okaz market area and the Technology Oasis
- **Souq Okaz:** the historic market site, part of the New Taif development plans
- **Rose farms:** spread across the Sarawat mountain slopes of the governorate, many selling direct to visitors

## Roses and seasonal trade
The rose harvest is the busiest time for Taif's farms and distilleries, and rose water, rose oil and related products are bought by visitors and traders from across the Kingdom. Small producers sell directly from farms and through city shops, while larger businesses supply perfumers and retailers elsewhere. The summer tourist season brings a second peak for hotels, restaurants and shops.

New businesses obtain a Commercial Registration from the Ministry of Commerce and a municipal licence through the Balady platform. The Taif Chamber supports local companies with certificates and events, and in 2026 met the head of Monsha'at, the small and medium enterprises authority, to discuss the needs of the governorate's small businesses.

## Frequently asked questions
### What is the area code for Taif?
Taif uses the 012 landline code, the same as Jeddah and Makkah.

### What is Taif known for?
Taif is known for its roses, grown in the Sarawat mountains and made into rose water and oil, and for its milder climate, which draws summer visitors.

### What is Souq Okaz?
Souq Okaz is the ancient Okaz market site at a crossroads of historical trade and Hajj routes, and the Okaz market area is one of the projects in the New Taif master plan.

### Is there a chamber of commerce in Taif?
Yes. The Taif Chamber serves the governorate, separate from the Jeddah and Makkah chambers.
