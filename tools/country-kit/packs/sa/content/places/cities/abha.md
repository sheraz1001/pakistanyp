Abha is the administrative capital of Asir Region, set high in the mountains of south-western Saudi Arabia. Its climate and setting draw visitors from across the Kingdom, particularly in summer, and the city's traditional markets and arts district give it a commercial character quite different from the Gulf or Red Sea cities.

The city had 334,290 residents in the 2022 census, up from 236,157 in 2010, and Abha Governorate had 422,243. Government offices, education, healthcare and tourism are the main sources of employment, and the city works closely with neighbouring Khamis Mushait, Asir's most populous governorate.

## Key facts
- **Status:** Capital of Asir Region
- **Population (2022 census):** 334,290 in the city; 422,243 in Abha Governorate (GASTAT)
- **Telephone area code:** 017
- **Cultural district:** Al Muftaha, home to the King Fahad Cultural Center and Talal Maddah Theatre
- **Best-known market:** Souq Al Thulatha, the Tuesday market
- **Landmark:** Abha Dam, built in 1974
- **Chamber of commerce:** Abha Chamber

## Economy and main industries
Abha's economy rests on public administration, services and tourism. As regional capital it hosts the government departments that serve all of Asir, and their staff and contractors support a steady local market for housing, retail, food and professional services.

Tourism is the main growth sector. Visitors come for the mountain climate, the cable car to the Soudah highlands and the city's heritage, and they support hotels, furnished apartments, restaurants, cafés, car rental firms and tour operators. Demand peaks in summer and on public holidays.

Agriculture around the city produces corn, peaches, figs, apricots, pomegranates, grapes, apples and strawberries, which reach consumers through Abha's markets alongside local honey.

## Main business areas and markets
- **Al Muftaha:** a cultural district along the Abha Valley, historically a centre for sword-making and metalwork, now with galleries, studios and murals, the King Fahad Cultural Center and the Talal Maddah Theatre
- **Souq Al Thulatha:** one of Asir's oldest markets, in the Al Muftaha district off King Khaled Street, known for handicrafts and honey, with a section run by women sellers
- **Souq Al Nesa:** a market specialising in women's accessories and clothing
- **Souq Al Khanajer:** a market for heritage items, including traditional daggers
- **Abha Dam area:** a reservoir built for water supply and flood control that has become a recreational spot close to the city
- **King Khaled Street:** the street off which Souq Al Thulatha and the Al Muftaha district are reached

## Markets and crafts
Abha's souqs remain an important part of local commerce. Traditionally, markets in Asir were named after the day of the week they were held, and Souq Al Thulatha, the Tuesday market, keeps that name although it now operates most of the week. Sellers offer local honey and handicrafts, and the women's section, run by female sellers, is a distinctive feature. Visitors should check opening hours locally, as published times vary.

New businesses register with the Ministry of Commerce for a Commercial Registration and obtain a municipal licence through the Balady platform. Tourism businesses may need additional licences. The Abha Chamber serves local companies with certificates, training and investment events.

## Frequently asked questions
### What is the area code for Abha?
Abha uses the 017 landline code, shared with Khamis Mushait and the rest of Asir.

### Where is Souq Al Thulatha?
Souq Al Thulatha is in the Al Muftaha district, off King Khaled Street, next to the Al Muftaha art village.

### What is the population of Abha?
The 2022 census counted 334,290 people in Abha city and 422,243 in Abha Governorate.

### Which chamber of commerce serves Abha?
The Abha Chamber is the chamber of commerce for the city and the surrounding areas of Asir.
