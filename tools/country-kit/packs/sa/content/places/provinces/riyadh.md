Riyadh Region covers a large share of central Saudi Arabia, around 380,000 square kilometres or roughly a fifth of the Kingdom's land area. At its heart is Riyadh city, the national capital, but the region also takes in farming governorates, desert towns and a growing ring of industrial land that serves the capital's market.

Commercially the region matters because it combines the seat of government with the country's largest consumer market. The 2022 census counted more people here than in any other region, and together with Makkah and the Eastern Province it accounts for roughly two-thirds of the Kingdom's population. For businesses that sell to government, to head offices or to households, Riyadh Region is usually the first market to cover.

## Key facts
- **Administrative status:** One of the 13 administrative regions of Saudi Arabia, divided into 22 governorates
- **Regional capital:** Riyadh, which is also the national capital
- **Population (2022 census, region):** 8,591,748, of whom 4,439,210 Saudi and 4,152,538 non-Saudi (GASTAT)
- **Area:** About 380,000 sq km, around 19.4 per cent of the Kingdom
- **Telephone area code:** 011 for Riyadh city and most of the region; some northern governorates such as Al-Majmaah use 016
- **Chambers of commerce:** Riyadh Chamber, with a separate chamber in Al-Majmaah
- **Industrial land:** Riyadh's MODON industrial cities, Sudair Industrial City and Al-Kharj Industrial City

## Economy and main industries
The public sector is the largest employer in the capital, as ministries, government agencies and their contractors are concentrated there. Around that core sits a large private economy of retail, construction, real estate, professional services, healthcare, education and logistics serving more than eight and a half million residents.

Manufacturing is organised mainly through industrial cities run by the Saudi Authority for Industrial Cities and Technology Zones (MODON). Riyadh's first industrial city was one of the three that opened in 1973, alongside Jeddah and Dammam, and the capital now has first, second and third industrial cities. Further out, Sudair Industrial City lies roughly 120 kilometres north of Riyadh and covers somewhere between 258 and 265 square kilometres depending on the source, making it a major location for factories that need space and highway access. Al-Kharj Industrial City, south-east of the capital, is listed at about 99.5 square kilometres.

Outside the industrial zones, the governorates support agriculture, livestock, trading and services for their own populations and for travellers on the routes that cross the region.

## Main business areas and markets
Riyadh city holds most of the region's offices, malls and wholesale markets, and it has its own guide. Beyond the capital, the main commercial centres are the governorate seats:
- **Al-Kharj:** the nearest large governorate to the south-east of the capital, with farming land and its own industrial city
- **Diriyah:** on the north-western edge of Riyadh, a governorate in its own right
- **Al-Majmaah:** a northern governorate with its own chamber of commerce, close to Sudair Industrial City
- **Al-Dawadmi:** a governorate to the west of the capital on the road towards the Hejaz
- **Wadi ad-Dawasir:** a governorate in the south of the region

Each governorate seat has its own municipality, shops, workshops and service providers, so many everyday needs, from vehicle repairs to building materials, are met locally rather than in the capital.

## Industrial cities and land for business
For manufacturers and logistics firms the key decision is often which industrial city to locate in. MODON leases serviced plots in its Riyadh industrial cities and in Sudair, and investors normally apply to MODON directly for land, utilities and building permits within those zones. Shops, offices and workshops outside industrial cities need a municipal licence through the Balady platform from the relevant municipality, alongside the Commercial Registration issued by the Ministry of Commerce. Membership of the Riyadh Chamber, or the Al-Majmaah chamber in the north, is the usual route to certificates of origin, trade documents and business events.

## Finding businesses in Riyadh Region
Searches across the region tend to focus on contractors, building materials, industrial suppliers, vehicle services, logistics companies and professional services such as accountants and lawyers. In the governorates people also look for agricultural suppliers, livestock traders and local workshops. SaudiYP lists businesses by city and category, so you can narrow results to the capital or to a specific governorate seat.

## Frequently asked questions
### What is the telephone area code for Riyadh Region?
Riyadh city and most of the region use 011. A few northern governorates, including Al-Majmaah, fall under the 016 zone shared with Qassim and Hail.

### Where are the main industrial areas near Riyadh?
MODON runs the Riyadh first, second and third industrial cities, Sudair Industrial City about 120 kilometres north of the capital, and Al-Kharj Industrial City to the south-east.

### Which chamber of commerce covers Riyadh Region?
The Riyadh Chamber serves the capital and most of the region, and Al-Majmaah has its own chamber for the northern governorates. Both sit within the Federation of Saudi Chambers.

### How many governorates are in Riyadh Region?
Riyadh Region has 22 governorates, the highest number of any Saudi region, in addition to the capital.
