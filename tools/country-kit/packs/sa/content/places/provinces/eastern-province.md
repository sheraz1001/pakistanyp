The Eastern Province runs along the Kingdom's Gulf coast and extends deep into the interior, taking in the Dammam metropolitan area, the oasis of Al-Ahsa, the industrial port of Jubail and the northern town of Hafr Al-Batin. It is the heartland of the Saudi oil and gas industry, and much of the country's energy, petrochemical and engineering activity is managed from here.

With just over five million residents in 2022, it is the third most populous region. Its economy is more industrial than any other region's, and that shapes the business landscape: contractors, engineering firms, industrial suppliers, logistics companies and training providers make up a large part of the market, alongside the retail and services that any large population needs.

## Key facts
- **Administrative status:** One of the 13 administrative regions of Saudi Arabia
- **Regional capital:** Dammam
- **Population (2022 census, region):** 5,125,254, of whom 2,949,854 Saudi and 2,175,400 non-Saudi (GASTAT)
- **Category A governorates:** Al-Ahsa, Hafr Al-Batin, Jubail, Qatif and Khobar
- **Other governorates include:** Khafji, Ras Tanura, Abqaiq, Al-Nuayriyah and Qaryat al-Ulya
- **Telephone area code:** 013
- **Airports:** King Fahd International Airport (Dammam), Al-Ahsa International Airport and Al-Qaisumah International Airport (Hafr Al-Batin)
- **Chambers of commerce:** Asharqia Chamber (Dammam, with branches in Jubail, Hafr Al-Batin, Khafji and Qatif), Al-Ahsa Chamber and Hafr Al-Batin Chamber

## Economy and main industries
Oil and gas dominate. Saudi Aramco is headquartered in Dhahran, and the province hosts production, processing and export infrastructure, including the oil port at Ras Tanura. Around this core is a large services economy: drilling and oilfield services, engineering and construction, inspection and testing, equipment rental, industrial maintenance and specialist training.

Downstream industry is concentrated at Jubail on the northern coast and in the MODON industrial cities around Dammam, which have first, second and third industrial cities. Dammam's first industrial city was among the three opened in 1973. Al-Ahsa adds agriculture together with its own MODON industrial zone, Modon Oasis in Al-Ahsa. Research and technology are centred in Dhahran, where Dhahran Techno Valley, owned by King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals, hosts oil and gas research centres.

## Main business areas and markets
- **Dammam, Khobar and Dhahran:** the three cities form one metropolitan area, with Dammam's port and industrial cities, Khobar's retail and office corridors and Dhahran's energy and research institutions; each has its own guide
- **Jubail:** an industrial governorate on the coast north of Dammam, with an Asharqia Chamber branch
- **Al-Ahsa:** an inland oasis governorate centred on Hofuf and Al-Mubarraz, with 1,104,267 residents in 2022 and its own chamber
- **Qatif:** a Category A coastal governorate between Dammam and Jubail, with an Asharqia Chamber branch
- **Hafr Al-Batin:** the main town of the north of the province, with its own chamber and airport
- **Khafji:** a northern coastal governorate, also served by an Asharqia Chamber branch

## Ports, airports and links to the Gulf
The province is the Kingdom's main gateway to the Gulf. King Abdulaziz Port in Dammam is the province's main commercial seaport, and Ras Tanura is a major oil export terminal. The King Fahd Causeway, about 25 kilometres long and opened in 1986, links Khobar with Bahrain, which makes the Dammam area a natural base for companies working across both markets. King Fahd International Airport, notable for its very large land area, serves the metropolitan area, while Al-Ahsa and Hafr Al-Batin have their own international airports.

Companies setting up here obtain a Commercial Registration from the Ministry of Commerce and a municipal licence through the Balady platform. Industrial investors lease land from MODON in the Dammam or Al-Ahsa industrial cities. Most firms in the Dammam area join the Asharqia Chamber, while those in Al-Ahsa and Hafr Al-Batin join their local chambers.

## Finding businesses in the Eastern Province
Typical searches include oilfield and engineering services, industrial suppliers, contractors, inspection and safety companies, freight and customs agents, and staffing firms. Households look for schools, clinics, car services and home maintenance across the Dammam area and Al-Ahsa. SaudiYP lists businesses by city, so you can search Dammam, Khobar, Jubail or Al-Ahsa separately.

## Frequently asked questions
### What is the area code for the Eastern Province?
The whole province uses the 013 landline code, including Dammam, Khobar, Dhahran, Jubail and Al-Ahsa.

### Which chamber of commerce covers the Eastern Province?
The Asharqia Chamber is based in Dammam and has branches in Jubail, Hafr Al-Batin, Khafji and Qatif. Al-Ahsa and Hafr Al-Batin also have chambers of their own.

### How do you travel from the Eastern Province to Bahrain by road?
The King Fahd Causeway links Khobar with Al Jasra in Bahrain. It is about 25 kilometres long and was officially opened in November 1986.

### Where are the industrial cities in the Eastern Province?
MODON runs first, second and third industrial cities in Dammam and Modon Oasis in Al-Ahsa. Jubail on the northern coast is a separate industrial centre.
