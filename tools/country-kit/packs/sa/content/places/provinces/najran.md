Najran Region lies in the south of the Kingdom along the border with Yemen, reaching from the farmed Najran valley eastward into open desert. Its capital, Najran city, sits in the valley, while Sharurah, the region's other main centre, lies far to the east.

The regional economy rests on commerce, mining and industry, with agriculture an important contributor. Citrus fruit, dates and, increasingly, coffee give the region a distinctive farm sector, and heritage tourism has gained attention since the Hima Cultural Area was added to the UNESCO World Heritage List. Business activity has been rising: registrations in the region have grown sharply in recent years as investment programmes have targeted the south.

## Key facts
- **Administrative status:** One of the 13 administrative regions of Saudi Arabia
- **Regional capital:** Najran
- **Population (2022 census, region):** 592,300 (GASTAT)
- **Category A governorate:** Sharurah
- **Category B governorates:** Habuna, Badr al-Janub, Yadmah, Thar and Khubash
- **Telephone area code:** 017
- **Airports:** Najran Regional Airport, with capacity for 1.4 million passengers a year, and Sharurah Domestic Airport
- **Chamber of commerce:** Najran Chamber

## Economy and main industries
Commerce, mining and industry are described as the three main pillars of Najran's economy. Trade is concentrated in Najran city, which serves as the market and service centre for the whole region. Mining has been identified as a strategic sector for investment, alongside agriculture and tourism.

Agriculture is significant for a region of this size. Najran is one of the Kingdom's largest sources of citrus fruit, and farms also grow peaches, apricots, apples and lemons. Date varieties grown in the region include Khalas, Bayadh, Rotab, Barhi and Mawakil. Coffee is a newer crop in the region, and cultivation has now expanded to nearly 100,000 trees.

Government services, construction and retail make up much of the rest of the economy.

## Main business areas and markets
- **Najran city:** the regional capital, with 381,431 residents in the 2022 census and the region's government offices, the main markets, the regional airport and most professional services; it has its own guide
- **Sharurah:** the region's only Category A governorate, which also absorbed the population of the former Al-Kharkhir governorate when it was abolished in 2014; it has its own domestic airport
- **Habuna, Badr al-Janub, Yadmah, Thar and Khubash:** smaller governorates with farming, livestock and local trade

## Investment and business growth
Najran has been a focus of investment promotion. At the Najran Investment Forum the Najran Chamber signed 14 cooperation agreements with public and private bodies, and reports have pointed to a 56 per cent rise in business registrations in the region. The Ministry of Investment lists mining, agriculture and tourism as the region's strategic sectors.

Anyone starting a business needs a Commercial Registration from the Ministry of Commerce and, for premises, a municipal licence through the Balady platform. Membership of the Najran Chamber gives access to certificates of origin, training and investment events. For travel, Najran Regional Airport in the capital, with capacity for 1.4 million passengers a year, is supported by a domestic airport at Sharurah, which helps businesses serving the far east of the region.

## Frequently asked questions
### What is the area code for Najran?
Najran Region uses the 017 landline code, which it shares with Asir, Jazan and Al-Baha.

### What does Najran produce?
Najran is a major source of citrus fruit and also grows dates, stone fruit and apples. Coffee cultivation is expanding.

### What happened to Al-Kharkhir governorate?
Al-Kharkhir Governorate was abolished in 2014, and its administrative centres and population were transferred to Sharurah Governorate.

### Which chamber of commerce covers Najran?
The Najran Chamber covers the region. It signed 14 cooperation agreements with public and private bodies during the Najran Investment Forum.
