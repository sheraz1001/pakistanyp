Al-Baha Region sits in the Sarawat mountains of south-western Saudi Arabia, between Makkah Region and Asir. It had 339,174 residents in the 2022 census, the fewest of any region and only a little over half the population of Al-Jouf or Najran, and its towns are spread across highland valleys, escarpments and the lower land to the east and west.

Agriculture has always been the mainstay of the regional economy, and its mountain landscape attracts domestic visitors, particularly in summer. In recent years these two strands have come together in rural tourism, with licensed farms opening to visitors and honey festivals drawing buyers from other regions. Small family businesses dominate the local market.

## Key facts
- **Administrative status:** One of the 13 administrative regions of Saudi Arabia, divided into nine governorates
- **Regional capital:** Al-Baha
- **Population (2022 census, region):** 339,174 (GASTAT)
- **Second city:** Baljurashi
- **Largest governorate by area:** Al-Aqiq
- **Telephone area code:** 017
- **Airport:** King Saud Domestic Airport at Al-Aqiq, about 45 kilometres from Al-Baha city
- **Chambers of commerce:** Al-Baha Chamber and Al-Makhwah Chamber

## Economy and main industries
Agriculture is the backbone of Al-Baha's economy. Farms across the governorates supply local markets, and honey is one of the region's signature products, promoted through honey festivals that combine market sales with tourism.

Tourism and agriculture are the region's strategic sectors for investment. Rural farm tourism has grown quickly, with several dozen farms licensed or in the process of licensing to receive visitors, offering farm stays, produce sales and outdoor activities. Exact counts vary from year to year as new farms are licensed.

Government services, education, healthcare, retail and construction account for much of the rest of the economy. With a modest population, the business community is made up mainly of shops, workshops, contractors and service providers.

## Main business areas and markets
- **Al-Baha city:** the regional capital, with government offices, retail streets, hotels and most professional services
- **Baljurashi:** the second city by population, with its own commercial centre and markets
- **Al-Mandaq:** a governorate whose Bani Hasan area is known for its scenery
- **Al-Aqiq:** the largest governorate by area and the location of the region's airport, which offers domestic flights only, so most international travellers connect through other Saudi airports
- **Al-Mikhwah:** a governorate with its own chamber of commerce
- **Ghamid Al-Zinad:** a mountainous governorate

## Tourism and seasonal trade
Demand in Al-Baha is strongly seasonal. During the summer months and public holidays, hotels, furnished apartments, restaurants, car rental firms and farm-stay operators see their busiest periods, and honey and produce sellers benefit from the extra visitors. Outside the peak, trade relies mainly on residents and government employees. One recent report counted 30 rural farms officially licensed in the region, with licensing under way for a further 35, which gives a sense of how quickly farm tourism is being formalised.

New businesses obtain a Commercial Registration from the Ministry of Commerce and a municipal licence through the Balady platform. Farm-stay and other tourism businesses also need tourism licensing. Firms join the Al-Baha Chamber, or the Al-Makhwah Chamber in that governorate, for certificates and business support.

## Frequently asked questions
### What is the area code for Al-Baha?
Al-Baha Region uses the 017 landline code, shared with Asir, Jazan and Najran.

### Where is Al-Baha's airport?
King Saud Domestic Airport is in Al-Aqiq Governorate, about 45 kilometres from Al-Baha city, and offers domestic flights.

### How many governorates does Al-Baha Region have?
The region is divided into nine governorates. Al-Baha city is the capital, Baljurashi is the second city by population, and Al-Aqiq is the largest by area.

### Are there chambers of commerce in Al-Baha?
Yes. The Al-Baha Chamber serves the region, and Al-Mikhwah has its own chamber of commerce.
