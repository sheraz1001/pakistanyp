The Northern Borders Region stretches along the Kingdom's northern frontier, facing Iraq and Jordan. It is one of the least populous regions, with fewer than 400,000 residents in 2022, but it carries strategic weight for two reasons: its large phosphate deposits, and the Jadidat Arar border crossing that links Saudi Arabia with Iraq.

Arar, the regional capital, holds more than half of the population and most of its offices and shops. Rafha, Turaif and Al-Uwayqilah are the other governorate seats. Between them lie long distances of open desert, so each town serves a wide hinterland for retail, vehicle services, healthcare and government business.

## Key facts
- **Administrative status:** One of the 13 administrative regions of Saudi Arabia, with four governorates
- **Regional capital:** Arar
- **Population (2022 census, region):** 373,577 (GASTAT)
- **Governorates (2022 census):** Arar 202,719, Rafha 84,536, Turaif 66,004 and Al-Uwayqilah 20,318
- **Telephone area code:** 014
- **Airports:** domestic airports at Arar, Rafha and Turaif
- **Border crossing:** Jadidat Arar, on the frontier with Iraq
- **Chamber of commerce:** Arar Chamber of Commerce and Industry, serving the region

## Economy and main industries
Mining is the region's defining industry. Large phosphate deposits are worked at Waad Al-Shamal, near Turaif, and phosphate concentrate has been carried by rail from Al Jalamid to Ras Al Khair on the Gulf coast for processing. In 2023 the investment minister described a SR33 billion phosphate project expected to make Saudi Arabia the world's third-largest phosphate producer. The region's mining resources have been reported to be worth about $1.22 trillion.

Manufacturing has grown alongside mining. Press reports cite a 58 per cent increase in the number of factories in the region, and commercial registrations rose by 32 per cent between 2018 and 2023. Much of this activity is linked to mining support services, construction, transport and supply.

The rest of the economy is made up of government services, retail, livestock trading and small workshops serving the four governorate towns.

## Main business areas and markets
- **Arar:** the regional capital, with government offices, the main markets, the chamber of commerce and most professional services
- **Turaif:** a governorate close to the Waad Al-Shamal phosphate area, with its own domestic airport
- **Rafha:** the second most populous governorate, with its own domestic airport and town centre
- **Al-Uwayqilah:** the smallest governorate, with local trade and services

## Cross-border trade
The Jadidat Arar crossing was closed in 1990 and reopened for regular traffic in November 2020 after almost 30 years. Since then it has become an important route for trade between Saudi Arabia and Iraq, and truck movements through the crossing rose by 81.3 per cent in the first half of 2024 compared with the same period of 2023. That trade supports clearing agents, freight companies, truck services, warehousing and fuel stations in and around Arar. The crossing also handles pilgrims from Iraq travelling to Makkah during the Hajj season.

Businesses in the region register with the Ministry of Commerce for a Commercial Registration and obtain municipal licences through the Balady platform. Exporters and importers usually need chamber membership for certificates of origin, which the Arar chamber provides.

## Finding businesses in the Northern Borders
Typical searches include customs clearance and freight agents, truck repair and spare parts, mining and industrial suppliers, contractors, equipment hire and accommodation for workers. Residents look for car dealers, clinics, schools and household services. SaudiYP lets you search Arar, Rafha and Turaif by category.

## Frequently asked questions
### What is the capital of the Northern Borders Region?
Arar is the regional capital and its largest governorate, with 202,719 residents in the 2022 census.

### Which border crossing links the region with Iraq?
The Jadidat Arar crossing, near Arar, links Saudi Arabia with Iraq. It reopened to regular traffic in 2020 after being closed in 1990.

### What is mined in the Northern Borders Region?
Phosphate is the main mineral, mined at Waad Al-Shamal near Turaif and at Al Jalamid, with processing on the Gulf coast at Ras Al Khair.

### What is the area code for Arar?
Arar and the rest of the Northern Borders Region use the 014 landline code.
