Al-Jouf Region lies in the north of Saudi Arabia, close to the Jordanian border. Its capital is Sakaka; the historic town of Dumat al-Jandal and the northern town of Al-Qurayyat are the region's other main centres.

The region has developed a distinctive economic profile. Large irrigated farms, above all olive groves, have made Al-Jouf one of the Kingdom's main food-producing areas, and its open land has attracted two of the country's best-known renewable energy plants. For businesses, that means demand for agricultural inputs, processing, energy services and logistics alongside the usual retail and public services.

## Key facts
- **Administrative status:** One of the 13 administrative regions of Saudi Arabia
- **Regional capital:** Sakaka
- **Population (2022 census, region):** 595,822 (GASTAT)
- **Main towns:** Sakaka, Dumat al-Jandal and Al-Qurayyat
- **Telephone area code:** 014
- **Airports:** Al-Jouf airport at Sakaka and Al-Qurayyat Domestic Airport
- **Chambers of commerce:** Al-Jouf Chamber and Qurayyat Chamber
- **Industrial land:** Modon Oasis in Al-Jouf, managed by MODON

## Economy and main industries
Olives are Al-Jouf's best-known product. The region had about 25 million olive trees by 2023, and the Al-Jouf Agricultural Development Company runs a very large organic olive farm. Estimates of output vary by year and measure, but one report puts the region's contribution at about 5,000 tonnes of olive oil and 2,700 tonnes of table olives. An annual olive festival in the region promotes local producers and farm development. Other crops add to a farm sector that has led the press to describe Al-Jouf as Saudi Arabia's food basket.

Renewable energy is the region's second distinctive industry. The Sakaka solar plant, a 300 MW photovoltaic project about 6 kilometres south of the Al-Jouf airport, was commissioned in April 2021 by its developers, ACWA Power, which holds 70 per cent, and AlGihaz Renewable Energy Company, which holds 30 per cent. The Dumat al-Jandal wind farm, with 400 MW of capacity, was the Kingdom's first wind farm. Together the two plants feed around 700 MW into the national grid.

Food processing and light industry are supported by Modon Oasis in Al-Jouf, while Sakaka's government offices, retail and construction sectors make up much of the urban economy.

## Main business areas and markets
- **Sakaka:** the regional capital, with government offices, the main markets, the airport and the solar plant on its southern outskirts
- **Dumat al-Jandal:** a historic town and the site of the region's wind farm
- **Al-Qurayyat:** the northern centre of the region, with its own chamber of commerce and domestic airport
- **Modon Oasis in Al-Jouf:** serviced industrial land for manufacturers and processors

## Farming, energy and logistics
The olive harvest and pressing season is the busiest period for many local businesses, from presses and bottlers to packaging suppliers and transport firms. The renewable energy plants have brought specialist operation and maintenance contractors to the region.

Businesses register with the Ministry of Commerce for a Commercial Registration and obtain a municipal licence through the Balady platform. Firms in Sakaka and Dumat al-Jandal usually join the Al-Jouf Chamber, while those in the north can join the Qurayyat Chamber.

## Frequently asked questions
### What is the area code for Al-Jouf?
Al-Jouf Region uses the 014 landline code, shared with Madinah, Tabuk and the Northern Borders.

### What is Al-Jouf known for?
Al-Jouf is known for olive farming, with about 25 million olive trees, and for the Sakaka solar plant and the Dumat al-Jandal wind farm.

### How large is the Sakaka solar plant?
The Sakaka plant is a 300 MW photovoltaic project, commissioned in April 2021. With the 400 MW Dumat al-Jandal wind farm, the region supplies around 700 MW of renewable capacity.

### Which chambers of commerce operate in Al-Jouf?
The Al-Jouf Chamber serves the region from Sakaka, and Al-Qurayyat has its own chamber of commerce.
