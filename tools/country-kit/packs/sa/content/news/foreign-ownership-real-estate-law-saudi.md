Yes, foreigners can buy property in Saudi Arabia under a new law that took effect in January 2026. The Law of Real Estate Ownership and Investment by Non-Saudis lets non-Saudi individuals and companies own property in designated zones in Riyadh and Jeddah, with tighter conditions in Makkah and Madinah, and lets foreign residents buy a home to live in.

The Council of Ministers approved the law on 8 July 2025, and it was published in the official gazette on 25 July 2025. The detailed implementing regulations, and the zones themselves, were approved in mid-2026. The Real Estate General Authority (REGA) runs the system, and applications go through its official platform, **Saudi Properties**. This explainer covers who can buy, where, the costs and the process, and flags the points that still need checking with REGA.

:::takeaways
- The new non-Saudi ownership law replaced the 2000 law and took effect in January 2026.
- Foreign ownership is allowed in designated zones in Riyadh and Jeddah, with special rules for Makkah and Madinah.
- Foreign residents with a valid iqama can own one home for their own use, subject to conditions.
- Every buyer must register with REGA, apply through Saudi Properties and pay through the official electronic payment system.
- The implementing regulations set REGA's fee at 2% on transactions in the designated zones; other taxes and costs apply separately.
:::

## How the non-Saudi ownership law came about: timeline

Before 2026, foreigners could own property only in narrow cases under a law dating from 2000, typically with special approval. The new law sets out a general framework instead.

:::steps From approval to the first applications
- 8 July 2025 | Council of Ministers approves the new law.
- 25 July 2025 | The law is published in the official gazette.
- January 2026 | The law enters into force; REGA announces it (law firms date this to 22 January 2026).
- Late June to early July 2026 | Implementing regulations and designated zones approved by the Council of Ministers.
- Now | Applications handled through REGA's Saudi Properties platform.
:::

> [!NOTE]
> Reports give slightly different dates for the regulations: one law firm cites Council of Ministers approval on 23 June 2026, another a decision dated in early July 2026. The legal effect is the same, but always rely on the official text published by REGA at https://rega.gov.sa.

## Who can buy property in Saudi Arabia under the new law

The law treats different buyers differently. Here is the broad picture, based on the law and law-firm summaries of the regulations.

| Buyer | What they can own | Main conditions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foreign resident (valid iqama) | One residential property for personal housing | Not in Makkah or Madinah except under their special rules |
| Non-resident foreign individual | Property within designated zones | Registration with REGA; zone and use limits |
| Foreign company | Property within designated zones | Must register with REGA and disclose owners and controllers |
| Unlisted Saudi company with foreign shareholders | Property outside the zones for business or staff housing | Subject to the regulations' conditions; not Makkah or Madinah |
| Muslim foreign individuals and Saudi companies with foreign shareholders | Property in designated zones in Makkah and Madinah | Stricter rules; check the exact conditions with REGA |
| GCC nationals | Covered by their own existing rules, with some changes | Check the current position with REGA |

### Owning a home does not give you residency

Owning property does not automatically give you an iqama or residency. Residency remains a separate process. If you are a resident, keep your iqama valid; our guide to [iqama issuance and renewal](/expat-guide/iqama-issuance-renewal) explains how.

## Where foreigners can buy: the designated zones

The Council of Ministers, on REGA's proposal and with the approval of the Council of Economic and Development Affairs, decides the zones where non-Saudis may own property. The zones approved in 2026 are specified areas within **Riyadh, Jeddah, Makkah and Madinah**. Outside them, foreign ownership is generally not allowed, apart from the resident and company cases above.

Zone boundaries are set by maps, not by city names. A development being "in Riyadh" does not mean it is inside a designated zone.

> [!WARNING]
> Never pay a deposit on the strength of a marketing claim that a project is "open to foreigners". Ask the developer or broker for written confirmation that the plot falls inside a designated zone, and check it against REGA's published maps before you sign.

## Fees and costs when foreigners buy property in Saudi Arabia

The law allowed a fee of up to 5% on non-Saudi transactions. The implementing regulations set REGA's fee at **2%** on disposals of properties in the designated zones of Riyadh, Jeddah, Makkah and Madinah.

That fee is not the only cost. Budget for the following as well:

- **Real estate transaction tax (RETT):** 5% on most property sales in Saudi Arabia, charged separately.
- **Brokerage commission:** agreed with your broker; get it in writing.
- **Legal and due diligence fees:** for title checks, zone confirmation and contract review.
- **Translation and attestation:** if documents from abroad must be used.
- **Financing costs:** if you take a mortgage, where available to your category of buyer.

:::stats Key numbers in the new framework
- 2% | REGA fee on transactions in designated zones
- 5% | real estate transaction tax on most sales
- 1 | home a foreign resident may own for personal use
- 4 | cities with designated zones
:::

## How to apply: registration and the Saudi Properties platform

REGA requires non-Saudi buyers, and Saudi companies with non-Saudi shareholders, to register before they buy. Ownership becomes valid only once it is recorded in the national real estate register.

1. **Register with REGA** through the official digital platform, Saudi Properties.
2. **Foreign companies without a presence in the Kingdom** may first need a unified number through the Ministry of Investment; check the current requirement.
3. **Confirm eligibility and zone** for the specific property.
4. **Agree terms** with the seller, using a licensed broker.
5. **Pay through the official electronic payment system**, which is linked to the Saudi Central Bank's payment infrastructure. The regulations require all payments linked to the purchase to go through this system.
6. **Register the title** so your ownership is legally recognised.

:::checklist
- Valid passport, and iqama if you are a resident
- Proof of funds and the bank account you will pay from
- Company documents and ownership details, if buying through a company
- Written confirmation that the property is in a designated zone
- Title deed details from the seller, checked by a lawyer
- Translated and attested documents where needed
:::

## What the law means for the property market and local businesses

The new rules widen the pool of buyers in Riyadh and Jeddah. That matters for developers, brokers and [property sales and development firms](/categories/property-sales-development), and for the services around a sale: legal advice, valuation, interior fit-out and moving.

For renters, very little changes immediately. In Riyadh, the [five-year rent freeze](/news/riyadh-rent-freeze-what-tenants-landlords-need-to-know) still applies to leases inside the urban boundary, including on properties bought by foreign investors.

### Points still to watch

- Publication of detailed zone maps and any later changes to them
- How Makkah and Madinah rules are applied in practice
- Bank lending policies for non-Saudi buyers
- Any fee changes set by later decisions

:::faq
### Can foreigners buy property in Saudi Arabia in 2026?
Yes. The Law of Real Estate Ownership and Investment by Non-Saudis took effect in January 2026. It allows non-Saudi individuals and companies to own property in designated zones within Riyadh, Jeddah, Makkah and Madinah, with stricter rules in the two holy cities. Foreign residents can also own one home for personal use, subject to conditions set by REGA.

### Can expats with an iqama buy a house in Saudi Arabia?
Yes. Foreign individuals legally resident in Saudi Arabia may own one residential property for their own housing, subject to conditions. The general resident route does not apply in Makkah and Madinah, which have their own rules. Owning a home does not grant residency, so you still need to keep your iqama valid separately.

### Where can foreigners buy property in Saudi Arabia?
In designated geographic zones within Riyadh, Jeddah, Makkah and Madinah, approved by the Council of Ministers in 2026 on REGA's proposal. Outside these zones, foreign ownership is generally not allowed, except for residents buying a home and Saudi companies with foreign shareholders buying for business use or staff housing. Always check the exact zone maps with REGA.

### Can non-Muslims buy property in Makkah or Madinah?
No. Ownership in Makkah and Madinah is limited. Under the regulations, Muslim foreign individuals and Saudi companies with non-Saudi shareholders may own property in designated zones in those cities, under stricter conditions. Non-Muslims cannot enter Makkah and so cannot own there. Check REGA's official text for the exact conditions that apply.

### What fees do foreigners pay when buying property?
The implementing regulations set REGA's fee at 2% on transactions involving properties in the designated zones. The real estate transaction tax of 5% applies separately to most property sales. You should also budget for brokerage commission, legal fees, translation and attestation and any financing costs. Fees can change, so confirm current rates with REGA before you buy.

### Does buying property give me Saudi residency?
No. Owning property in Saudi Arabia does not automatically give you residency or an iqama. Residency is handled through separate routes, such as employment or other residency programmes. If you are already resident, make sure your iqama stays valid, because your eligibility as a resident buyer depends on it.

### How do foreigners apply to buy property?
Applications go through REGA's official digital platform, Saudi Properties. You register as a non-Saudi buyer, confirm your eligibility and the property's zone, agree terms through a licensed broker, pay through the official electronic payment system and then register the title. Ownership is valid only after it is recorded in the national real estate register.

### Can foreign companies buy property in Saudi Arabia?
Yes, within the designated zones and after registering with REGA, which requires disclosure of direct and indirect owners and controllers. Unlisted Saudi companies with foreign shareholders can also own property outside the zones for their business activities or staff housing, except in Makkah and Madinah. Foreign companies without a Saudi presence may first need a Ministry of Investment number.

### When did the new foreign ownership law start?
The Council of Ministers approved the law on 8 July 2025 and it was published in the official gazette on 25 July 2025. It entered into force in January 2026, with law firms dating this to 22 January 2026. The implementing regulations and designated zones were approved by the Council of Ministers in mid-2026.

### Can foreigners get a mortgage in Saudi Arabia?
It depends on the bank and on your buyer category. The ownership law does not guarantee financing, and each bank applies its own lending policy to non-Saudi buyers. Ask several banks early about eligibility, deposit requirements and processing times, and make sure any financing fits the official payment process required for non-Saudi transactions.
:::
