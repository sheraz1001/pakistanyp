The next group of businesses to join **ZATCA e-invoicing** Phase 2 is Wave 25. The Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA) has set the threshold at VAT-subject revenue above **SAR 187,500** in any of the years 2022, 2023, 2024 or 2025, and businesses it notifies must connect their invoicing systems to the Fatoora platform by **1 February 2027**.

That threshold is half the SAR 375,000 used for Wave 24, whose deadline was 30 June 2026. In practice, it brings in many small shops, workshops, clinics and service firms that registered for VAT but have not yet integrated. This explainer sets out how the waves work, what integration means and what to do in the next three months.

:::takeaways
- Wave 25 covers taxpayers with VAT-subject revenue above SAR 187,500 in 2022, 2023, 2024 or 2025.
- ZATCA's integration deadline for notified Wave 25 taxpayers is 1 February 2027.
- Wave 24 (revenue above SAR 375,000 in 2022–2024) had to integrate between 1 April and 30 June 2026.
- ZATCA says it notifies each wave directly at least six months before its integration date.
- Integration means an approved e-invoicing solution, onboarding on Fatoora, and sending invoices to ZATCA in real time or within 24 hours.
:::

## How ZATCA e-invoicing works: Phase 1 and Phase 2

Saudi Arabia's e-invoicing rules (known as *Fatoora*, the name of ZATCA's platform) came in two phases.

**Phase 1, the Generation phase,** started on 4 December 2021. Every VAT-registered business had to stop issuing handwritten or simple word-processor invoices and use an electronic system to generate and store invoices, with a QR code on simplified (consumer) invoices.

**Phase 2, the Integration phase,** started on 1 January 2023. It requires your invoicing system to connect to Fatoora and exchange invoices with ZATCA. Because this is a bigger technical step, ZATCA rolls it out in waves, starting with the largest companies and moving down by revenue.

:::steps From paper invoices to Fatoora integration
- 4 December 2021 | Phase 1 begins: all VAT-registered businesses issue electronic invoices.
- 1 January 2023 | Phase 2 begins with Wave 1 (revenue above SAR 3 billion in 2021).
- 2023 to 2025 | Waves 2 to 23 bring in progressively smaller businesses.
- 1 April to 30 June 2026 | Wave 24 (above SAR 375,000) integrates.
- By 1 February 2027 | Wave 25 (above SAR 187,500) must integrate.
:::

## Wave 25: the new ZATCA e-invoicing threshold explained

ZATCA published the Wave 25 criteria in late July 2026. Three points matter.

### The SAR 187,500 test

You fall into Wave 25 if your revenue subject to VAT exceeded SAR 187,500 in **any one** of 2022, 2023, 2024 or 2025. One strong year is enough, even if your turnover has since dropped.

### Why SAR 187,500 matters

SAR 187,500 is also the threshold for voluntary VAT registration, while SAR 375,000 is the mandatory registration threshold. In plain terms, Wave 24 covered businesses above the compulsory VAT line, and Wave 25 reaches down to the smaller firms that sit between the two.

### Notification comes first

ZATCA notifies the taxpayers in each wave directly. Check the email and phone number on your ZATCA account are current, because that is where the notice goes.

:::bars Phase 2 revenue thresholds for recent waves (SAR)
- Wave 23 | 750000 | Above SAR 750,000
- Wave 24 | 375000 | Above SAR 375,000
- Wave 25 | 187500 | Above SAR 187,500
:::

> [!NOTE]
> Wave dates and thresholds come from ZATCA announcements, as summarised by tax advisers. The official list of waves, the technical rules and the latest guidance are on https://zatca.gov.sa. Check there before you sign a contract with a provider.

## What Fatoora integration actually involves

Integration is mainly a software job, but it changes how you issue invoices day to day.

- **An approved e-invoicing solution.** Your accounting or POS system must meet ZATCA's technical rules: structured XML (or PDF/A-3 with XML embedded), a unique identifier, a cryptographic stamp and a hash that links each invoice to the one before it.
- **Onboarding on Fatoora.** You register each device or system unit on the Fatoora portal, usually with a one-time password, so ZATCA can recognise it.
- **Clearance for B2B invoices.** Standard tax invoices to other businesses go to ZATCA for clearance before you share them with the buyer.
- **Reporting for B2C invoices.** Simplified invoices to consumers are issued straight away and reported to ZATCA within 24 hours.

### Phase 1 versus Phase 2 at a glance

| Feature | Phase 1 (Generation) | Phase 2 (Integration) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start date | 4 December 2021 | From 1 January 2023, in waves |
| Who | All VAT-registered businesses | Notified taxpayers, wave by wave |
| Invoice format | Electronic, with QR code on simplified invoices | Structured XML or PDF/A-3, stamped and hashed |
| Connection to ZATCA | None required | Real-time clearance (B2B) or reporting within 24 hours (B2C) |
| System | Any compliant electronic system | Solution onboarded on Fatoora |

- 1 Feb 2027 | integration deadline
- 6 months | minimum notice ZATCA gives each wave
:::

## What to do now if you might be in Wave 25

Three months is tight for a small business, mostly because good providers get busy near each deadline. Start now.

:::checklist
- Pull your VAT returns for 2022 to 2025 and check whether any year exceeded SAR 187,500
- Confirm the contact email and mobile on your ZATCA account
- Ask your current accounting or POS supplier, in writing, whether their product is Phase 2 compliant
- List every device that issues invoices, including tablets and branch tills
- Agree an onboarding date with your provider well before 1 February 2027
- Test clearance and reporting with real sample invoices
- Train staff on credit notes, debit notes and cancelled sales
:::

> [!TIP]
> Ask any provider for proof of compliance with the current Fatoora technical specifications, a clear onboarding timeline, and what happens to your old invoice history. Avoid open-ended contracts until you have seen a working demo with your own invoice types.

## Penalties and enforcement: what we know

Missing your wave deadline is treated as a breach of the e-invoicing rules. Advisers commonly quote penalties of SAR 5,000 to SAR 50,000 per violation, with ZATCA usually starting with a warning and escalating for repeat or serious breaches.

ZATCA also ran an initiative to cancel fines and exempt taxpayers from penalties, which was extended to 30 June 2026. We could not confirm whether it has been extended again, so check the current position on ZATCA's website rather than assuming it still applies.

> [!WARNING]
> Plan as if the Wave 25 deadline is fixed. No extension has been announced, and a rushed integration usually costs more than starting early.

## Who else is affected: suppliers, landlords and freelancers

Even if you are below the threshold, your customers may not be. Larger buyers that have already integrated may ask for invoices that meet Fatoora requirements before they pay.

- **Small suppliers** to companies in earlier waves should expect stricter invoice checks.
- **Commercial landlords** registered for VAT need compliant rent invoices too.
- **Freelancers and home-based businesses** that registered voluntarily for VAT may be inside Wave 25 even if they think of themselves as very small.

### What is ZATCA e-invoicing Wave 25?
Wave 25 is the next group of taxpayers required to integrate with ZATCA's Fatoora platform under Phase 2 of e-invoicing. It covers businesses whose revenue subject to VAT exceeded SAR 187,500 in any of 2022, 2023, 2024 or 2025. ZATCA notifies those taxpayers directly, and they must complete integration by 1 February 2027.

### What is the deadline for Wave 25 integration?
Taxpayers notified by ZATCA under Wave 25 must integrate their e-invoicing solutions with the Fatoora platform by 1 February 2027. ZATCA has said it notifies each wave at least six months before its integration date. If you think you qualify but have not received a notice, check your ZATCA account and contact details, and ask ZATCA or your tax adviser.

### What was the Wave 24 deadline?
Wave 24 covered VAT-registered taxpayers whose VAT-subject revenue exceeded SAR 375,000 in 2022, 2023 or 2024. They had to integrate with Fatoora between 1 April and 30 June 2026. If you were in Wave 24 and have not integrated, speak to a tax adviser or ZATCA promptly, because you may already be in breach of the rules.

### What is the difference between Phase 1 and Phase 2?
Phase 1, which started on 4 December 2021, requires all VAT-registered businesses to generate and store invoices electronically. Phase 2, which began on 1 January 2023 and is rolled out in waves, requires your system to connect to Fatoora. B2B invoices are cleared by ZATCA before sharing, and B2C invoices are reported within 24 hours.

### Do small businesses need e-invoicing in Saudi Arabia?
Yes. Every VAT-registered business has had to issue electronic invoices since Phase 1 began in December 2021. Phase 2 integration applies wave by wave, and Wave 25 reaches businesses with VAT-subject revenue above SAR 187,500. Businesses that are not registered for VAT are outside the e-invoicing rules, but they should check whether they now need to register.

### What are the penalties for not complying with e-invoicing?
Tax advisers commonly cite penalties of SAR 5,000 to SAR 50,000 per violation, often after an initial warning, with higher amounts for repeat breaches. ZATCA also ran a fine-cancellation initiative that was extended to 30 June 2026. Check ZATCA's official website for the current penalty schedule and whether any relief is still available.

### Can I use Excel or Word to issue invoices?
No. Since Phase 1, handwritten invoices and invoices made in basic word-processing or spreadsheet tools are not accepted for VAT-registered businesses. You need an electronic invoicing solution that meets ZATCA's requirements, and for Phase 2 it must also be able to connect to Fatoora, stamp each invoice and send it for clearance or reporting.

### How do I know if my e-invoicing software is compliant?
Ask your provider for written confirmation that the solution meets ZATCA's current Phase 2 technical requirements and can be onboarded on Fatoora. Ask for a demo using your own invoice types, including credit notes. ZATCA publishes the technical specifications and guidance on its website, which you or your adviser can use to check claims.

### What is a simplified tax invoice?
A simplified tax invoice is the invoice you issue to consumers, typically at a till or for B2C sales. Under Phase 2, it is issued to the customer straight away and reported to ZATCA within 24 hours, and it carries a QR code. Standard tax invoices, used for sales to other VAT-registered businesses, must be cleared by ZATCA first.

### Will there be a Wave 26?
ZATCA has not announced a Wave 26 at the time of writing. Each wave so far has been announced separately, with its own threshold and deadline, and ZATCA has said it notifies taxpayers at least six months before their integration date. Follow ZATCA's announcements on its official website for any future wave.
