The **Saudi World Cup 2034** is confirmed: FIFA named Saudi Arabia sole host of the 2034 men's tournament at an Extraordinary FIFA Congress on 11 December 2024. It will be the first 48-team World Cup played in a single country, and the bid placed matches in five cities: Riyadh, Jeddah, Al Khobar, Abha and NEOM.

Eight years out, the business story is about building and planning rather than ticket sales. Stadium construction is under way in several cities, while 2026 brought reports that plans are being trimmed, including a hold on the NEOM stadium. For local firms, the opportunity runs through construction now, and through hospitality, transport, food and services as the tournament approaches.

:::takeaways

- FIFA has not yet confirmed tournament dates.
- Riyadh's pipeline of events, from Riyadh Season to Expo 2030, means demand builds well before 2034.

## How Saudi Arabia won the 2034 World Cup

FIFA ran the 2030 and 2034 decisions together. On 11 December 2024, its 211 member associations met virtually at an Extraordinary Congress and confirmed Morocco, Portugal and Spain as 2030 hosts, with centenary matches in Argentina, Paraguay and Uruguay, and Saudi Arabia as sole host for 2034.

Saudi Arabia was unopposed after Australia said in October 2023 that it would not bid. The decision drew criticism from human rights groups, including Amnesty International, over worker protections, and Norway's federation abstained over concerns about the bidding process. FIFA's own announcement is at https://inside.fifa.com.

## What changed in 2026: stadium plans under review

Two developments this year are worth knowing about, and both need careful reading.

First, Reuters reported that the planned NEOM stadium had been put on hold indefinitely. The tournament's hosting authority said construction milestones were making substantial progress overall, but gave no details on NEOM.

Second, reports in October 2026 said Saudi Arabia was considering cutting the number of stadiums from 15 to 10 as it reassessed spending on several large projects. These were reports of options under consideration, not an announced decision.

> [!NOTE]
> The final list of venues has to be agreed with FIFA. Until either FIFA or the Saudi organisers publish a revised plan, treat stadium counts and city allocations as the bid plan, subject to change.

Work continues elsewhere. Aramco Stadium in Al Khobar, King Salman International Stadium and Prince Mohammed bin Salman Stadium in Qiddiya have all been reported as in progress, with the King Salman stadium expected around 2029.

## When will the Saudi World Cup 2034 be played?

FIFA has not confirmed the dates. The tournament will almost certainly avoid the Saudi summer, as Qatar 2022 did when it moved to November and December. Some commentators have suggested January 2035 because a late-2034 window would overlap with Ramadan and with the 2034 Asian Games in Riyadh, but FIFA has said nothing official.

For planning, assume a cooler-season tournament and wait for FIFA's announcement before fixing anything that depends on exact dates, such as hotel pricing or seasonal staffing contracts.

## Business opportunities: construction first, services later

The tournament creates demand in waves. Firms that understand the timing can build capacity gradually rather than all at once.

### 2026 to 2030: construction and infrastructure

Many of the same firms are working on [Expo 2030 Riyadh](/news/expo-2030-riyadh-opportunities-for-smes), so supply chains overlap.

### 2030 to 2034: hospitality and services

As the tournament nears, demand shifts to [hotels and furnished units](/categories/hotels-furnished-units), restaurants, [transport and taxi services](/categories/transport-taxis), [car rental](/categories/car-rental-leasing), security, cleaning and event staffing. ## Risks to plan around

A home World Cup is a long-term opportunity, but not a guaranteed one.

- **Changing plans.** Stadium numbers and locations may change, as 2026 reports show.
- **Labour standards.** International scrutiny of worker welfare is high. Contractors with weak practices risk being dropped by main contractors.
- **Over-investment.** Short tournament peaks can leave businesses with too much capacity afterwards. Our list of [business ideas in Saudi Arabia](/blogs/small-business-ideas-in-saudi-arabia) can help you find year-round demand alongside event work.

> [!TIP]
> Smaller events come first. Riyadh Season runs every winter, Saudi Arabia hosts the AFC Asian Cup from 7 January to 5 February 2027 in Riyadh, Jeddah and Al Khobar, and the Expo opens in 2030. Use them to test your capacity, pricing and staffing before the World Cup.

## Final word: the Saudi World Cup 2034 is a decade-long project

The Saudi World Cup 2034 will not reward businesses that wait until the year before. Construction demand is here now, service demand will grow after 2030, and plans will keep changing as the government reviews spending. Track official announcements, build your capacity step by step, and make sure the companies placing orders can find you.

### How will the World Cup benefit local businesses in Saudi Arabia?
Construction and supply firms benefit first, through stadiums, hotels and transport projects. Closer to 2034, demand moves to hotels, restaurants, transport, car rental, security, cleaning, event staffing and retail. Sports clubs and academies may see growing interest before the tournament. Firms with strong compliance records are best placed to win work from main contractors.

### Is the NEOM stadium cancelled?
Reuters reported in 2026 that plans for the NEOM stadium had been put on hold indefinitely. The tournament's hosting authority said overall construction milestones were progressing but did not comment on NEOM specifically. A hold is not the same as an official cancellation, so check announcements from the organisers and FIFA for the final venue list.

### How many teams will play in the 2034 World Cup?
The 2034 World Cup will feature 48 teams, using the expanded format that debuts at the 2026 tournament in North America. The 48-team format has 104 matches. Saudi Arabia will be the first country to host this format on its own, which is why the bid needed 15 stadiums across five cities.

### Why was the Saudi 2034 World Cup bid criticised?
Human rights organisations, including Amnesty International, criticised FIFA's decision, citing risks to migrant workers building the infrastructure and wider rights concerns. Norway's football federation abstained over concerns about the bidding process. Saudi authorities have pointed to labour reforms. For businesses, it means worker welfare will be closely watched on World Cup projects.

### What other major events is Saudi Arabia hosting before 2034?
Saudi Arabia is due to host the AFC Asian Cup from 7 January to 5 February 2027, Expo 2030 Riyadh from October 2030 to March 2031 and the Asian Games in Riyadh in 2034, alongside Riyadh Season every winter. Together they create a long run of demand for hospitality, events, transport and construction businesses, rather than a single peak.
