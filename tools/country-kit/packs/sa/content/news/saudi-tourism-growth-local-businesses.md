**Saudi tourism** set new records in 2025. According to the Ministry of Tourism's annual statistical report, the Kingdom recorded about 123 million domestic and inbound tourists, roughly 6% more than in 2024, and total tourism spending reached a record SAR 304 billion (about USD 81 billion), up 7%.

For hotels, restaurants, cafés, tour operators, drivers and shops, those figures translate into more guests and a longer busy season. Early 2026 data points the same way: preliminary first-quarter figures showed about 37.2 million tourists and SAR 82.7 billion in spending. This explainer breaks down the numbers and what they mean for local businesses.

:::takeaways
- Saudi Arabia recorded about 123 million tourists in 2025, combining 93.3 million domestic and 29.3 million inbound tourists.
- Total tourism spending reached SAR 304 billion: SAR 176.6 billion from inbound visitors and SAR 127.1 billion from domestic tourists.
- Tourism supported about 1.03 million jobs in 2025, according to the ministry.
- The Vision 2030 target is now 150 million visits a year by 2030, after the original 100 million goal was passed in 2023.
- Preliminary data for the first quarter of 2026 showed domestic tourism up about 16% year on year.
:::

## Saudi tourism in 2025: the headline figures

The Ministry of Tourism published its 2025 annual statistical report in 2026. Its figures update the preliminary numbers announced at the World Economic Forum in January 2026, which put visitors at about 122 million and spending at around SAR 300 billion.

:::stats Tourism in 2025
- 123 million | domestic and inbound tourists
- SAR 304 billion | total tourism spending
- 1.03 million | jobs in the tourism sector
- 47% | share of tourism jobs held by Saudis that are held by Saudi women
:::

The jobs figure stands out. The ministry says Saudi women held 47% of the tourism jobs held by Saudi nationals in 2025, up from 5% in 2018. That shift affects how hospitality businesses recruit and meet Saudization targets.

What does this mean in practice? Inbound visitors, including pilgrims and business travellers, drive spending on hotels, flights and organised tours. Domestic travellers drive volume: weekend trips, family holidays, events and seasonal festivals.

> [!NOTE]
> "Tourists" in the ministry's figures include religious, business, leisure and family visits, and domestic trips by residents. The full methodology and latest releases are on the Ministry of Tourism's website, https://mt.gov.sa.

## Early 2026: what the first-quarter figures show

Preliminary ministry data for January to March 2026 showed about 37.2 million tourists and total spending of SAR 82.7 billion.

- **Domestic tourism** rose about 16% year on year to around 28.9 million tourists, with spending up about 8% to SAR 34.7 billion.
- **Inbound tourists** were about a fifth of the total but generated nearly 60% of spending, at around SAR 48 billion.
- **Hotel occupancy** in tourist accommodation averaged around 59% in the quarter, with Madinah highest at about 82% and Makkah at around 60%, according to reporting on the ministry's data.

The quarter included Ramadan and Eid al-Fitr, which lifted travel across the Kingdom. Full-year 2026 figures will not be published until 2027, so treat any annual 2026 estimate you see now with caution.

## How tourism growth reaches local businesses

Tourism money spreads well beyond hotels. Here is where the benefit tends to land.

### Accommodation

[Hotels and furnished units](/categories/hotels-furnished-units) gain most directly, especially in [Madinah](/cities/madinah) and Makkah, where occupancy is highest. Smaller operators, such as furnished apartments and [rest houses and chalets](/categories/rest-houses-chalets), benefit from domestic family travel at weekends and in holidays.

### Food and coffee

Visitors eat out more than residents. [Saudi and Gulf restaurants](/categories/saudi-gulf-cuisine), [cafés and coffee shops](/categories/cafes-coffee) and [date shops](/categories/dates-date-shops) catch both everyday spending and gifts to take home.

### Tours, transport and experiences

[Tour operators](/categories/tour-operators), [desert and outdoor activity providers](/categories/desert-outdoor), [car rental firms](/categories/car-rental-leasing) and drivers serve visitors who want to see more than one city. Domestic trips to the Asir highlands, AlUla and the Red Sea coast support local guides and rental businesses.

### Religious travel

Pilgrims for Umrah and Hajj remain a core part of inbound visits. [Hajj and Umrah service providers](/categories/hajj-umrah) work within their own licensing rules, but transport, food, retail and accommodation around the two holy cities all benefit.

## A practical checklist for small businesses

Higher visitor numbers do not automatically bring customers to your door. Visitors find businesses online first, often in a language other than Arabic.

:::checklist
- Make sure your business appears with accurate details, hours and location online
- Add English (and other key languages) to menus, signs and listings
- Accept cards and mobile payments, including for small amounts
- Collect reviews from visitors and reply to them
- Plan staffing around Ramadan, Eid, school holidays and event seasons
- Check your licences, including tourism licences where your activity needs one
:::

## What comes next: events, targets and risks

The Vision 2030 target is now 150 million visits a year by 2030, raised after the original 100 million target was passed in 2023. Big events will help: the AFC Asian Cup in January and February 2027, [Expo 2030 Riyadh](/news/expo-2030-riyadh-opportunities-for-smes) from October 2030, and the [Saudi World Cup 2034](/news/world-cup-2034-saudi-arabia-business-opportunities).

There are risks too. Regional tensions can affect international travel, new hotel supply can push down room rates, and staffing costs are rising as hospitality competes for workers. Businesses that rely on a single season or a single source of visitors are most exposed.

## Final word: Saudi tourism growth favours businesses that are easy to find

Saudi tourism has gone from a niche to one of the economy's fastest-growing sectors, and the 2025 figures confirm the trend. For local businesses, the gains go to those who are visible, well reviewed, open when visitors want them and able to serve guests in more than one language.

### How many tourists visited Saudi Arabia in 2025?
According to the Ministry of Tourism's 2025 annual statistical report, Saudi Arabia recorded about 123 million domestic and inbound tourists in 2025, up roughly 6% from 2024. That figure combines 93.3 million domestic tourists and 29.3 million inbound tourists. Earlier preliminary figures announced in January 2026 had put the total at about 122 million.

### How much did tourists spend in Saudi Arabia in 2025?
Total tourism spending reached a record SAR 304 billion, about USD 81 billion, up around 7% on 2024, according to the Ministry of Tourism. Inbound tourists spent SAR 176.6 billion and domestic tourists SAR 127.1 billion. Preliminary figures announced earlier in 2026 had put total spending at around SAR 300 billion.

### What is Saudi Arabia's tourism target for 2030?
The target is 150 million visits a year by 2030. Saudi Arabia originally aimed for 100 million annual visits under Vision 2030, but passed that level in 2023, well ahead of schedule, and raised the goal. The target includes domestic and international visits, so both resident travel and inbound tourism count towards it.

### How many people work in Saudi tourism?
The Ministry of Tourism says the sector employed about 1.03 million people in 2025. Saudi women accounted for 47% of tourism jobs held by Saudi nationals, up from 5% in 2018. For hospitality employers, that means a growing pool of Saudi staff, which also matters for meeting Nitaqat Saudization targets.

### What were Saudi tourism figures for early 2026?
Preliminary ministry data for the first quarter of 2026 showed about 37.2 million tourists and SAR 82.7 billion in spending. Domestic tourism grew about 16% to roughly 28.9 million tourists. Inbound visitors made up about a fifth of tourists but nearly 60% of spending. Full-year 2026 figures are expected in 2027.

### Which Saudi cities get the most tourists?
Makkah and Madinah receive very large numbers of religious visitors, and Madinah recorded the highest hotel occupancy, at about 82%, in the first quarter of 2026. Riyadh and Jeddah attract business travel, events and leisure visitors, while destinations such as AlUla, the Asir highlands and the Red Sea coast are growing in domestic and international leisure travel.

### How can a small business benefit from Saudi tourism growth?
Be easy to find and easy to buy from. Keep accurate online listings with hours, location and photos, add English to menus and signs, accept card and mobile payments, collect reviews and plan staffing around holidays and event seasons. Check whether your activity needs a tourism licence, especially for accommodation and tours.

### Do I need a licence to rent out a holiday home in Saudi Arabia?
Tourist accommodation, including private holiday homes and furnished units rented to visitors, is regulated by the Ministry of Tourism, which licenses accommodation activities. Requirements depend on the type of property and how you rent it. Check the current licensing rules on the Ministry of Tourism's website before you list a property for short stays.

### Is domestic tourism growing in Saudi Arabia?
Yes. Domestic tourists numbered about 93.3 million in 2025 and spent SAR 127.1 billion, according to the Ministry of Tourism. Preliminary data for the first quarter of 2026 showed domestic tourism up about 16% year on year, helped by Ramadan and Eid al-Fitr travel. Weekend and holiday trips by residents drive much of this demand.

### Where can I find official Saudi tourism statistics?
The Ministry of Tourism publishes annual statistical reports and quarterly releases on its website, mt.gov.sa, including visitor numbers, spending, occupancy and employment. The General Authority for Statistics also publishes related data. News reports often quote preliminary figures first, so check whether a number is preliminary or final before relying on it.
