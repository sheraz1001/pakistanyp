The biggest changes to **Saudi labour law** in a decade took effect on 19 February 2025. Probation can now last up to 180 days, employees on open-ended contracts give 30 days' notice instead of 60, resignation has a legal definition, and maternity leave rose from 10 to 12 weeks on full pay.

The amendments were published in the official gazette on 23 August 2024 and came into force 180 days later. The Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD) followed in 2026 with a revised, stricter schedule of violations and penalties. This explainer covers what changed, what it means for your contracts and payroll, and where details still need checking against the official text.

:::takeaways
- The amendments took effect on 19 February 2025, after publication in the official gazette on 23 August 2024.
- Probation is capped at 180 days in total, and either party may end the contract during it.
- On indefinite contracts, employees give 30 days' notice; employers still give 60.
- Maternity leave is 12 weeks on full pay, with six weeks to be taken after the birth.
- MHRSD's revised penalty schedule in 2026 sets stricter fines, many charged per worker.
:::

## The main Saudi labour law changes at a glance

The table compares the main points before and after the amendments, based on law-firm summaries of the amended law. The official Arabic text is on the Bureau of Experts' legal portal, https://laws.boe.gov.sa.

| Topic | Before February 2025 | After 19 February 2025 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Probation | Up to 90 days, extendable in writing to 180 | Up to 180 days in total |
| Ending during probation | Allowed unless the contract said otherwise | Either party may end the contract |
| Employee notice (indefinite contract) | 60 days | 30 days |
| Employer notice (indefinite contract) | 60 days | 60 days |
| Resignation | No statutory definition | Defined in law, with rights preserved |
| Fixed-term contract with no stated term (non-Saudis) | Tied to the work permit | One year from the actual start date, renewing for the same period |
| Maternity leave | 10 weeks on full pay | 12 weeks on full pay, 6 weeks mandatory after birth |

:::stats Key numbers in the amended law
- 180 days | maximum probation period
- 30 days | employee notice on an indefinite contract
- 12 weeks | maternity leave on full pay
- 2 months' wage | minimum compensation for unjustified termination
:::

## Probation, notice and resignation

### Probation

You can now agree a probation period of up to 180 days without a separate written extension. Both employer and employee can end the contract during probation. If you already use a 90-day probation, nothing forces you to change it, but new contracts may use the longer period.

### Notice periods

On an indefinite (open-ended) contract, an employee who resigns now gives **30 days'** notice. An employer ending the contract for a valid reason must still give **60 days**. The law also lets an employer postpone accepting a resignation for up to 60 days where the work requires it, with written reasons.

Use our [notice period calculator](/calculators/notice-period) to work out the last working day.

### Resignation

The amended law defines resignation and confirms that an employee whose contract ends by resignation keeps the rights the law gives them. Law firms note the definition is tied to fixed-term contracts, which matters because most contracts for non-Saudis are fixed-term.

> [!NOTE]
> MHRSD has reaffirmed that contracts for foreign employees stay fixed-term even after renewal, under Article 37. If you are an expat, check your contract type before you resign. Our guide to [employment rights in Saudi Arabia](/expat-guide/employment-rights) explains the basics.

## Termination and compensation under Article 77

Article 77 covers compensation when a contract is ended without a valid reason. The amendments let the parties agree compensation in the contract itself. Where they have not, the default is:

- **Indefinite contracts:** 15 days' wage for each year of service.
- **Fixed-term contracts:** the wage for the remaining term.
- **In either case:** never less than two months' wage.

This compensation is separate from end-of-service benefits. To estimate what you are owed when a job ends, use our [end of service calculator](/calculators/end-of-service), and for holiday pay due on exit, the [unused leave calculator](/calculators/unused-leave).

## Leave changes: maternity and other leave

Maternity leave is now **12 weeks** on full pay, up from 10. Six weeks must be taken after the birth, and the leave can begin up to four weeks before the expected delivery date. Law-firm commentary also reports a new three-day bereavement leave for the death of a sibling.

Annual leave rules were not part of the headline changes. Staff still earn 21 days a year, rising to 30 days after five years with the same employer. Our [annual leave calculator](/calculators/annual-leave) applies these rules.

> [!TIP]
> Update your leave policy and payroll settings at the same time as your contracts. A policy that still says 10 weeks of maternity leave is now out of line with the law, and maternity leave breaches carry their own fines under the revised penalty schedule.

## The 2026 penalty schedule: stricter Saudi labour law enforcement

In February 2026 MHRSD issued a ministerial decision revising its schedule of labour violations and penalties. The ministry described the new schedule as more detailed and significantly stricter, with many fines charged per violation and per affected worker.

Reported examples include:

- **SAR 10,000** per worker for employing a foreign worker without a valid work permit
- **SAR 3,000** per worker for keeping an employee's passport or iqama
- **SAR 1,000** per worker for failing to give statutory maternity leave
- doubled fines or temporary suspension for repeat violations

Reports differ on some amounts and dates, so check the official schedule on MHRSD's website, https://www.hrsd.gov.sa, before relying on a figure.

> [!WARNING]
> Holding an employee's passport or iqama is a violation, even with their agreement. If you keep documents "for safekeeping", give them back.

## What employers should do now

If you have not yet reviewed your paperwork since February 2025, these steps cover the essentials.

:::steps Labour law compliance in six steps
- Review contracts | Update probation, notice and compensation clauses.
- Check contract type | Confirm fixed-term and indefinite contracts are recorded correctly on Qiwa.
- Update policies | Revise leave, resignation and disciplinary policies.
- Fix payroll | Apply the 12-week maternity leave and correct notice pay.
- Return documents | Make sure no passports or iqamas are held by the company.
- Train managers | Brief line managers on probation and notice rules.
:::

:::checklist
- Employment contracts reflect the 180-day probation cap
- Notice clauses state 30 days for employees and 60 for the employer on indefinite contracts
- Article 77 compensation is set out in the contract or left to the legal default
- Maternity policy says 12 weeks, with six weeks after the birth
- Contracts are registered on Qiwa, the ministry's labour platform
- GOSI registration is correct for every employee
:::

## What the changes mean for employees

For staff, the headline gains are clearer resignation rights, shorter notice when you leave an indefinite contract, and longer maternity leave. The longer possible probation is the main change that favours employers.

If you are an expat thinking about a move, read our guide to [changing employers in Saudi Arabia](/expat-guide/changing-employers) and check your GOSI record with the [GOSI calculator](/calculators/gosi) before you resign.

## Final word: check your contracts against Saudi labour law today

Nearly two years after the amendments took effect, many small businesses still use contract templates written for the old rules, and the 2026 penalty schedule makes that more expensive. Review contracts, policies and payroll together, and get advice where your situation is unusual.

### When did the new Saudi labour law amendments take effect?
The amendments took effect on 19 February 2025. They were published in the official gazette, Umm Al-Qura, on 23 August 2024 and came into force 180 days later. Some early reports gave 18 February 2025, but most legal sources use 19 February. The implementing regulations were updated separately.

### What is the probation period under Saudi labour law?
Probation can last up to 180 days in total. Before February 2025, the limit was 90 days, which could be extended to 180 only by written agreement. Either the employer or the employee may end the contract during the probation period. Employers do not have to use the full 180 days, and many keep a shorter period.

### What is the notice period in Saudi Arabia now?
On an indefinite contract, an employee who resigns gives 30 days' notice, down from 60. An employer ending an indefinite contract for a valid reason must still give 60 days. Fixed-term contracts end on their expiry date unless the contract says otherwise. An employer may postpone accepting a resignation by up to 60 days for work reasons.

### How long is maternity leave in Saudi Arabia?
Maternity leave is 12 weeks on full pay, up from 10 weeks before February 2025. Six of those weeks must be taken after the birth, and the leave can start up to four weeks before the expected delivery date. Failing to give statutory maternity leave is a listed violation in MHRSD's penalty schedule.

### Can an employer refuse my resignation in Saudi Arabia?
An employer cannot simply refuse a resignation, but under the amendments it can postpone accepting it for up to 60 days if the work requires it, giving written reasons. The amended law also confirms that an employee whose contract ends by resignation keeps the rights the law gives them, such as end-of-service benefits under the usual rules.

### What compensation is due for unfair termination?
Under Article 77, the parties may agree compensation in the contract. If they have not, the default is 15 days' wage for each year of service on an indefinite contract, or the wage for the remaining term on a fixed-term contract. In both cases, compensation cannot be less than two months' wage. It is separate from end-of-service benefits.

### Are contracts for expats fixed-term in Saudi Arabia?
Yes. Contracts for non-Saudi employees must be written and fixed-term. If a contract does not state its term, it is treated as one year from the actual start date and renews for the same period if work continues. MHRSD has reaffirmed that renewal does not turn a foreign employee's contract into an indefinite one.

### What are the fines for labour law violations in 2026?
MHRSD revised its schedule of violations and penalties in February 2026. Reported examples include SAR 10,000 per worker for employing a foreign worker without a work permit and SAR 3,000 per worker for keeping a passport or iqama. Repeat violations can be doubled. Check the official schedule on MHRSD's website for the current amounts.

### Did annual leave change under the amendments?
Annual leave was not among the headline changes. Employees are entitled to 21 days of paid annual leave a year, rising to 30 days after five years of continuous service with the same employer. Contracts can offer more. Unused leave due at the end of employment must be paid, and our calculators can help estimate it.

### Where can I read the official Saudi labour law text?
The official Arabic text of the Labour Law and its amendments is published on the Bureau of Experts at the Council of Ministers legal portal, laws.boe.gov.sa. MHRSD publishes guidance, the implementing regulations and the penalty schedule on hrsd.gov.sa. English translations from law firms are useful, but the Arabic text is the legal reference.
:::
