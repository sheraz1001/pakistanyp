**Expo 2030 Riyadh** will run from 1 October 2030 to 31 March 2031 on a site of about 6 million square metres in north Riyadh, with organisers aiming for around 42 million visits. Construction is under way, the first infrastructure contracts have been awarded, and the large building packages are now being tendered.

For small and medium businesses, the opportunity is not one contract. It is four years of demand flowing through main contractors, suppliers, hotels, caterers, transport firms and service providers, peaking in the six months the Expo is open. This explainer sets out where the project stands in late 2026 and how SMEs can position themselves.

:::takeaways
- Riyadh won the right to host the World Expo on 28 November 2023 at the 173rd General Assembly of the Bureau International des Expositions (BIE) in Paris.
- The Expo runs from 1 October 2030 to 31 March 2031, and organisers target around 42 million visits.
- Early works and two infrastructure packages were awarded between late 2025 and April 2026, all to Saudi contractors.
- Work on key buildings, including the Saudi Pavilion, was planned to start from the third quarter of 2026.
- Most SME work will come through tier-one contractors and their supply chains, so prequalification and paperwork matter now.
:::

## Expo 2030 Riyadh: the key facts

Riyadh beat Busan and Rome in the BIE vote in November 2023, taking 119 votes. The Expo 2030 Riyadh Company (ERC), set up to deliver the event, is responsible for the site, construction and operations.

:::stats The Expo at a glance
- 1 Oct 2030 | opening day (closes 31 March 2031)
- 42 million | target visits, according to the organisers
- 6 million m² | total site, with about 2 million m² inside the gate
- 6 months | length of the Expo
:::

The official theme is built around "foresight", presented in the bid as *The Era of Change: Together for a Foresighted Tomorrow*. The masterplan, designed by the architecture firm LAVA, was published in September 2025.

### Where the site is

The site is in north Riyadh, next to the planned King Salman International Airport and near KAPSARC, the King Abdullah Petroleum Studies and Research Centre. It is designed to be reached by road and by a new metro station, which the Royal Commission for Riyadh City has tendered. Our explainer on the [Riyadh Metro](/news/riyadh-metro-lines-fares-guide) covers the wider expansion plans, including 94 new trains due from 2028.

> [!NOTE]
> Participation figures are moving as countries sign contracts. The organisers' website refers to national pavilions for invited countries, while press tallies during 2026 counted between roughly 145 and 170 confirmed participants. Check https://www.expo2030riyadh.sa for the current number.

## Where construction stands in late 2026

The project has moved from planning to building. In April 2026 the Saudi delegation told the BIE in Paris that construction was well under way on site, and in June 2026 the BIE's 178th General Assembly approved the Siège Agreement, the legal framework for countries taking part.

:::steps Milestones so far
- 28 November 2023 | Riyadh wins the vote at the BIE General Assembly.
- September 2025 | LAVA's masterplan is published.
- November 2025 | Early works contract (demolition, earthworks, logistics) awarded to Binyah.
- Late 2025 to January 2026 | Main utilities and civil works package awarded to Nesma & Partners.
- April 2026 | Second infrastructure package (roads and utilities) awarded to Al Yamama Company.
- June 2026 | BIE approves the Siège Agreement.
- From Q3 2026 | Work planned to start on key buildings, including the Saudi Pavilion.
:::

These awards were reported by trade press such as MEED and AGBI; contract values were mostly not disclosed. Reports in July 2026 said the company was inviting bids for the "Expo Icon" structure and moving towards its first major building contracts, with nine tier-one contractors, local and international, reportedly invited to bid for the main vertical works.

> [!WARNING]
> Be wary of anyone offering to "register you as an Expo supplier" for a fee. Use only official channels from ERC and the procurement teams of the contractors who have actually won work.

## Where SMEs fit: the main Expo 2030 Riyadh opportunity areas

Very few small firms will contract directly with the Expo company. Most will win work as subcontractors or suppliers to the firms that do, or by serving the workforce, visitors and participating countries.

| Phase | Timing | Typical SME opportunities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Infrastructure and earthworks | 2025 to 2027 | Equipment rental, trucking, site security, worker accommodation, catering |
| Building and fit-out | 2026 to 2030 | MEP subcontracting, steel and metal works, finishing materials, interior fit-out |
| Pavilions for participating countries | 2028 to 2030 | Local design and build partners, translation, permits support, logistics |
| Operations | October 2030 to March 2031 | Food and beverage, cleaning, staffing, transport, hospitality, retail |
| Legacy and dismantling | 2031 onwards | Reuse, refurbishment, waste and recycling services |

*Timings are indicative, based on the published construction milestones.*

### Construction and the supply chain

The biggest early demand is for [contracting companies](/categories/contracting-companies), [MEP and HVAC contractors](/categories/mep-hvac-contractors), [building materials suppliers](/categories/building-materials) and [equipment rental firms](/categories/equipment-rental). Tier-one contractors need dependable local suppliers who can document quality, safety and capacity.

### Services for participating countries

Each country building or fitting out a pavilion needs local partners for logistics, permits, staffing and day-to-day supplies. [Translation and attestation offices](/categories/translation-attestation), [freight and shipping firms](/categories/freight-shipping) and [event management companies](/categories/event-management) are well placed here.

### Visitors and operations

When the gates open, demand moves to food, transport, accommodation and services. [Hotels and furnished units](/categories/hotels-furnished-units), [event catering and hospitality](/categories/event-catering-hospitality) firms and [cleaning services](/categories/cleaning-services) should plan staffing and capacity years ahead, not months.

## How to position your business for Expo work

Big contractors prequalify suppliers long before they place orders. The firms that get the calls will be the ones whose paperwork, capacity and track record are easy to check.

:::checklist
- A valid commercial registration (CR) covering the activities you offer
- VAT registration and Fatoora-compliant invoicing
- Up-to-date Nitaqat (Saudization) status and GOSI records
- Health, safety and quality documents relevant to your trade
- A short capability statement with past projects and photos
- Bank references and audited or reviewed accounts where available
- An up-to-date business profile with accurate contact details
:::

If you are not yet set up properly, our guide on [how to start a business in Saudi Arabia](/blogs/how-to-start-a-business-in-saudi-arabia) covers the CR and licences, and our explainer on [ZATCA e-invoicing](/news/zatca-e-invoicing-fatoora-waves) covers the invoicing rules that large buyers will expect you to meet.

> [!TIP]
> Approach the procurement teams of contractors that have already won Expo work, not just the Expo company. Ask how they register subcontractors and suppliers, what documents they need and when their next packages go to tender.

## Expo 2030 Riyadh in the wider events calendar

The Expo does not stand alone. Riyadh already hosts Riyadh Season every winter, and Saudi Arabia was confirmed by FIFA in December 2024 as host of the 2034 World Cup. The city is also preparing the Asian Games in 2034.

For a small business, that means a decade of large events rather than a single six-month peak. Investment in staff, equipment and certifications made for the Expo can keep paying off after March 2031, especially in hospitality, events, logistics and facilities management.

## Risks and realities for SMEs

The Expo is a long project, and smaller firms should plan for its pressures as well as its rewards.

- **Payment terms.** Subcontractors on large projects often wait longer to be paid. Make sure your cash flow can absorb it.
- **Competition for staff.** Skilled workers will be in demand across Riyadh's large projects, and wages may rise.
- **Changing schedules.** Timelines shift on projects of this size, so avoid committing all your capacity to one package.
- **Compliance.** Large buyers check Saudization, safety and tax compliance. Gaps can rule you out.

## Final word: Expo 2030 Riyadh rewards early preparation

The Expo opens in under four years, but the supply chains are forming now. If your business builds, supplies, transports, feeds or serves people, get your documents in order, approach the contractors that are winning work, and make it easy for them to find you.

### When is Expo 2030 Riyadh?
Expo 2030 Riyadh will run for six months, from 1 October 2030 to 31 March 2031. Riyadh won the right to host it on 28 November 2023 at the 173rd General Assembly of the Bureau International des Expositions in Paris. The dates have stayed consistent in official and press sources since the registration dossier was approved.

### Where is the Expo site?
The site is in north Riyadh, next to the planned King Salman International Airport and near the King Abdullah Petroleum Studies and Research Centre (KAPSARC). It covers about 6 million square metres, with roughly 2 million inside the gated Expo area. It is planned to be served by roads and a new Riyadh Metro station.

### How many visitors are expected?
The organisers aim for around 42 million visits over the six months. That figure is a target set in the bid and repeated by the organisers, not a forecast based on ticket sales. Visitor numbers will depend on international participation, travel demand and pricing closer to the event.

### How can small businesses get work at Expo 2030 Riyadh?
Most small firms will win work as suppliers or subcontractors to the main contractors, or by serving participating countries, workers and visitors. Get your CR, VAT, Saudization and safety documents in order, prepare a capability statement, and contact the procurement teams of contractors that have already won Expo packages to ask how they register suppliers.

### Who has won Expo contracts so far?
Trade press reported that Binyah won the early works package in November 2025, Nesma & Partners the main utilities and civil works package around the turn of 2026, and Al Yamama Company a second infrastructure package in April 2026. Bechtel was reported as project management consultant. Building contracts were being tendered during 2026.

### What is the theme of the Expo?
The theme centres on foresight. The bid presented it as "The Era of Change: Together for a Foresighted Tomorrow", and the organisers' website uses the shorter "Foresight for Tomorrow". The theme is meant to shape pavilion content and the wider programme, and more detail is published on the official Expo website.

### Is construction under way?
Yes. Early works and infrastructure contracts were awarded from late 2025, and in April 2026 the Saudi delegation told the BIE that construction was well under way on site. Work on key buildings, including the Saudi Pavilion, was planned to start from the third quarter of 2026, with larger building contracts being tendered.

### How many countries are taking part?
Numbers are still changing as countries sign participation contracts. Press tallies during 2026 ranged from about 145 to 170 confirmed participants, while the organisers refer to pavilions for all invited countries and international organisations. Check the official Expo website for the latest confirmed list.

### Will the Expo create jobs?
Yes, in construction during the build, and in hospitality, transport, retail, security and services during the six months of operation. Official job forecasts vary by source, so we have not quoted one. For employers, the practical point is to plan Saudization and staffing early, because demand for skilled workers in Riyadh will be high.

### How does the Expo connect to the World Cup?
The Expo closes in March 2031, and Saudi Arabia hosts the FIFA World Cup in 2034. Infrastructure built for one, such as metro capacity, hotels and roads, will support the other. For businesses, the two events create a long run of demand in Riyadh rather than a single short peak.
