GST registration is compulsory for most small businesses only once their annual turnover crosses ₹40 lakh for goods or ₹20 lakh for services, with lower limits in some special category states. It is required from day one, regardless of turnover, if you supply goods to another state or fall into a few other compulsory categories.

The application itself is free and online on the GST portal. Applicants who complete Aadhaar authentication can usually expect approval within a few working days, and a newer simplified route promises a decision within three working days for small, low-risk businesses.

:::takeaways
- Most states: register above ₹40 lakh turnover for goods, ₹20 lakh for services.
- Inter-state sellers of goods and several other categories must register whatever their turnover.
- The application is free on the official GST portal; Aadhaar authentication speeds it up.
- Small traders can choose the composition scheme and pay tax at a low flat rate.
- From 22 September 2025, most goods and services sit in the 5% or 18% slab.
:::

## Who needs GST registration in India

The test is your **aggregate turnover**: the total value of all taxable, exempt and export supplies made under the same PAN across India, excluding the GST itself. It is counted over a financial year.

| Business type | Most states | Special category states (lower limits) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supplier of goods only | Above ₹40 lakh | Above ₹20 lakh in states that kept the lower limit |
| Supplier of services, or goods and services | Above ₹20 lakh | Above ₹10 lakh |

Which states use the lower limits has changed since 2019, as several states chose different options for goods. Check the current position for your state on the GST portal or with a tax adviser before relying on a threshold.

### When registration is compulsory regardless of turnover

- You supply **goods from one state to another**. Small service providers have an exemption for inter-state services below the normal threshold.
- You are a **casual taxable person**, for example selling at a trade fair in another state.
- You must pay tax under **reverse charge**.
- You are an **e-commerce operator**, or must deduct or collect tax at source.
- You sell through e-commerce marketplaces in cases not covered by the relief for small intra-state sellers. Check your marketplace's onboarding rules.

> [!NOTE]
> The threshold is turnover, not profit. A trader with ₹45 lakh of sales and thin margins still crosses the ₹40 lakh line for goods.

## Should you register voluntarily?

Many businesses below the limit register anyway, for commercial rather than legal reasons.

**Benefits:** you can claim input tax credit on purchases, larger companies and government buyers prefer GST-registered suppliers, and you can sell inter-state and on most marketplaces. A GSTIN (the 15-character GST Identification Number) also counts as strong business proof for banks.

**Costs:** you must charge GST on every sale, raise proper tax invoices and file returns every period, even with no sales. If most of your customers are households who cannot claim credit, registering can make your prices less competitive.

If you sell mainly to other businesses, voluntary registration usually pays. If you run a small neighbourhood shop selling to walk-in customers, compare the numbers with your accountant first.

## Documents you need to register for GST

The portal asks for scanned copies in JPEG or PDF, within its size limits. Keep these ready before you start, because the application times out.

:::checklist
- PAN of the business (or of the proprietor)
- Aadhaar of the proprietor, partners, directors or authorised signatory
- Photograph of each promoter or partner
- Proof of constitution: partnership deed, LLP or company incorporation certificate
- Principal place of business proof: property tax receipt, electricity bill, or rent agreement with the owner's consent letter
- Bank details: cancelled cheque, passbook page or statement showing the account holder's name
- Letter of authorisation or board resolution for the authorised signatory
- Mobile number and email linked to the primary signatory
:::

> [!TIP]
> Make sure the name and address on your PAN, Aadhaar and bank account match exactly. Small differences, such as an initial spelled out on one document and not another, are a common reason for clarification notices.

## How to apply for GST registration online, step by step

The official application runs on the [GST portal](https://www.gst.gov.in) in Form GST REG-01, in two parts.

:::steps Applying on the GST portal
- Start Part A | Enter PAN, state, mobile number and email; verify with OTPs to get a Temporary Reference Number (TRN).
- Complete Part B | Log in with the TRN and fill in business, promoter, address, goods/services and bank details.
- Upload documents | Attach the files from the checklist above for each section.
- Opt for Aadhaar authentication | Choose it and complete the OTP or biometric check for each listed person.
- Verify and submit | Sign with DSC (compulsory for companies and LLPs) or EVC, and receive an ARN.
- Track and receive GSTIN | Respond to any query in Form REG-03 within seven working days; on approval, download the certificate (REG-06).
:::

### How long approval takes

Under the rules, an application with successful Aadhaar authentication is generally decided within a few working days. If authentication is not done, or the system flags the application as higher risk, the officer can order a physical verification of your premises and has up to 30 days.

Since 1 November 2025, a simplified registration scheme (Rule 14A of the CGST Rules) lets applicants opt in if their monthly output tax on supplies to registered businesses will not exceed ₹2.5 lakh. Approval is due within three working days of Aadhaar authentication. If your liability later goes above the limit, you must withdraw from the scheme.

:::stats Registering for GST in numbers
- ₹40 lakh | goods turnover threshold in most states
- ₹20 lakh | services threshold in most states
- 3 working days | simplified route after Aadhaar authentication
- 30 days | maximum when physical verification is ordered
:::

## Composition scheme: a lighter option for small traders

Once registered, a small business can opt for the composition scheme instead of normal GST. You pay tax at a low flat rate on turnover, file fewer returns and keep simpler books. In return, you cannot claim input tax credit, cannot collect GST from customers on your invoices, and generally cannot make inter-state sales.

:::bars Composition tax rates on turnover (%)
- Traders and manufacturers | 1 | 1%
- Restaurants (not serving alcohol) | 5 | 5%
- Eligible service providers | 6 | 6%
:::

The turnover limit is ₹1.5 crore for goods (₹75 lakh in special category states) and ₹50 lakh for service providers. Rates and eligibility can change, so confirm them on the portal before you opt in.

## What changed with the September 2025 GST rate cut

The GST Council's 56th meeting approved a simpler rate structure from 22 September 2025. The 12% and 28% slabs were folded into **5% for essentials and merit goods, 18% as the standard rate, and 40% for a small list of sin and luxury goods**, with some items moving to nil.

Recheck the HSN or SAC code and rate for every product or service before you print invoices. And if you buy stock across the changeover, the applicable rate depends on the time of supply, so ask your accountant about transitional invoices.

## After you register: your first compliance steps

- **Display your GSTIN** on your signboard and on every invoice.
- **Issue proper tax invoices** with your GSTIN, the customer's GSTIN for business sales, HSN or SAC codes and the tax split.
- **File returns on time.** Regular taxpayers file GSTR-1 for sales and GSTR-3B for tax payment. Businesses with turnover up to ₹5 crore can choose quarterly filing with monthly payment.
- **Collect digitally where possible.** UPI and bank receipts make reconciliation far easier; see our explainer on [UPI's newest features for shopkeepers](/news/upi-new-features-shopkeepers).

Missing returns builds up late fees, and long gaps can lead to your registration being suspended or cancelled. ## Final word: register when the numbers say so

GST registration is not something to fear, but it is a commitment. Register as soon as you are legally required to, or earlier if your buyers are businesses that want tax invoices. Keep documents consistent, opt for Aadhaar authentication and file every return on time.

:::faq
### What is the turnover limit for GST registration?
In most states, a supplier of goods must register once aggregate turnover crosses ₹40 lakh in a financial year, and a supplier of services at ₹20 lakh. Several special category states apply lower limits, such as ₹10 lakh for services. Aggregate turnover covers taxable, exempt and export supplies under the same PAN across India, excluding GST itself.

### Is there any fee to register for GST?
No. The government charges nothing to register on the official GST portal. You only pay if you hire a chartered accountant, tax practitioner or agent to file it for you, and those professional fees vary by city and firm. Be wary of websites that look official but charge a "government fee".

### How many days does GST registration take?
With complete documents and successful Aadhaar authentication, applications are typically decided within a few working days, and the simplified scheme from November 2025 promises a decision within three working days for eligible applicants. If Aadhaar authentication is skipped or the application is flagged, physical verification can be ordered and the process can take up to 30 days.

### Can I get a GSTIN without a shop or office?
Yes, you can register using your home address as the principal place of business, provided you upload proof such as an electricity bill or property tax receipt and, if the home is rented, a rent agreement with the owner's consent. Officers may verify the address, so the business should genuinely operate there or be reachable at that address.

### Do online sellers need to register for GST?
Selling goods to customers in other states requires registration whatever your turnover. Small sellers supplying goods only within their own state through e-commerce platforms received relief from compulsory registration in 2023, subject to conditions. Marketplace rules can be stricter than the law, so check your platform's seller requirements before you list products.

### What happens if I do not register for GST when required?
You can be asked to pay the tax you should have collected, plus interest, and penalties under the CGST Act, which can run to at least ₹10,000 or more depending on the tax involved. You also cannot claim input tax credit for the unregistered period. Register within 30 days of becoming liable to avoid these problems.

### Can a business have more than one GSTIN?
Yes. Registration is state-wise, so a business operating in several states needs a separate GSTIN in each state where it supplies from. Within one state, a business can also take separate registrations for different places of business if it chooses. All registrations under the same PAN count towards one aggregate turnover.

### What is a GSTIN?
A GSTIN is the 15-character GST Identification Number issued on registration. The first two digits are the state code, the next ten are the business PAN, followed by an entity number, a default letter and a check digit. You must show it on invoices and your signboard, and customers can verify it on the GST portal.

### Can I cancel my GSTIN if my business closes?
Yes. You can apply for cancellation on the GST portal in Form REG-16 if the business closes, is transferred or your turnover falls below the threshold and you registered voluntarily. You must file a final return and pay any tax due on stock held. Unfiled returns can hold up cancellation, so clear them first.

### Who should choose the composition scheme?
The composition scheme suits small traders, manufacturers and restaurants with turnover up to ₹1.5 crore (₹75 lakh in special category states) who sell mostly to consumers and do not need input tax credit. You pay a flat percentage of turnover and file fewer returns. It is a poor fit if you sell inter-state or mainly to GST-registered businesses.
:::
