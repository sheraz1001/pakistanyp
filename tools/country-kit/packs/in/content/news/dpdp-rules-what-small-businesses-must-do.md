The **DPDP Act rules**, formally the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025, were notified by the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) in November 2025, with the gazette notification dated 13 November 2025. They put into practice the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, and they apply to any business that collects personal data digitally, including small shops, clinics, coaching centres and service firms.

The main duties do not apply yet. Most obligations, including notice, consent, security safeguards and breach reporting, take effect 18 months after notification, which is **May 2027**. The registration of consent managers starts after 12 months, around **November 2026**. That gives small businesses a few months to prepare, and this explainer sets out what to do.

:::takeaways
- The DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025 and phase in over 18 months.
- Data Protection Board provisions applied immediately; consent manager registration follows after 12 months; core duties apply from May 2027.
- Any business that collects customer data digitally, such as phone numbers, addresses or health details, is a "data fiduciary" under the Act.
- Penalties can reach ₹250 crore for failing to protect data, though the Board must consider the nature and size of the business.
- A MeitY proposal in January 2026 to shorten some deadlines had not been notified at the time of the last reports; check MeitY for updates.
:::

## What the DPDP Act and Rules cover

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act received Presidential assent on 11 August 2023. It governs **digital personal data**: information about an identifiable person that is collected online, or collected offline and then digitised. A paper register you later type into a spreadsheet counts.

The Act uses its own terms, which are worth learning:

| Term | Meaning | Small business example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Data principal | The person the data is about | Your customer, patient or student |
| Data fiduciary | Whoever decides why and how data is used | Your shop, clinic or coaching centre |
| Data processor | A firm that processes data for you | Your billing software or WhatsApp marketing vendor |
| Consent manager | A registered platform that lets people give and withdraw consent | A future service, registered with the Board |
| Data Protection Board | The body that enforces the law | Hears complaints and imposes penalties |

## The DPDP Act rules timeline: what applies when

MeitY chose a phased rollout so that businesses have time to adjust. According to the notified Rules and MeitY's explanatory note:

:::steps DPDP Rules phase-in
- Notification | November 2025: Rules published; Board provisions apply at once.
- Board set up | From November 2025: the Data Protection Board is established as a digital body.
- Consent managers | Around November 2026: registration of consent managers begins.
- Core duties | Around May 2027: notice, consent, security, breach reporting, retention and rights apply.
:::

> [!NOTE]
> In January 2026, MeitY proposed shortening the 18-month window to 12 months for some obligations, and consultation closed on 4 February 2026. Legal commentators said in September 2026 that no amending notification had been issued. Check https://www.meity.gov.in for the current position before planning around May 2027.

The Data Protection Board is set up as a fully digital body. MeitY invited nominations for its chairperson and members in May 2026; reports in August 2026 said appointments had not yet been announced.

:::stats DPDP penalties under the Act (maximum)
- ₹250 crore | failure to take reasonable security safeguards
- ₹200 crore | failure to report a breach, or breach of children's data duties
- ₹50 crore | other breaches of the Act or Rules
- ₹10,000 | breach of duties by an individual, such as a false complaint
:::

## What small businesses must do under the Rules

The Act does not exempt small businesses as a class, though it allows the government to exempt notified categories, including startups, from some provisions. Unless a notification covers you, plan to comply. The core duties, from May 2027, are:

### Give a clear notice and take consent

Before collecting personal data, give a notice in clear, plain language that says what you collect, why, and how the person can withdraw consent or complain. Consent must be free, specific and given by a clear action, such as ticking a box. Notices can be in English or any language in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution.

### Keep data secure

Use reasonable safeguards: access controls, encryption or masking where possible, backups and logs. The Rules require logs of access to personal data to be kept, generally for at least one year.

### Report breaches

If personal data is breached, inform affected people without delay and notify the Data Protection Board, with a detailed report within 72 hours, as the Rules set out.

### Respect people's rights

People can ask to see, correct or erase their data and can nominate someone to act for them. Publish how they can contact you, and respond to grievances within the period the Rules set (up to 90 days).

### Take care with children's data

For anyone under 18, you need verifiable consent from a parent or guardian. This matters for [coaching classes](/categories/coaching-classes), schools and tuition centres.

## What this means for common local businesses

Most small businesses already hold more personal data than they realise: phone numbers in WhatsApp, addresses for delivery, health details in a clinic file. Some examples:

- **[Clinics and doctors](/categories/doctors-clinics):** patient records are sensitive; limit who can open them and keep backups secure.
- **Home service firms:** if you collect addresses and phone numbers for bookings, delete them when they are no longer needed. Our guide to [finding reliable home services](/blogs/how-to-find-reliable-home-services-india) shows how customers judge trust.
- **Shops taking UPI or delivery orders:** payment data is handled by your bank, but your own customer lists are your responsibility. See our explainer on [UPI's new rules for shopkeepers](/news/upi-new-features-shopkeepers).
- **Solar and installation firms:** subsidy paperwork such as [PM Surya Ghar](/news/pm-surya-ghar-rooftop-solar-progress) applications includes bank and identity details; store them carefully.

> [!WARNING]
> Sending marketing messages to customers who never agreed to them will be a risk once consent rules apply. If you run WhatsApp or SMS campaigns, start keeping a record of who opted in and when.

## A practical DPDP checklist for small businesses

You do not need expensive software to begin. Start with this list over the next few months.

:::checklist
- List what personal data you collect, where it is stored and who can access it
- Delete data you no longer need, such as old enquiry lists
- Write a short privacy notice in plain language and show it where you collect data
- Add a clear consent step (tick box or signed form) to booking and enquiry forms
- Ask your software, marketing and delivery vendors how they protect your customers' data
- Set strong passwords and two-step login on email, billing and cloud accounts
- Name one person to handle privacy requests and breach reports
- Keep a simple record of consents, requests and incidents
:::

### What are the DPDP Rules 2025?
They are the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025, notified by MeitY in November 2025 with a gazette date of 13 November 2025. They set out how the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 works in practice, covering notices, consent, security, breach reporting, children's data, consent managers and the Data Protection Board, with obligations phased in over 18 months.

### When do the DPDP Act rules come into force?
In three stages. Provisions on the Data Protection Board applied immediately in November 2025. Registration of consent managers starts 12 months later, around November 2026. The core obligations, including notice, consent, security safeguards and breach reporting, apply 18 months after notification, around May 2027, unless MeitY notifies a change.

### Does the DPDP Act apply to small businesses?
Yes. The Act applies to any person or business that processes digital personal data, regardless of size, unless the government exempts a class by notification. A shop that keeps customer phone numbers in a spreadsheet, a clinic with digital patient files or a coaching centre with student records is a data fiduciary and should prepare for the core duties.

### What is the penalty under the DPDP Act?
The Act sets maximum penalties of up to ₹250 crore for failing to take reasonable security safeguards, up to ₹200 crore for failing to report a breach or meet duties on children's data, and up to ₹50 crore for other breaches. The Board must consider factors such as the nature, gravity and duration of the breach when fixing the amount.

### What is a data fiduciary?
A data fiduciary is any person or organisation that decides why and how personal data is processed. For a small business, that is usually the business itself. A data processor, such as a billing software company or marketing vendor, processes data on the fiduciary's behalf, but the fiduciary remains responsible for compliance under the Act.

### How quickly must a data breach be reported?
Under the Rules, a data fiduciary must inform affected individuals without delay and notify the Data Protection Board, followed by a detailed report within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach, unless the Board allows more time. These duties apply once the core obligations take effect, which is expected around May 2027.

### Do I need consent to send WhatsApp offers to customers?
Once the core duties apply, processing personal data for marketing will generally need the person's consent or another lawful ground under the Act. Consent must be free, specific and given by a clear action. Keep a record of who opted in and when, and make it easy for customers to stop receiving messages.

### What is a consent manager under the DPDP Rules?
A consent manager is a platform registered with the Data Protection Board that lets people give, manage, review and withdraw consent across different businesses. Registration of consent managers begins around November 2026, 12 months after the Rules were notified. Small businesses are not required to become consent managers.

### Has the Data Protection Board been formed?
MeitY has established the Board as a digital body under the Act and Rules, which provide for a chairperson and members. MeitY invited nominations in May 2026, and reports in August 2026 said appointments had not been announced. Check MeitY's website or recent PIB releases for the current status of appointments.

### What should a small business do first to prepare?
Start by listing the personal data you collect, where it is stored and who can access it. Delete what you no longer need, write a plain-language privacy notice, add a consent step to forms and secure your accounts with strong passwords. Ask your software and marketing vendors how they protect customer data and keep records.
