The **PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana**, the Centre's rooftop solar scheme for homes, had reached more than **50 lakh households** by early August 2026, according to the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE). The scheme pays a subsidy of up to ₹78,000 for a home rooftop system and aims to cover one crore households by the end of financial year 2026-27, which is March 2027.

For a household, the answer is straightforward: you apply on the national portal, choose a vendor registered with your electricity distribution company (DISCOM), get the system installed and net-metered, and the subsidy is paid into your bank account. For local installers and electricians, the scheme has created steady demand across the country.

:::takeaways
- The scheme was launched on 13 February 2024 and approved by the Union Cabinet on 29 February 2024 with an outlay of ₹75,021 crore.
- MNRE reported more than 50 lakh households benefiting and about 14.8 GW of rooftop capacity by early August 2026.
- The subsidy is ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW, capped at ₹78,000.
- Households must use a vendor registered with their DISCOM and apply through pmsuryaghar.gov.in.
- The target is one crore households by March 2027; later figures from the scheme's own channels suggest strong momentum, but check official releases.
:::

## What the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana is

Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced the scheme on 13 February 2024, and the Union Cabinet approved it on 29 February 2024. It aims to help households install rooftop solar and receive up to 300 units of free electricity a month, with surplus power exported to the grid through net metering.

MNRE runs the scheme with state DISCOMs, which approve applications, register vendors and install net meters. Applications, vendor lists and subsidy status are handled on the national portal at https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in.

## Progress in 2026: where the scheme stands

The scheme has accelerated sharply since 2025. Official milestones, as released through the Press Information Bureau (PIB):

- **22 July 2026:** 39.72 lakh rooftop systems installed, benefiting 48.03 lakh households, according to a PIB release. Some installations, such as those in housing societies or through aggregated models, serve more than one household.
- **July 2026:** MNRE said it was the highest month yet, with about 5.06 lakh households added.
- **Early August 2026:** a PIB release reported more than **50.06 lakh households** benefiting, nearly **19 lakh** households receiving zero electricity bills and about **14.8 GW** of rooftop capacity commissioned.

In October 2026, the scheme's official social media account said beneficiary households had crossed 61 lakh. That figure had not appeared in a PIB release at the time of writing, so treat it as provisional and check pib.gov.in for the latest official count.

> Reaching one crore households by March 2027 would need the recent monthly pace to continue or rise. MNRE has not announced any change to the target; check its releases for updates.

## How the subsidy works for homes

The central financial assistance (CFA) depends on system size. As set out by MNRE:

| System size | Central subsidy | Typical suitable for (indicative) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 kW | ₹30,000 | Low consumption, up to about 150 units a month |
| 2 kW | ₹60,000 | Moderate consumption, about 150–300 units a month |
| 3 kW and above | ₹78,000 (maximum) | Higher consumption, above about 300 units a month |
| Housing societies and RWAs (common facilities) | ₹18,000 per kW | Shared loads such as lifts and lighting, within scheme limits |

:::bars Central subsidy by system size (₹)
- 1 kW | 30000 | ₹30,000
- 2 kW | 60000 | ₹60,000
- 3 kW or more | 78000 | ₹78,000 (cap)
:::

Some states add their own subsidy on top, and the scheme also links households to collateral-free bank loans for small systems. Our [guide to the rooftop solar subsidy and choosing a vendor](/blogs/rooftop-solar-subsidy-india-guide) explains costs and loan options in more detail.

### How to apply

:::steps Applying for PM Surya Ghar
- Register | Sign up on pmsuryaghar.gov.in with your state, DISCOM and consumer number.
- Apply | Submit the rooftop solar application and wait for DISCOM feasibility approval.
- Choose a vendor | Pick an installer registered with your DISCOM and agree the price.
- Install | The vendor installs the system and you submit details on the portal.
- Net metering | The DISCOM installs a net meter and inspects the system.
- Get the subsidy | After commissioning, enter bank details; the subsidy is credited to your account.
:::

## What the scheme means for local solar installers

The scheme has turned rooftop solar into a mass-market service. Demand reaches well beyond the big solar companies to local [solar energy businesses](/categories/solar-energy), [electrical contractors](/categories/electrical-works) and firms selling [power backup and inverters](/categories/power-backup).

To take part, a vendor must be empanelled or registered with the DISCOM in each state where it works, follow the scheme's technical standards (including the use of domestically manufactured solar modules) and upload installation details on the portal. Payment terms are agreed with the household, which then receives the subsidy directly.

### Where demand is strongest

Uptake has been strongest in states with high power bills, strong DISCOM processes and active vendor networks. States such as [Gujarat](/gujarat), [Maharashtra](/maharashtra) and [Uttar Pradesh](/uttar-pradesh) have featured prominently in official progress updates; check the state-wise data on the national portal for current rankings.

> [!TIP]
> For installers: the fastest-growing businesses keep a simple file for each customer, including DISCOM approval, module serial numbers, inverter details and photos of the finished site. Inspections and subsidy release go faster when the paperwork is complete.

### The aggregated model for low-income households

MNRE has also tested a utility-led aggregation model, where DISCOMs or state agencies install systems for groups of eligible low-income households, such as those under PMAY or below the poverty line. Official updates reported in 2026 put the figure at about 1.6 lakh such installations across four states, with the model sanctioned in 12 states. This opens tender-based work for installers.

## Checks before a household signs up

The subsidy does not remove the need to choose carefully. Before you pay a vendor, run through this list.

:::checklist
- The vendor appears on your DISCOM's registered list on the national portal
- The quote states system size, module and inverter brands, and warranty terms in writing
- Modules meet the scheme's domestic content requirement
- The price includes structure, wiring, earthing and net-metering paperwork
- You know who handles maintenance and for how long
- Your roof has enough shade-free space and can bear the load
- You have a cancelled cheque or bank details ready for the subsidy credit
:::

Solar equipment also became cheaper in 2025, when GST on renewable energy devices was cut from 12% to 5% under the [new GST rates](/news/gst-rate-changes-two-slab-explained). Ask your vendor to show GST separately on the invoice.

### What is PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana?
It is the Central Government's rooftop solar scheme for households, launched on 13 February 2024 and approved by the Union Cabinet on 29 February 2024 with an outlay of ₹75,021 crore. It gives a subsidy for home rooftop solar systems and aims to help one crore households get up to 300 units of free electricity a month.

### How much subsidy do I get under the scheme?
The central subsidy is ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW, so a 1 kW system gets ₹30,000, 2 kW gets ₹60,000 and 3 kW or more gets the maximum of ₹78,000. Housing societies get support for common facilities. Some states offer an extra subsidy on top.

### How many households have benefited so far?
A PIB release in early August 2026 reported more than 50.06 lakh households benefiting, about 14.8 GW of rooftop capacity and nearly 19 lakh households with zero electricity bills. In October 2026 the scheme's official social media account said the total had crossed 61 lakh households. Check pib.gov.in for the latest official figure.

### How do I apply for rooftop solar subsidy?
Register on pmsuryaghar.gov.in with your state, DISCOM and electricity consumer number, then submit the rooftop solar application. After the DISCOM approves feasibility, choose a registered vendor, get the system installed and apply for net metering. Once the DISCOM inspects and commissions the system, enter your bank details to receive the subsidy.

### Can I choose any solar installer?
No. To receive the subsidy, the system must be installed by a vendor registered with your DISCOM under the scheme. Vendor lists are shown on the national portal. You can compare several registered vendors on price, equipment brands and service, and you should get the final quote and warranty terms in writing before work starts.

### How long does it take to get the subsidy?
It depends on your DISCOM and how quickly inspection and net metering are completed. The subsidy is released after the system is commissioned and your bank details are verified on the portal. Delays often come from incomplete paperwork, so keep invoices, photos and approval documents ready. Track the status on the portal.

### Is a loan available under the scheme?
Yes. The scheme links households with banks offering collateral-free loans for small rooftop systems, typically up to 3 kW. Interest rates and terms are set by the lending bank within the scheme's guidelines. Apply through the portal or ask your bank, and compare the loan cost against your expected savings on electricity bills.

### What is the target of PM Surya Ghar?
The target is to cover one crore households with rooftop solar by the end of financial year 2026-27, which is March 2027. By early August 2026, official figures showed more than 50 lakh households covered. Meeting the target depends on the pace of installations continuing to rise in the remaining months.

### How can a solar business register as a vendor?
Vendors register or are empanelled with the DISCOM of each state where they plan to work, through the process listed on the national portal. They must follow the scheme's technical standards, including domestic content rules for modules, and upload installation details. Check your state DISCOM's requirements, as documents and fees can differ.

### Who is eligible for the rooftop solar scheme?
Indian households with their own house, a roof suitable for solar panels and a valid electricity connection can apply. A household should not have received another subsidy for solar panels earlier. Eligibility details and FAQs are listed on pmsuryaghar.gov.in. Tenants usually need the owner's consent, since the system is installed on the property.
