Lakshadweep is a union territory made up of coral islands in the Arabian Sea, off the coast of Kerala. Only ten of its islands are inhabited: Kavaratti, Agatti, Amini, Kadmat, Kiltan, Chetlat, Bitra, Andrott, Kalpeni and Minicoy. Kavaratti is the capital and the administrative centre, while Agatti has the territory's only airport.

With a land area of only about 32 km² and a population of about 64,000 at the last census, Lakshadweep has a small, island-based economy. Fishing and coconut cultivation are the main occupations, and tourism is growing under controlled conditions. Most goods are shipped in from the mainland, chiefly through Kochi.

## Key facts
- **Status:** Union territory
- **Capital:** Kavaratti
- **Population (2011 census, union territory):** 64,473
- **Land area:** about 32 km²
- **Inhabited islands:** 10
- **Airport:** Agatti Airport, on Agatti Island (the only airport in the territory)
- **Main mainland gateway:** Kochi, Kerala
- **Main sectors:** fishing (especially tuna), coconut and coir, tourism, government services

## Economy and main industries
Fishing and coconut cultivation are the main sources of livelihood. Tuna makes up the largest part of the fish catch and is the territory's main export item. Coconut is grown across the inhabited islands, and coir production, including fibre and yarn made in government units, is a significant local industry, with coir units operating on Kavaratti.

Tourism is a growing source of income, but access is regulated and visitor numbers are limited, so tourism businesses tend to be small: resorts, guesthouses, diving and water-sports operators, and local transport and guiding. Government departments and public services account for a large share of formal employment.

## Main business areas and markets
Kavaratti is the main administrative and commercial island, with government offices, shops, banks and service providers. Agatti, as the airport island, has accommodation and transport services for arriving visitors. Other inhabited islands, including Minicoy, Andrott, Kalpeni, Amini and Kadmat, have their own small markets, fishing communities and, in some cases, tourist accommodation.

Many suppliers serving the islands are based on the mainland, particularly in Kochi, where the administration also has an office that handles visitor permits.

## Getting there and permits
Entry to Lakshadweep is restricted, and visitors need a permit issued by the Lakshadweep Administration, whose office handling permits is in Kochi. Processing times reported by travel sources vary from a few working days to several weeks, and in recent years the rules have been relaxed for tourists. Entry is granted only to the islands named on the permit. Anyone planning business travel should check current requirements with the administration's official portal.

Access is by air to Agatti, from where visitors continue to other islands by boat, or by passenger ship from Kochi, with sailing times of roughly 14 to 18 hours. Ships on the route have included the MV Kavaratti, MV Arabian Sea, MV Lakshadweep Sea, MV Corals and MV Amindivi.

## Finding businesses in Lakshadweep
People looking for businesses in Lakshadweep usually search for resorts and guesthouses, tour and diving operators, local transport, and suppliers of goods and building materials, many of whom work from Kochi. Coir and coconut product sellers and fish traders are also of interest. IndiaYP lists businesses in Kavaratti.

## Frequently asked questions
### Do I need a permit to visit Lakshadweep?
Yes. Visitors need an entry permit from the Lakshadweep Administration, valid only for the islands named on it. Check the administration's official portal for current rules.

### Where is the airport in Lakshadweep?
The only airport is on Agatti Island. Visitors travel on to other islands by boat.

### How do goods and passengers reach Lakshadweep?
Mainly by ship from Kochi, a voyage of about 14 to 18 hours, or by air to Agatti.

### What are Lakshadweep's main industries?
Fishing, particularly tuna, and coconut cultivation with related coir production, along with small-scale tourism.
