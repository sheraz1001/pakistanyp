Assam lies in the Brahmaputra and Barak valleys and is the gateway state to north-east India. Most road and rail routes into the region pass through it, and Guwahati, its largest city, acts as the distribution, banking and transport base for the neighbouring hill states as well as for Assam itself. The state capital, Dispur, is within the Guwahati urban area.

Two industries give Assam its national profile: tea and petroleum. The state grows roughly half of India's tea, and its four oil refineries include the oldest in the country. Around these sit a large trading economy, a growing warehousing sector on the north bank of the Brahmaputra and the usual services of a populous state.

## Key facts
- **Status:** State of India
- **Capital:** Dispur, within Guwahati
- **Population (2011 census, state):** 31,205,576
- **Largest city:** Guwahati
- **Main airport:** Lokpriya Gopinath Bordoloi International Airport, about 22 km from central Guwahati, managed by the Adani Group since October 2021
- **Tea:** about half of India's output; Assam produced 402.57 million kg of the country's 811.56 million kg between January and August 2025
- **Refineries:** Digboi, Guwahati (Noonmati), Bongaigaon and Numaligarh
- **Tea auction:** Guwahati Tea Auction Centre, opened on 25 September 1970

## Economy and main industries
Tea is the best-known industry and a major employer. Large estates and many small growers supply leaf to factories that produce CTC and orthodox teas. Much of the crop is sold through the Guwahati Tea Auction Centre, which has been described as one of the largest CTC auction centres in the world. It sold 204 million kg in 2019–20. Tea creates demand for brokers, warehousing, packaging, transport, fertilisers and machinery repair.

Oil and gas is the other main industry. Digboi refinery in Tinsukia district was commissioned in 1901 and is India's oldest. Indian Oil runs Digboi, and there are further refineries at Noonmati in Guwahati and at Bongaigaon. Numaligarh refinery in Golaghat district is being expanded from 3 to 9 million tonnes a year. Upstream oil and gas production and the refineries support engineering contractors, pipeline services, chemical suppliers and transport firms.

Outside these two industries, most rural households depend on farming, and the larger towns have the trade and services found in any populous state.

## Main business areas and markets
Guwahati holds most of the state's wholesale and service businesses. Fancy Bazar, a market more than a century old, is the main wholesale area for textiles, groceries, hardware and consumer goods. Traffic congestion has led the state government to plan moving the wholesale market to North Guwahati with a new logistics park. Traders should check the progress of that move before signing new premises.

North Guwahati and Amingaon on the north bank have become warehousing and distribution locations. Several Guwahati businesses have already shifted there because of congestion in the old city, and Amingaon has an Export Promotion Industrial Park. Bamunimaidam on NH-37 in Kamrup Metropolitan district has a long-established industrial estate. In Kamrup district, the Industrial Growth Centre at Chaygaon-Jambari offers serviced plots for manufacturers. Brahmaputra Industrial Park, also near Guwahati, is a private park for manufacturing and warehousing.

Outside Guwahati, the refinery towns of Digboi, Numaligarh and Bongaigaon support industrial suppliers and contractors. Silchar is the main town of the Barak valley in the south, while Dibrugarh, Jorhat and Tezpur are the main regional towns of the Brahmaputra valley.

## Tea trade and the auction system
For buyers, the tea trade is the most distinctive part of doing business in Assam. Estates and bought-leaf factories send their tea to brokers, who catalogue and sell it at the Guwahati auction. Packers, exporters and blenders buy there or directly from estates. Premium lots can fetch high prices, and record prices for Assam CTC tea at Guwahati auctions are reported most seasons. Firms entering the trade usually work through a broker and need warehouse arrangements. Service providers around the auction centre include tasters, logistics firms, packaging suppliers and testing laboratories.

## Finding businesses in Assam
People most often search for wholesalers and distributors in Guwahati, transport and logistics firms serving the north-east, tea brokers and packers, and contractors and engineering firms for the oil sector. Hospitals, colleges and hotels in Guwahati also draw visitors from the neighbouring states. IndiaYP lists businesses across Guwahati, Silchar, Dibrugarh, Jorhat and Tezpur.

## Frequently asked questions
### What is the difference between Dispur and Guwahati?
Dispur is the capital of Assam and houses the state secretariat. It lies inside the Guwahati urban area, so most visitors treat it as part of the city.

### Where are the main wholesale markets in Guwahati?
Fancy Bazar is the traditional wholesale centre. Warehousing and some wholesale trade are moving to Amingaon and North Guwahati.

### Where is tea from Assam sold?
Much of it is sold through brokers at the Guwahati Tea Auction Centre, which has run since 1970. Some estates also sell directly to packers and exporters.

### Which refineries operate in Assam?
There are four: Digboi, Guwahati (Noonmati), Bongaigaon and Numaligarh.
