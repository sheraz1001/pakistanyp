Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu is a union territory in western India, formed on 26 January 2020 by merging two former union territories, Dadra and Nagar Haveli, and Daman and Diu, under legislation passed in December 2019. It consists of three separate areas: Dadra and Nagar Haveli, inland between Gujarat and Maharashtra; Daman, on the coast of southern Gujarat; and Diu, an island off the Saurashtra coast of Gujarat. Daman is the capital of the combined territory.

For businesses, the territory is above all a manufacturing location. Silvassa, the headquarters of the Dadra and Nagar Haveli district and the territory's largest town, and Daman both have large numbers of factories, while Diu is smaller and more oriented to visitors.

## Key facts
- **Status:** Union territory (since 26 January 2020)
- **Capital:** Daman
- **Largest town:** Silvassa
- **Population (2011 census, combined territory):** 585,764
- **Population (2011 census, Dadra and Nagar Haveli):** 342,853
- **Area:** about 603 km²
- **Districts:** 3 – Dadra and Nagar Haveli, Daman and Diu
- **Telephone area code (Silvassa):** 0260
- **Notable industries:** textiles, plastics, engineering, pharmaceuticals, paper, packaging, brewing

## Economy and main industries
Industry dominates the territory's economy. Silvassa and the surrounding Dadra and Nagar Haveli district host many textile, plastics, engineering and pharmaceutical units, along with paper mills. Daman is known for light engineering, packaging and brewing.

This concentration of factories in a small area creates steady demand for industrial suppliers, machinery repair, transport and logistics, packaging materials, chemicals, contract labour, and accounting and compliance services. Many of these suppliers are based in the territory itself, while others work from nearby industrial towns in southern Gujarat and northern Maharashtra.

## Main business areas and markets
Silvassa is the territory's main commercial and industrial town. Industrial units are spread across the surrounding villages and estates of Dadra and Nagar Haveli. Shops, hotels, banks and service providers in Silvassa cater to both residents and visiting buyers.

Daman, the capital, combines the territory's administrative offices with light industry, and also has hotels, restaurants and shops serving visitors.

Diu, the most distant part of the territory, has a smaller economy in which hotels, guesthouses, restaurants and shops serve residents and visitors.

## Working across three separate areas
Because Dadra and Nagar Haveli, Daman and Diu are not contiguous, businesses usually deal with them separately in practice. Silvassa and Daman are relatively close to each other and to the Mumbai–Ahmedabad corridor, while Diu is reached through Gujarat's Saurashtra region. Since the merger, registrations and approvals are handled by a single union territory administration with its capital at Daman, which simplifies compliance for firms operating in more than one part of the territory.
