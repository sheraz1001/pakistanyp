Odisha lies on India's eastern coast along the Bay of Bengal, between West Bengal and Andhra Pradesh. Its capital is Bhubaneswar, which sits a short drive from the older commercial city of Cuttack, and together the two form the state's main urban and services area. Inland, the state's northern and western districts hold large mineral reserves that have shaped its industrial growth.

For businesses, Odisha matters mainly as a base for metals and mining. The state has a large share of India's bauxite reserves and a long-established steel industry, and its ports handle much of the raw material and finished metal moving through eastern India. Around this core sits a wide network of contractors, logistics firms, engineering suppliers and professional services.

## Economy and main industries
Metals dominate Odisha's industrial profile. The state holds roughly 59 per cent of India's bauxite reserves, mostly in the Eastern Ghats, and this has made it a centre for alumina and aluminium production. Angul hosts aluminium parks linked to NALCO and Vedanta that are meant to encourage downstream manufacturers, while Jharsuguda and Sambalpur are known for alumina and power generation.

Steel is the other pillar. The Rourkela Steel Plant in Sundargarh district, run by SAIL, was India's first public-sector integrated steel plant, and SAIL has announced plans to expand its capacity substantially. Kalinganagar in Jajpur district hosts stainless and other steel plants, and new integrated steel projects are being built around Paradip. The state government also lists power generation, petrochemicals, textiles and food processing among its growing sectors.

This heavy-industry base creates steady demand for mining equipment, industrial maintenance, transport, warehousing, construction materials and engineering services across the mineral belt.

## Main business areas and markets
Bhubaneswar is the state's administrative and services centre. Chandaka Industrial Estate, developed by IDCO (the Odisha Industrial Infrastructure Development Corporation), includes Infocity, the state's main IT park, near Patia. Mancheswar Industrial Estate and Khurda Industrial Estate are other established industrial areas in and around the city.

Cuttack, about 20 km from Bhubaneswar along NH-16, is the older of the two cities and remains an important trading centre. Rourkela is the main steel town in the north-west, with ancillary engineering units built up around the plant. Sambalpur serves as a commercial centre for western Odisha, while Berhampur is the main city of the south and Puri is primarily a pilgrimage and tourism town.

## Ports and logistics
Paradip Port is the state's main cargo gateway and handled 156.45 million tonnes in 2025–26, its highest throughput so far. It is chiefly a bulk-cargo port serving the state's mineral and metal industries, and new steel plants are being built close to the port to take advantage of it. NH-16 runs along the coast, linking Bhubaneswar and Cuttack with Kolkata to the north and Chennai to the south, and the state's rail network carries ore from the interior mining districts to the coast.

For companies serving the metals sector, the corridor between the mining districts, the processing centres at Angul and Jharsuguda, and the port at Paradip is where much of the freight, warehousing and contracting work is concentrated.

## Frequently asked questions
### What is the telephone area code for Bhubaneswar?
The STD code for Bhubaneswar is 0674. Mobile numbers do not need an area code.

### Where are Odisha's main industrial estates?
Around Bhubaneswar, the main estates are Chandaka (including the Infocity IT park), Mancheswar and Khurda. Heavier industry is concentrated at Rourkela, Angul, Jharsuguda, Kalinganagar and around Paradip.

### Which agency handles industrial land in Odisha?
IDCO, the Odisha Industrial Infrastructure Development Corporation, develops and manages the state's industrial estates and allots land to units.

### What is Odisha's main port?
Paradip Port is the main cargo port and handled 156.45 million tonnes in 2025–26.
