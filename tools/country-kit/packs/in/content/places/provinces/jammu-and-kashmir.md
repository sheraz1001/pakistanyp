Jammu and Kashmir is a union territory in the north of India. It was formed on 31 October 2019 under the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019, which reorganised the former state into two union territories, Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh. It has two divisions, the Kashmir Division centred on Srinagar and the Jammu Division centred on Jammu, which serve as the summer and winter capitals respectively.

The territory's economy rests on horticulture, handicrafts and tourism, alongside government services and a smaller industrial base in planned estates. The Kashmir Valley is the main apple-growing region of India and the source of Kashmir saffron, while Jammu is the main commercial gateway by road and rail.

## Key facts
- **Status:** Union territory (since 31 October 2019)
- **Capitals:** Srinagar (summer) and Jammu (winter)
- **Population (2011 census, districts now in the union territory):** 12,267,013
- **Districts:** 20, in the Jammu and Kashmir divisions
- **Telephone area codes:** Srinagar 0194; Jammu 0191
- **Notable sectors:** apples and other horticulture, saffron, pashmina and other handicrafts, tourism, food processing and cold storage

## Economy and main industries
Horticulture is the backbone of the rural economy. Kashmir produces more than 70 per cent of India's apples by most estimates, and the government reported apple production of over 21 lakh tonnes in 2024–25, almost all of it from the Kashmir Division. The harvest supports a large trade in packaging, transport, cold storage and wholesale marketing, and trade bodies regularly press for smooth movement of fruit trucks on the national highway during the season.

Saffron is grown mainly around Pampore, near Srinagar. Kashmir saffron received a Geographical Indication in 2020, which growers and officials expected to curb adulteration and improve prices. Handicrafts are another major source of livelihoods, and Kashmir Pashmina is among the territory's GI-registered products.

Tourism, centred on the Kashmir Valley and on pilgrimage sites in the Jammu region, supports hotels, houseboats, travel agents, transport operators and craft retailers.

## Main business areas and markets
Srinagar is the commercial centre of the Kashmir Division, with most of its wholesale trade, retail, hotels and professional services. Its industrial estate at Rangreth, developed by SIDCO, hosts small manufacturing units. South of the city, the Industrial Growth Centre at Lassipora in Pulwama district and the industrial estate at Aglar in Shopian district are the main centres for controlled-atmosphere cold storage for fruit.

Jammu is the commercial centre of the Jammu Division and the main entry point for goods coming by road and rail from the rest of India. The SIDCO Industrial Complex at Bari Brahmana, near Jammu, is one of the territory's main manufacturing locations. Anantnag and Baramulla are important district towns in the Kashmir Valley, serving the surrounding horticultural areas.

## Trade bodies and industry support
Business interests in the Kashmir Division are represented by the Kashmir Chamber of Commerce and Industry in Srinagar, while the Federation Chamber of Industries Kashmir speaks for industrial units. In Jammu, the Chamber of Commerce and Industry Jammu represents traders and manufacturers. The government has said it is working with the Industries and Commerce Department to set up sector-specific industrial estates for horticulture and post-harvest management in more districts.

## Finding businesses in Jammu and Kashmir
People looking for businesses in Jammu and Kashmir often search for apple and dry-fruit traders, saffron sellers, pashmina and handicraft makers, cold storage operators, and hotels and travel agents in Srinagar. In Jammu, wholesale suppliers, transporters and manufacturers at Bari Brahmana are common searches. IndiaYP lists businesses in Srinagar, Jammu, Anantnag and Baramulla.
