Chennai is the capital of Tamil Nadu. At the 2011 census the city, within its pre-expansion limits of 174 square kilometres, had 4,646,732 residents. The corporation was later expanded to 426 square kilometres, and the state government put the population of the enlarged area at 6,748,026. The urban agglomeration counted 8,696,010.

Chennai has a long industrial history and a strong manufacturing base. Small-industry estates set up in the 1950s and 1960s are still active, the belt to the south-west towards Sriperumbudur and Oragadam is dominated by automobile and electronics plants, and George Town in the north remains the heart of wholesale trade.

## Economy and main industries
Manufacturing is central to Chennai's economy. The corridor from Sriperumbudur to Oragadam, south-west of the city, hosts car makers, auto component suppliers and electronics manufacturers, along with a large network of auto ancillary units in areas such as Irungattukottai and Oragadam. North Chennai, including Ennore, is primarily industrial.

Services have grown alongside industry. The south and west of the city, along GST Road, Old Mahabalipuram Road (OMR) and NH 48, have become commercial areas with large numbers of IT and financial services companies. Healthcare, education and trade make up much of the rest of the economy.

## Main business areas and markets
George Town, around Parry's Corner, is Chennai's main wholesale district. Its narrow streets are organised by trade: Mint Street is known for paper and stationery, and Godown Street for fabric and saree materials. Flowers, accessories and home decor items are also sold in bulk there.

Ritchie Street, which began trading in 1970, is one of the largest electronics markets in the country, dealing in mobile phones, computer accessories and electronic spares. In T. Nagar, Ranganathan Street is a busy shopping street with a well-known wholesale footwear trade alongside jewellery and textiles.

Koyambedu Wholesale Market Complex, spread over about 295 acres with more than 3,000 shops, is one of Asia's largest markets for perishable goods and supplies vegetables, fruit, flowers and groceries across the city.

Guindy Industrial Estate in the south-west, set up in 1956, and Ambattur Industrial Estate in the north-west, opened in 1964, remain important bases for small and medium manufacturers.

## Manufacturing and suppliers
The concentration of automobile and electronics plants has produced a dense supplier network. Buyers can find tool rooms, castings and forgings, rubber and plastic moulders, and logistics firms in the industrial estates and along the Sriperumbudur–Oragadam belt. Guindy and Ambattur suit smaller orders and job work, while the larger plants further out generally deal with established tier-one suppliers.

### What is the STD code for Chennai?
Chennai landlines use the STD code 044. From abroad, dial +91 44 before the number.

### Where is the electronics market in Chennai?
Ritchie Street is the main market for mobile phones, computer accessories and electronic spares.

### Which are Chennai's main industrial estates?
Guindy Industrial Estate (1956) and Ambattur Industrial Estate (1964) are the oldest, while the Sriperumbudur–Oragadam belt hosts most large automobile and electronics plants.
