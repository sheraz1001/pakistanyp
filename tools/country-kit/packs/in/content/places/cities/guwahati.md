Guwahati is the largest city in Assam and the main commercial gateway to north-east India. The state capital, Dispur, lies within the city. At the 2011 census the Guwahati Municipal Corporation area had 957,352 residents in 229,718 households, of whom 495,362 were men and 461,990 women. Including outgrowths, the figure was 962,334.

The city's role is that of a distribution and trading centre. Goods arriving by road and rail from the rest of India are broken down and sent on to the north-eastern states, and Guwahati is also the country's busiest tea auction centre, handling much of the output of Assam's tea gardens.

## Economy and main industries
Wholesale trade and distribution are the core of Guwahati's economy. Traders in the city supply retailers across Assam and the neighbouring north-eastern states.

Tea is the city's most distinctive trade. The Guwahati Tea Auction Centre, established in 1970, is described as the busiest tea-trading centre in India and the third busiest in the world. Brokers, buyers, warehouse operators and packers work around the auctions.

Government, education, healthcare and transport services make up much of the rest of the economy.

## Main business areas and markets
Fancy Bazaar is Guwahati's oldest commercial district and its main wholesale hub. It is surrounded by Pan Bazaar and Lakhtokia, and is also the usual place to buy tea in bulk at wholesale prices, though buyers should note that many shops sell blended tea of unspecified origin without tasting.

Paltan Bazaar is home to the city's main railway station. The Guwahati Tea Auction Centre is a short drive from Fancy Bazaar.

The airport at Borjhar is the 12th busiest in India and the busiest in the north-east.

## Tea trade
Buyers of Assam tea in bulk usually work through the auction system or through brokers registered with the Guwahati Tea Auction Centre. Smaller buyers often buy from wholesalers in Fancy Bazaar instead, where tea is sold loose and in packs alongside other groceries. For specific garden teas or grades, it is worth asking a broker rather than buying blended stock from market shops.
