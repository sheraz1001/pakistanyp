Sukkur stands on the Indus in upper Sindh. Its best-known landmark, the Sukkur Barrage, was built between 1923 and 1932 and feeds a canal system that irrigates a large part of Sindh, so the city's prosperity has long depended on agriculture in the surrounding districts.

Today Sukkur is a wholesale, transport and service centre for upper Sindh. Its markets supply retailers in nearby towns, and the Multan–Sukkur Motorway has made it the southern end of a fast road link with Punjab, which matters for freight operators, traders and travellers alike.

## Key facts
- **Province:** Sindh
- **Population (2023 census, district):** 1,639,897
- **City size:** one of ten cities with a population between half a million and one million in the 2023 census
- **Telephone area code:** 071
- **Infrastructure:** Sukkur Barrage, completed in 1932 (originally named Lloyd Barrage)
- **Road link:** M-5 Multan–Sukkur Motorway, 392 km, opened in September 2019
- **Business body:** Sukkur Chamber of Commerce and Industry (SCCI)

## Economy and main industries
Agriculture in the barrage command area is the foundation of the local economy, and much of Sukkur's trade involves moving farm produce, inputs and consumer goods between the countryside and larger cities. Wholesale trade in cloth, spices, fruit and general goods is a major source of employment, as are transport, warehousing and vehicle repair.

The city also has a sizeable public sector and service base, with government offices, hospitals, colleges and banks serving a wide area. The Sukkur Chamber of Commerce and Industry represents traders and industrialists and has backed joint action with the authorities against counterfeit medicines.

## Main business areas and markets
The Clock Tower, or Ghanta Ghar, about 90 feet high and opened in 1937, is the reference point for the old commercial centre. The lanes beneath it are lined with shops and stalls, and the area is known as a centre of the spice and fruit trade.

Around it lie the city's main markets: Shahi Bazaar, Frere Road, Sarrafa Bazaar for gold and jewellery, the wholesale cloth market, Shaheed Ganj electronics market, Nishter Road and Bunder Road. Small traders in these markets are organised through bodies such as the Sukkur Small Traders Association.

Beyond the old centre, business addresses spread across Old Sukkur and New Sukkur and along Military Road, Minara Road, Queens Road and Workshop Road, with further shops and offices in Barrage Colony and on Airport Road and Shikarpur Road.

## Transport and logistics
The six-lane M-5 motorway, built under the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor, was opened to traffic on 13 September 2019 and was expected to cut the Sukkur–Multan journey from about six hours to three and a half. It connects with the M-4 towards Faisalabad and Lahore. Floods have closed sections at times; a 78 km stretch was reopened in October 2025 after floodwater was cleared. For wholesalers and transporters the motorway is now the main route for goods moving between upper Sindh and Punjab.

## Frequently asked questions
### What is the area code for Sukkur?
Sukkur's telephone area code is 071, or +92 71 from abroad.

### Where are the main markets in Sukkur?
The main markets are around the Clock Tower, including Shahi Bazaar, Frere Road, Sarrafa Bazaar, the wholesale cloth market, Shaheed Ganj electronics market, Nishter Road and Bunder Road.

### When was the Sukkur Barrage built?
Construction ran from 1923 to 1932. It was originally called the Lloyd Barrage.
