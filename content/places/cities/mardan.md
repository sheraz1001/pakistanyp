Mardan is one of the larger cities of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, set in the fertile plain north-east of Peshawar. The district is mainly agricultural, and its main crops of Virginia tobacco, sugarcane and wheat have shaped much of the local industry and trade.

For business seekers, Mardan works as a market town for a wide rural hinterland. Farmers, traders and families from surrounding villages come into the city for wholesale goods, farm inputs, medical care, education and household shopping, which keeps its central bazaars crowded throughout the year.

## Key facts
- **Province:** Khyber Pakhtunkhwa
- **Population (2023 census, district):** 2,744,898
- **Telephone area code:** 0937
- **Main crops:** Virginia tobacco, sugarcane and wheat
- **Industries:** sugar, tobacco, matches, furniture, marble, flour milling, steel and aluminium goods
- **Business body:** Mardan Chamber of Commerce and Industry (MCCI)
- **Heritage site:** Takht-i-Bahi Buddhist ruins, about 16 km north-west of the city (UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1980)

## Economy and main industries
Agriculture and agro-processing dominate. Tobacco growing supports curing, grading and trading businesses, while sugarcane historically fed several sugar mills in the district. That sector has struggled: in 2021 Premier Sugar Mills, the oldest sugar mill in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, closed because of a shortage of cane, and two other mills in Mardan had shut months earlier. Flour mills, marble cutting, furniture making, steel and aluminium workshops and handicrafts make up much of the remaining industrial base.

The Mardan Chamber of Commerce and Industry has signed agreements with the Small and Medium Enterprises Development Authority, the Board of Investment, the State Bank of Pakistan and commercial banks, and has worked on plans to revive closed industrial units in the district.

## Main business areas and markets
Bank Road is the city's best-known shopping street, with clothing, footwear, electronics and banking branches. It is also one of the most congested; the district administration has repeatedly run anti-encroachment drives there and at Neher Chowk in Par Hoti. Saddar Bazaar, near the cantonment, has its own traders' union and serves the Cantt area. Shahedano Bazaar is another central market that sees heavy traffic.

Hoti Bazaar runs towards the Hoti bridge and serves the older Hoti and Par Hoti neighbourhoods. Shamsi Road is known for its concentration of private clinics, diagnostic centres and educational institutions. Because parking in the central bazaars is difficult, larger supermarkets and mega marts have opened along Nowshera Road. Other localities often used in business addresses include Bagh-e-Iram, College Chowk, Sheikh Maltoon Town and Toru.

## Heritage and visitors
The Buddhist monastery at Takht-i-Bahi, founded in the first century CE and used until the seventh century, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1980 together with the nearby remains at Sahr-i-Bahlol. It lies about 16 km north-west of Mardan and draws students, researchers and day visitors, which supports transport operators, guides and roadside eateries on the route from the city.

## Frequently asked questions
### What is the area code for Mardan?
Mardan's telephone area code is 0937, or +92 937 when dialling from abroad.

### Which are the main bazaars in Mardan?
Bank Road, Saddar Bazaar, Shahedano Bazaar and Hoti Bazaar are the main traditional markets, with newer supermarkets along Nowshera Road.

### What does Mardan produce?
The district is best known for Virginia tobacco, sugarcane and wheat, and for industries built on them such as tobacco processing, sugar and flour milling.

### How far is Takht-i-Bahi from Mardan?
The Takht-i-Bahi ruins are about 16 km north-west of Mardan city.
