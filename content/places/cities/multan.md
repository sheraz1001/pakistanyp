Multan is the largest city in southern Punjab and the commercial centre for a wide agricultural region. Its old walled city, with its gates, bazaars and shrines, still defines the centre of town.

For business, Multan is a regional market. Farmers, traders and processors from surrounding districts come here to sell crops and buy inputs, machinery and consumer goods, and the city's chamber of commerce represents a business community focused on agriculture, textiles and trade.

## Key facts
- **Province:** Punjab; headquarters of Multan district and division
- **Population (2023 census):** 5,362,305 in Multan district, including 2,555,486 in Multan City tehsil
- **Telephone area code:** 061
- **Airport:** Multan International Airport, with an international terminal inaugurated by Prime Minister Nawaz Sharif
- **Chamber of commerce:** Multan Chamber of Commerce and Industry
- **Industrial estate:** Multan Industrial Estate, Phases I and II
- **Known for:** mangoes, cotton, blue pottery and Multani khussa footwear

## Economy and main industries
Agriculture drives the local economy. Multan is one of the main mango-growing areas in Pakistan, and many varieties are grown in its orchards, supporting packers, cold storage operators and exporters. Cotton is the other major crop, supplying ginning and textile units in the region.

Textiles and food processing are the main manufacturing activities. Handicrafts are a distinctive part of the economy: Multani blue pottery, decorated in dark blue and turquoise, embroidered clothing, camel skin products and khussa shoes are sold in local bazaars and further afield.

Exporters have long pressed for better cargo and cold storage facilities at Multan airport so that mangoes and other fresh produce can be flown directly to buyers in the Gulf and elsewhere.

## Main business areas and markets
The old walled city, entered through gates such as Delhi Gate, Bohar Gate, Haram Gate, Pak Gate, Lohari Gate and Daulat Gate, holds the traditional bazaars. Hussain Agahi Bazaar is one of the oldest and best-known, selling cloth, kitchenware, electronics, jewellery, food and handicrafts. Chowk Bazaar and the Sarafa market for jewellery are close by, and Ghanta Ghar, the clock tower, marks the centre of the old city.

Saddar Bazaar in the cantonment serves the newer parts of the city, along with residential localities such as Gulgasht Colony, Shah Rukn-e-Alam Colony and Model Town. Bosan Road, which runs from Chungi No. 9 to Bahauddin Zakariya University, is regarded as the city's model road and is lined with shops, offices and educational institutions. Newer housing schemes such as DHA, Wapda Town and Buch Villas have their own commercial areas.

Industry is concentrated in Multan Industrial Estate, managed by the Punjab Industrial Estates Development and Management Company. Phase I covers 743 acres and Phase II a further 667 acres. Main routes out of the city, including Vehari Road, Khanewal Road and the Northern Bypass, link it with neighbouring districts.

## Crafts and handicrafts
Visitors and buyers looking for traditional products usually head to the old city. Blue pottery workshops produce tiles, vases and tableware, and the bazaars around Hussain Agahi stock khussa footwear, embroidered textiles and camel skin goods. Blue pottery in particular is recognisable by its repeated patterns in dark blue and turquoise.

## Frequently asked questions
### What is the area code for Multan?
Multan's landline code is 061. Callers from abroad dial +92 61.

### What is Multan famous for commercially?
Multan is best known for mangoes, cotton and handicrafts such as blue pottery and khussa shoes.

### Where is the main market in Multan?
Hussain Agahi Bazaar in the old walled city is the best-known traditional market, with Chowk Bazaar and Ghanta Ghar nearby.

### Where are factories located in Multan?
Most organised industry is in Multan Industrial Estate, Phases I and II, run by the Punjab Industrial Estates Development and Management Company.
