Bahawalpur is a city in southern Punjab and the headquarters of Bahawalpur district and division. It was the capital of the princely state of Bahawalpur, which acceded to Pakistan in October 1947 and remained autonomous until it was merged into West Pakistan in 1955. The nawabs' palaces, including Noor Mahal, have given it the name City of Palaces.

Bahawalpur district is the largest in Punjab by area, stretching south into the Cholistan desert. The city serves this wide and largely rural area as its main market, administrative and educational centre.

## Key facts
- **Province:** Punjab; headquarters of Bahawalpur district and division
- **Population (2023 census, Bahawalpur district):** 4,284,964, about 38 per cent urban
- **Area (district):** about 24,830 square kilometres, the largest district in Punjab
- **Telephone area code:** 062
- **Chamber of commerce:** Bahawalpur Chamber of Commerce and Industry
- **Energy:** Quaid-e-Azam Solar Park at Lal Suhanra in Cholistan
- **Education:** Islamia University of Bahawalpur
- **Road link:** National Highway N-5, connecting the city with Karachi and Lahore

## Economy and main industries
Agriculture is the base of the economy. The district is a major cotton producer, and the city's traders, ginners and input dealers serve farmers across the district. Livestock is another important activity, and the local chamber has proposed a livestock export zone.

The Bahawalpur Chamber of Commerce and Industry has pushed for more industry in the region, recommending textile mills and food processing plants. It has also campaigned for an industrial estate, and the Bahawalpur Industrial Estate is now one of those managed by the Punjab Industrial Estates Development and Management Company.

Energy is a newer sector. The Quaid-e-Azam Solar Park, on land set aside near Lal Suhanra in Cholistan, began with a 100-megawatt first phase that came online in 2015. It was planned for 1,000 megawatts in total, although later phases have fallen behind the original targets. The park is about 10 km from Bahawalpur airport.

## Main business areas and markets
The old walled city, entered through gates including Farid Gate, Bohar Gate and Ahmedpur Gate, holds Bahawalpur's traditional bazaars, among them Shahi Bazaar and Machli Bazaar. Circular Road runs around the old city and links these markets with the rest of town.

Newer residential and commercial areas include Model Town, Satellite Town, Cantt, Islamia Colony and Abbasia Town, along with University Chowk and Baghdad-ul-Jadeed, which are associated with the Islamia University of Bahawalpur. The main roads out of the city, Yazman Road, Hasilpur Road and Multan Road, connect it with the farming towns of the district and with Multan.

## Tourism and events
Bahawalpur's heritage and desert landscape bring visitors throughout the year. Noor Mahal and Farid Gate are well-known landmarks, and the International Cholistan Desert Jeep Rally is the region's best-known event. The 21st rally, from 11 to 15 February 2026, was held across Bahawalpur, Bahawalnagar and Rahim Yar Khan districts. Events like this create demand for hotels, tour operators, caterers and transport firms.

## Frequently asked questions
### What is the area code for Bahawalpur?
Bahawalpur landlines use 062. From abroad, dial +92 62 and the number.

### Is Bahawalpur the largest district in Punjab?
Yes, by area. Bahawalpur district covers about 24,830 square kilometres, including much of the Cholistan desert.

### What is the Quaid-e-Azam Solar Park?
It is a government-backed solar power project near Lal Suhanra in Cholistan, close to Bahawalpur. Its first 100-megawatt phase began producing electricity in 2015.

### When was the latest Cholistan Jeep Rally held?
The 21st International Cholistan Desert Jeep Rally ran from 11 to 15 February 2026, across Bahawalpur, Bahawalnagar and Rahim Yar Khan districts.
