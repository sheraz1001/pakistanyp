Quetta is the capital of Balochistan, Pakistan's largest province by area, and its largest city. It sits in a valley in the north of the province and is the seat of the provincial government and the main base for banks, hospitals and universities serving Balochistan.

Commercially, Quetta is a gateway city. Goods moving between Pakistan and southern Afghanistan through the Chaman border pass through its markets, and the city is a collection point for fruit grown in the surrounding valleys and imported from Kandahar.

## Key facts
- **Province:** Balochistan (provincial capital)
- **Population (2023 census, Quetta district):** 2,595,492
- **Telephone area code:** 081
- **Airport:** Quetta International Airport, about 12 km south-west of the city
- **Chamber of commerce:** Quetta Chamber of Commerce and Industry
- **Border link:** Chaman crossing to Afghanistan, north-west of the city
- **Known for:** fresh fruit, dry fruit, carpets and Afghan and Baloch handicrafts

## Economy and main industries
Trade is Quetta's largest economic activity. Wholesale markets deal in fruit, vegetables, dry fruit, textiles, electronics and household goods, much of it sold on to the rest of Balochistan. Fruit is especially important: apples and other orchard crops from the surrounding valleys, and pomegranates and other produce brought in from Kandahar, pass through Quetta's dealers on their way to markets elsewhere in Pakistan and abroad.

The Quetta Chamber of Commerce and Industry has repeatedly called for a clearer policy on the fruit and vegetable trade with Afghanistan, which shows how closely local business follows border conditions. Government, education, health services and the cantonment are other major employers.

## Main business areas and markets
Jinnah Road is the city's main commercial artery, lined with shops, offices, banks and eating places. Liaquat Bazaar, on Shahrah-e-Liaquat and one of the oldest and busiest markets in Quetta, sells handicrafts, jewellery, clothing, rugs and shawls. Kandahari Bazaar, on Shahrah-e-Iqbal, is known for Afghan goods, hand-woven carpets, embroidered clothing and dry fruit.

Suraj Ganj Bazaar is another of the city's established bazaars, and the streets between these markets hold many smaller wholesalers and retailers.

Airport Road connects the city to Quetta International Airport and has hotels, restaurants and showrooms. Other roads with shops and offices include Sariab Road, Samungli Road and Zarghoon Road, and residential areas such as Satellite Town, Jinnah Town, Shahbaz Town and Model Town have their own local markets.

## Cross-border trade
Chaman and Torkham are Pakistan's two main road routes into Afghanistan, and Chaman serves Quetta. Fresh fruit, including the prized Kandahari pomegranate, is imported through Chaman each season. When the crossing is suspended, traders and the local chambers have warned that the fruit and vegetable trade can be badly affected, and transport between the border and Quetta is also disrupted. Businesses dealing in imported produce or transit goods usually track the crossing's status closely.

## Finding businesses in Quetta
People searching in Quetta commonly look for fruit and dry fruit wholesalers, carpet and handicraft dealers, transport and goods companies, hotels, hospitals and government contractors. Searching by market is helpful: Kandahari Bazaar and Liaquat Bazaar for traditional goods, Jinnah Road for retail and services. PakistanYP lists Quetta businesses by market and area.

## Frequently asked questions
### What is the area code for Quetta?
Quetta's landline code is 081. From abroad, dial +92 81 followed by the number.

### What are the main markets in Quetta?
The best-known are Liaquat Bazaar, Kandahari Bazaar and the shops along Jinnah Road. They sell handicrafts, carpets, clothing, dry fruit and general goods.

### What is the population of Quetta?
The 2023 census counted 2,595,492 people in Quetta district.

### Which airport serves Quetta?
Quetta International Airport, about 12 km south-west of the city, has domestic flights and a small number of international routes, mainly to the Gulf.
