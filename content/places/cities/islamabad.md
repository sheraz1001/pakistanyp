Islamabad is the federal capital of Pakistan, a planned city laid out in a grid of lettered and numbered sectors at the foot of the Margalla Hills. Its economy is shaped by the federal government, the diplomatic community, international organisations and the large number of companies that keep offices close to ministries and regulators.

The sector plan makes Islamabad easier to navigate than most Pakistani cities. Each residential sector has its own Markaz, a central commercial block with shops, banks, restaurants and offices, while larger offices sit along Jinnah Avenue in Blue Area and light industry is grouped in the I sectors.

## Key facts
- **Status:** Federal capital; Islamabad Capital Territory
- **Population (2023 census, Islamabad Capital Territory):** 2,363,863, of whom about 47 per cent live in urban areas
- **Area:** 906 square kilometres
- **Telephone area code:** 051, shared with neighbouring Rawalpindi
- **Airport:** Islamabad International Airport, near Fateh Jang, opened to passengers in May 2018
- **Chamber of commerce:** Islamabad Chamber of Commerce and Industry, incorporated in 1984, Aiwan-e-Sanat-o-Tijarat Road, G-8/1
- **Planning authority:** Capital Development Authority

## Economy and main industries
Government is the largest employer, and much private business exists to serve it: law firms, consultancies, contractors, security companies, travel agents and hotels. Banks, telecom operators, development agencies and embassies add a large professional workforce, which in turn supports private schools, clinics, restaurants and real estate.

Islamabad has also built up an information technology sector, with software firms and IT service providers working from Blue Area, the F and G sector markets and newer commercial developments. Construction and property development are significant, especially in the private housing schemes along the Islamabad Expressway and Kashmir Highway.

## Main business areas and markets
Blue Area is the central business district, stretching along Jinnah Avenue between sectors F and G. It holds corporate offices, bank branches, hotels and government-linked organisations.

The best-known retail markets are in the sector Markaz areas. Jinnah Super Market in F-7 Markaz is an upmarket shopping area, Super Market in F-6 is one of the oldest markets in the city, and Kohsar Market in F-6/3 is a smaller upmarket area of cafes and restaurants. Aabpara Market on the south-west edge of G-6 dates from around 1960, and Melody Market, the G-6 Markaz, is a commercial centre that also houses the head office of National Bank of Pakistan.

For everyday wholesale goods, Karachi Company in G-9 Markaz is the busiest trading area, with clothing, electronics, household goods and many small wholesalers. G-10 Markaz, G-11 Markaz, F-10 Markaz, F-11 Markaz, I-8 Markaz and E-11 Markaz serve the residential sectors around them.

Industrial activity is concentrated in the I sectors. Parts of I-9 and I-10 form the Industrial Area, home to light manufacturing, printing presses, warehouses, workshops and distributors.

## Living and working in the capital
Many companies choose Islamabad for access to federal ministries, regulators and embassies, and for its security and planned infrastructure. Visitors and new businesses should note that land use is closely regulated by the Capital Development Authority, which has sealed shops in some markets for non-conforming use. Anyone renting premises should check that the building is approved for the intended commercial activity.

## Finding businesses in Islamabad
People searching in Islamabad often look for consultants, law firms, IT companies, hotels and guest houses, travel agents, private schools, hospitals and property dealers, as well as suppliers in the Industrial Area. Because the city is divided into sectors, searching by sector or Markaz is usually the fastest way to find a nearby provider. PakistanYP groups Islamabad listings by sector and market.

## Frequently asked questions
### What is Blue Area in Islamabad?
Blue Area is Islamabad's central business district along Jinnah Avenue. It contains many of the city's corporate offices, banks and hotels.

### Where is the Industrial Area in Islamabad?
The Industrial Area occupies parts of sectors I-9 and I-10. It has light manufacturing units, printing presses, warehouses and workshops.

### Where is the Islamabad Chamber of Commerce and Industry?
The ICCI is based at Chamber House on Aiwan-e-Sanat-o-Tijarat Road in G-8/1. It was incorporated in 1984.

### Does Islamabad have the same area code as Rawalpindi?
Yes. Both cities use the landline code 051, so a number alone does not tell you which side of the twin cities a business is on.
