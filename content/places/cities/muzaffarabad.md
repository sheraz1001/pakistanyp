Muzaffarabad is the capital of Azad Jammu and Kashmir, an administrative territory of Pakistan. The city lies where the Neelum River joins the Jhelum at Domel.

As the seat of the territory's government, Muzaffarabad hosts the Civil Secretariat, where the Legislative Assembly also meets, and the main government departments. Government employment, together with education, health care and tourism traffic to the Neelum valley, supports most of the city's retail and service businesses.

## Key facts
- **Status:** Capital of Azad Jammu and Kashmir, an administrative territory of Pakistan
- **Population (2023 census, district):** 703,661, as reported by the AJK Planning and Development Department
- **Telephone area code:** 05822
- **Rivers:** Jhelum and Neelum, meeting at Domel
- **Government:** AJK Civil Secretariat and Legislative Assembly
- **Business bodies:** Muzaffarabad Chamber of Commerce and Industry; Central Traders Association Muzaffarabad

## Economy and main industries
The public sector is the largest employer, and much private business depends on it: contractors, suppliers, printing and stationery firms, consultants, caterers and transport operators all work closely with government departments.

Retail and wholesale trade serve both city residents and people from the surrounding district, who come in for clothing, household goods, building materials and medical care. Tourism adds seasonal demand, since Muzaffarabad is the starting point for road trips into the Neelum valley, and hotels, guest houses, car rental and restaurants benefit in the summer months. The AJK government has listed a special economic zone at Muzaffarabad among its industrial projects in progress.

The Muzaffarabad Chamber of Commerce and Industry represents local traders and has worked with other chambers, including the Lahore Chamber, on business cooperation. It has also raised concerns about bureaucratic obstacles to investment in the territory.

## Main business areas and markets
Madina Market is the oldest and busiest commercial area in the city. It is known for traditional Kashmiri clothing, especially embroidered dresses, which draw shoppers before Eid. To ease congestion, the authorities have banned vehicles from entering the market, with scheduled access for residents, and have acted against illegal parking and encroachments.

Upper Adda, near the University of Azad Jammu and Kashmir, is another commercial area on the route towards Madina Market, and Chattar, particularly Upper Chattar near the government buildings, has hotels, offices and services used by officials and visitors. Domel, at the confluence of the rivers, is another established locality. Other areas used in business addresses include Lower Plate, Chehla Bandi, Gojra and Tariqabad.

## Government and visitors
Because the secretariat and departments are based here, people from across the territory travel to Muzaffarabad on official business. That steady flow supports hotels, eateries, documentation and photocopy shops, legal and tax advisers, and the transport services that link the city with the rest of Azad Jammu and Kashmir and with Islamabad and Rawalpindi. Upper Chattar, where a Pearl Continental hotel stands near the state buildings, is the usual base for official visitors. Traders are organised through the Central Traders Association, which speaks for market shopkeepers on issues such as strike calls and market closures.

## Frequently asked questions
### What is the area code for Muzaffarabad?
The telephone area code is 05822, or +92 5822 from abroad.

### What is the main market in Muzaffarabad?
Madina Market is the oldest and busiest market, known especially for embroidered Kashmiri clothing. Vehicles are not allowed inside to keep it accessible for shoppers.

### Where do the Jhelum and Neelum rivers meet?
They meet at Domel, on the edge of Muzaffarabad city.

### Is there a chamber of commerce in Muzaffarabad?
Yes. The Muzaffarabad Chamber of Commerce and Industry represents the local business community.
