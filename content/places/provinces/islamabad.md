The Islamabad Capital Territory (ICT) is the federal unit that contains Pakistan's capital. It is not part of any province: the territory is governed directly by the federal government, and laws and taxes that would elsewhere be provincial are handled by federal bodies or by the territory's own administration. For businesses this means a different set of offices from Punjab, even though Rawalpindi lies just across the boundary.

The territory is larger than the planned city. According to the 2023 census, slightly more than half of ICT's residents live in areas classed as rural, in villages and fast-growing settlements to the south and east of the sectors. This guide covers the territory as a whole; the city of Islamabad has its own guide.

## Key facts
- **Status:** Federal capital territory, not part of any province
- **Seat:** Islamabad
- **Population (2023 census, territory):** 2,363,863, of whom 1,108,872 were urban and 1,254,991 rural
- **Area:** about 906 sq km
- **Telephone area code:** 051, shared with Rawalpindi
- **Planning authority:** Capital Development Authority (CDA), set up under the CDA Ordinance 1960
- **Local government:** Metropolitan Corporation Islamabad, headed by a mayor
- **Airport:** Islamabad International Airport, opened in 2018 at Fateh Jang in Attock district, Punjab, about 30 km from central Islamabad

## Administration
Three bodies matter most to anyone doing business in the territory. The ICT Administration is led by a Chief Commissioner, who exercises powers that a provincial government would hold elsewhere, and a Deputy Commissioner who handles the day-to-day administration of the district.

The CDA, created in 1960, owns the master plan and controls land use, building approvals and the development of sectors. The Metropolitan Corporation Islamabad, set up under the ICT Local Government Act 2015, is the elected local tier and runs municipal services through union councils.

Because the territory is federal, sales tax on services in ICT is collected by the Federal Board of Revenue under the Islamabad Capital Territory (Tax on Services) Ordinance, 2001, rather than by a provincial revenue authority.

## The sector system
Islamabad was laid out under a master plan prepared by Constantinos Doxiadis in 1960, on a grid of square sectors. Each sector is named with a letter and a number, such as F-7, G-9 or I-10, and most residential sectors are built around a central commercial area known as a Markaz, such as F-7 Markaz or G-9 Markaz. Blue Area, along Jinnah Avenue, is the main office strip.

The CDA's Zoning Regulation of 1992 divided the territory into five zones. Zones I and II are the urban development zones where the CDA develops sectors; private housing schemes are permitted in Zones II and V; Zone III is a reserved area; and Zone IV is kept for mixed uses. This is why large private schemes such as DHA and Bahria Town sit outside the CDA's lettered sector grid.

## Main business areas and markets
The territory's commercial life falls into three groups. The first is the federal and corporate core: Blue Area and the sector markets, where banks, consultancies, law firms, telecom companies and head offices are concentrated.

The second is industry. The Islamabad Chamber of Commerce and Industry identifies sectors I-9 and I-10 and the Kahuta Industrial Triangle as the territory's three main industrial estates. The chamber has said these areas are close to full and has long pressed for a new industrial zone near the airport and motorway.

The third is the rural and peri-urban belt. Settlements such as Tarnol, Bhara Kahu, Tarlai, Sihala, Koral and Chak Shahzad have grown into busy local markets for building materials, transport, workshops, agricultural supplies and everyday retail, serving both villagers and residents of the newer housing schemes.

## Federal institutions
Most of Pakistan's federal government is based in the territory, including Parliament, the Supreme Court, the federal secretariat and the Diplomatic Enclave. Several regulators that businesses deal with directly are headquartered here, among them the Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan, whose head office is in Blue Area. This concentration supports a large market for legal and tax advisers, consultants, translators, travel agents and event companies.

The Margalla Hills National Park, established in 1980 and covering 17,386 hectares, lies within the territory, alongside the sectors.

## Finding businesses in Islamabad Capital Territory
People searching in the territory often want consultants, lawyers and tax advisers near the federal offices, contractors and building material suppliers in the rural areas and new housing schemes, and manufacturers or printers in I-9 and I-10. ## Frequently asked questions
### Is Islamabad part of Punjab?
No. The Islamabad Capital Territory is a separate federal unit administered by the federal government, although it is surrounded by Punjab and shares the 051 dialling code with Rawalpindi.

### Who approves building plans in Islamabad?
Within the sectors and most planned areas, building plans and land use are controlled by the Capital Development Authority. Private housing schemes must also follow CDA zoning rules.

### Where are the industrial areas in Islamabad Capital Territory?
The main industrial estates are in sectors I-9 and I-10, along with the Kahuta Industrial Triangle.

### Who collects sales tax on services in Islamabad?
The Federal Board of Revenue collects it in ICT, unlike the provinces, where provincial revenue authorities do this.
