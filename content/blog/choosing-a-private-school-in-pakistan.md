The best schools in Pakistan are not always the most famous or the most expensive. They are the ones that suit your child: the right exam system, qualified and stable teachers, a safe campus, a reasonable commute and fees you can sustain for ten or more years. Start by choosing the board (matric/FSc, AKU-EB or Cambridge O and A Levels), then shortlist registered schools near home, visit them, and compare the full fee schedule, not just monthly tuition.

Parents often decide on reputation alone and discover later that the fees rise every year, the commute takes an hour, or the board does not suit their child's plans. This guide explains the options, how fees and fee increases are regulated, how admissions usually work and the questions to ask before you pay an admission fee.

:::takeaways
- Choose the exam system first: matric and FSc, AKU-EB, or Cambridge O and A Levels.
- Check that the school is registered with the provincial authority or, in Islamabad, PEIRA.
- Ask for the complete fee schedule in writing, including admission, security, annual and exam charges.
- Annual fee increases are capped in several jurisdictions; know the rules where you live.
- Visit during school hours, meet teachers and check safety, transport and class sizes.
:::

## Step 1: Choose the right exam board

Pakistan's school system offers three main routes to the end of school.

| Board | Qualifications | Who runs it | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Federal and provincial boards | SSC (matric) and HSSC (FSc, FA, ICS, I.Com) | FBISE in Islamabad, BISEs in each region | Students aiming for local universities, medical and engineering entry tests |
| Aga Khan University Examination Board | SSC and HSSC | AKU-EB, a private, federally recognised board | Families wanting a national qualification with concept-based exams |
| Cambridge International | O Levels and A Levels (IGCSE in some schools) | Cambridge, through registered schools | Students planning to study abroad or at universities that prefer Cambridge |

**Matric and FSc** remain the most common route and are taught in thousands of private schools at many price points. The Federal Board of Intermediate and Secondary Education ([FBISE](https://www.fbise.edu.pk)) serves Islamabad and affiliated institutions; provincial boards (BISEs) serve districts such as Lahore, Karachi, Rawalpindi, Peshawar, Multan and Faisalabad.

**O and A Levels** are generally more expensive, both in school fees and exam fees, but popular with families considering foreign universities. To use them for admission to Pakistani colleges and universities, students need equivalence from the Inter Boards Coordination Commission ([IBCC](https://www.ibcc.edu.pk)). For O Levels, IBCC requires eight subjects, including English, Urdu, Islamiyat and Pakistan Studies, plus science or other subjects depending on the group.

> [!TIP]
> If your child might switch from O Levels to FSc, or apply for a local medical college, check the IBCC equivalence rules and entry test requirements early. Subject choices at age 13 or 14 can limit options later.

## Step 2: Check registration and regulation

Private schools must register with their regulator. In Islamabad, this is the Private Educational Institutions Regulatory Authority (PEIRA). Each province has its own registration system run by its education department.

Ask the school for its registration certificate and check that it covers the classes your child will attend. Registered schools are subject to rules on fees, facilities and teacher qualifications, and you have a regulator to complain to if things go wrong.

## Step 3: Understand school fees and fee increases

Monthly tuition is only part of the cost. Ask for a written schedule that lists:

- admission fee (usually one-time and non-refundable)
- security deposit (usually refundable when the child leaves)
- monthly tuition fee
- annual or development charges
- exam, lab, computer and activity fees
- books, uniforms and stationery
- transport, if offered

### Rules on fee increases

Fee increases at private schools have been contested in court for years. In June 2019, the Supreme Court upheld a Sindh High Court order limiting annual tuition increases to 5%. Regulators have since issued their own rules. PEIRA has directed Islamabad schools not to raise fees beyond prescribed limits, and in January 2026 an official said no school could raise fees by more than 8% in any circumstances.

Rules differ across provinces and change over time, so ask the school how its increases are set and check with your provincial education department or PEIRA if an increase looks excessive.

> [!WARNING]
> Be wary of schools that charge large "development" or "annual" fees not shown on the original fee schedule. Ask for every charge in writing before you pay the admission fee.

## Step 4: Visit and compare the best schools in Pakistan near you

A visit during school hours tells you more than any brochure. Watch how teachers speak to children, how crowded the corridors are and whether the toilets and drinking water are clean.

:::checklist
- What is the average class size, and the student-teacher ratio?
- What qualifications do teachers have, and how long do they usually stay?
- Which board and syllabus does each level follow?
- How is progress reported to parents, and how often are meetings held?
- What are the safety arrangements: gates, guards, CCTV, fire exits, first aid?
- Is there a library, science lab, computer lab and outdoor play area?
- What support is available for children who need extra help?
- What are the past results in board or Cambridge exams?
:::

Ask current parents for their views, not just the admissions office. Results matter, but so do teacher stability, discipline and how the school treats children who are struggling.

## Step 5: Plan for the admissions process

Admissions timelines vary, but many schools open registration months before the new session. Some well-known schools hold entry tests and interviews, especially for Class 1 and Class 6.

:::steps School admissions timeline
- Shortlist | Pick three to five registered schools within a reasonable commute.
- Visit | Tour each school during working hours and collect the full fee schedule.
- Register | Submit the registration form and fee before the deadline.
- Assess | Prepare your child for any entry test or interview.
- Confirm | Pay the admission fee and security deposit, and keep every receipt.
- Prepare | Buy books and uniforms and arrange transport before the first day.
:::

Documents usually requested include the child's B-Form (NADRA's Child Registration Certificate), birth certificate, parents' CNIC copies, photographs, and the previous school's leaving certificate and results.

## Step 6: Consider commute, transport and location

A long commute tires children and eats into family time. Map the route at school time, not on a Sunday. If the school offers transport, ask about the vehicle condition, seating, driver checks and whether a female attendant travels with younger children.

### How do I find the best schools in Pakistan for my child?
Decide on the exam board first: matric and FSc, AKU-EB, or Cambridge O and A Levels. Then shortlist registered schools within a reasonable commute, visit during school hours, meet teachers, ask current parents for feedback and compare the full fee schedule. The best school is the one that suits your child, not simply the most famous name.

### Which is better: matric or O Levels?
Neither is better for every child. Matric and FSc are the main route to most Pakistani universities and local entry tests, and are more affordable. O and A Levels are popular with families considering study abroad and are often taught with more emphasis on concepts, but cost more. Students using O Levels locally need IBCC equivalence.

### What is AKU-EB?
The Aga Khan University Examination Board is a private, federally recognised examination board offering SSC and HSSC qualifications, equivalent to matric and intermediate. Its exams focus on understanding concepts rather than memorisation. It is taught in a smaller number of schools across Pakistan, and its certificates are accepted by Pakistani colleges and universities like other boards.

### How much can private schools increase fees each year in Pakistan?
It depends on where you live. In 2019 the Supreme Court upheld a 5% annual cap on tuition increases arising from Sindh. In Islamabad, PEIRA regulates increases, and an official said in January 2026 that no school may raise fees by more than 8%. Check your provincial education department or regulator for current rules.

### What documents are needed for school admission in Pakistan?
Schools typically ask for the child's B-Form, also called NADRA's Child Registration Certificate, a birth certificate, parents' CNIC copies, recent photographs and, for older children, the previous school's leaving certificate and report card. Some schools also ask for vaccination records or proof of address. Check each school's list before applying.

### What is IBCC equivalence and who needs it?
IBCC equivalence converts O Level, A Level and foreign qualifications into matric and intermediate equivalents for use in Pakistan. Students with Cambridge or foreign qualifications usually need it for admission to Pakistani colleges and universities. For O Levels, IBCC requires eight subjects, including English, Urdu, Islamiyat and Pakistan Studies. Apply through the IBCC website.

### At what age do children start school in Pakistan?
Many private schools start playgroup or nursery at around age three, with Class 1 usually starting at around five or six. Age cut-off dates vary by school and province, so check each school's admission policy. Popular schools may test or interview children for early years entry, and places can fill months in advance.

### How do I check if a private school is registered?
Ask the school for its registration certificate from the provincial education authority or, in Islamabad, from PEIRA. Check the classes and campus it covers, and its validity. You can also contact the regulator directly to confirm. Registration gives you a route to complain about fees, facilities or safety concerns.

### Are school fees refundable if my child leaves?
The admission fee is usually non-refundable. The security deposit is generally refundable when your child leaves, subject to clearing dues and returning school property. Notice requirements for withdrawal vary, and some schools charge a month's fee in lieu of notice. Read the fee policy carefully and get refund terms in writing before admission.

### What questions should I ask on a school visit?
Ask about class sizes, teacher qualifications and turnover, the board and syllabus, past exam results, how progress is reported, safety arrangements such as gates and fire exits, facilities such as labs and libraries, and support for children who struggle. Also ask current parents for their honest views on communication and discipline.
:::
