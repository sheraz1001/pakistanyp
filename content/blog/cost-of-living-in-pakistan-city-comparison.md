The cost of living in Pakistan depends more on *where* in a city you live than on which city you choose. As a rough rule, Islamabad is the most expensive of the three big cities for housing, Lahore offers the widest range of mid-priced neighbourhoods, and Karachi has the largest gap between its cheapest and most expensive areas.

For most families, rent, electricity, school fees and transport take the biggest share of the monthly budget. Food prices are broadly similar across the three cities, while housing and schooling vary by locality. This guide compares those costs, flags the figures you should check yourself, and shows where households are saving money in 2026.

:::takeaways
- Rent is the biggest variable: the same 2-bedroom flat can cost two or three times more in a premium sector.
- Islamabad's F-sectors are the costliest of the three cities; Lahore and Karachi have many more mid-range options.
- Electricity tariffs are set nationally, but heat, load and usage patterns make Karachi bills seasonal and steep.
- Food and grocery prices differ little between the cities; your shopping habits matter more.
- Solar, school choice and commute distance are the three biggest levers for cutting costs.
:::

## Cost of living in Pakistan: the big picture for 2026

Inflation fell sharply during 2024 and 2025, then picked up again during the 2025-26 financial year, according to Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS) data reported in the press. Food, fuel and utility prices move every month, so treat any figure in this article as a snapshot. PBS publishes the monthly Consumer Price Index on its [official website](https://www.pbs.gov.pk) in the first days of each month.

Incomes have not kept pace for many households. The federal budget for 2026-27 raised the minimum wage to roughly Rs 40,000 a month or slightly more, with reports differing on the exact figure. Provinces set their own rates, and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa announced Rs 45,000. In practice, a single-income family on minimum wage struggles to afford rent in any formal housing in the big cities.

## Rent: Karachi vs Lahore vs Islamabad

Housing is where the three cities differ most. The ranges below are indicative, based on typical asking rents for 2-bedroom homes in commonly searched areas. Actual rents depend on the building, floor, parking, water supply and how recently the property was renovated.

:::bars Typical 2-bedroom rent by area (PKR per month, indicative)
- Lahore: Johar Town, Township | 30000 | Rs 20k–40k
- Karachi: Gulistan-e-Jauhar, Nazimabad | 37500 | Rs 25k–50k
- Islamabad: G-sectors | 37500 | Rs 25k–50k
- Lahore: Gulberg, Model Town | 70000 | Rs 50k–90k
- Islamabad: F-sectors | 90000 | Rs 60k–120k
:::

### What pushes rent up or down

- **Location and security.** Gated societies and central sectors charge a premium.
- **Water and power reliability.** A building with its own water supply or backup power commands higher rent.
- **Advance and security deposit.** Many landlords ask for several months' rent upfront, which hits cash flow harder than the monthly rent.
- **Commute.** A cheaper flat far from work can cost more once fuel and time are counted.

## Electricity, gas and water bills

Electricity tariffs are set nationally by NEPRA and the federal government, so the base rate per unit is the same whether you live in Lahore or Islamabad. Karachi is supplied by K-Electric, under the same national tariff framework.

For 2026, NEPRA kept the base rate for protected domestic consumers at Rs 10.54 per unit for the first 100 units and Rs 13.01 for 101 to 200 units. To be "protected", a household must use 200 units or less each month for six consecutive months. Above that, rates rise steeply, and taxes, fuel adjustments and fixed charges are added on top. From early 2026, fixed monthly charges also apply to many domestic connections based on sanctioned load.

What differs between cities is **usage**. Karachi's long, humid summer keeps air conditioners running for more months of the year. Islamabad and Lahore have shorter peaks but colder winters, when gas shortages push some households onto electric heaters.

Water is often a hidden cost. In parts of Karachi and some Lahore and Rawalpindi neighbourhoods, families regularly buy water from tankers. Find [water supply services](/categories/water-supply) in your area and ask neighbours what they pay before you sign a lease.

## Transport and commuting costs

Fuel prices are revised by the government every fortnight and apply nationwide, so the cost per litre is the same in all three cities. Your transport bill depends on distance and mode:

| Mode | Karachi | Lahore | Islamabad / Rawalpindi |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Public bus or BRT | Green Line and other BRT routes, plus minibuses | Metrobus and Orange Line train | Metrobus linking Islamabad and Rawalpindi |
| Motorcycle | Most common option for cost | Most common option for cost | Common, but distances are longer |
| Ride-hailing | Widely available | Widely available | Widely available |
| Private car | Long commutes and traffic | Shorter average commutes | Car-dependent in newer sectors and societies |

Households that live within reach of a BRT or metro line can save a large share of their commute costs. If you drive, fuel and maintenance usually cost more than buyers expect; keep a trusted mechanic's number to avoid overpaying.

## Schooling: often the second-biggest cost

For families with children, private school fees can rival rent. Fees range from low-fee neighbourhood schools to premium chains whose monthly charges are many times higher. On top of tuition, budget for admission fees, security deposits, annual charges, books, uniforms and transport.

Islamabad has a regulator, PEIRA, that caps how much private schools can raise fees each year. Other provinces have their own rules. Compare [schools](/categories/schools) and [academies and tuition centres](/categories/academies-tuition) in your area, and ask for the full fee schedule in writing before admission.

## Food, groceries and daily needs

Food prices move with national supply, so the difference between cities is small. What changes your grocery bill is where and how you buy:

- **Wholesale markets and mandis** are cheaper for fruit, vegetables, flour and rice bought in bulk.
- **Supermarkets** cost more for fresh produce but often run discounts on packaged goods.
- **Neighbourhood shops** offer convenience and credit, but prices can be higher.

Find [shops and supermarkets](/categories/shops-supermarkets) and fruit and vegetable sellers near you, and compare prices for your ten most-bought items once a month.

:::checklist
- Write down your fixed costs: rent, school fees, loan payments
- Check your last 12 electricity bills for units used and fixed charges
- Price your monthly grocery basket at two different shops
- Add up fuel, ride-hailing and school transport for a typical month
- Set aside a fund for rent advance, repairs and medical costs
- Review mobile and internet packages once a year
:::

## How households are cutting costs in 2026

Families across all three cities are making similar changes:

1. **Rooftop solar.** Higher grid tariffs have pushed many homeowners to install solar systems, even after changes to net metering rules for new users. Speak to several [solar energy companies](/categories/solar-energy) and compare quotes.
2. **Moving closer to work or school.** Paying slightly more rent can save more on fuel and time.
3. **Switching schools.** Some parents move children to mid-fee schools with good results instead of premium brands.
4. **Becoming a filer.** Filers pay lower withholding taxes on banking, property and vehicles. Our guide on [how to become a filer](/blogs/how-to-become-a-filer-in-pakistan) explains the steps.
5. **Bulk buying.** Staples such as flour, rice, pulses and cooking oil cost less per kilo in larger quantities.

> [!NOTE]
> The cost of living in Pakistan changes quickly. Use the ranges here as a starting point and check current rents, fees and tariffs locally before making a big decision such as relocating.

## Which city is cheapest to live in?

There is no single answer. Lahore usually offers the most choice of affordable, well-served neighbourhoods. Karachi has the biggest job market and very cheap areas, but water and heat can add costs. Islamabad offers good services and greenery but higher rents, which is why many families working in Islamabad live in [Rawalpindi](/cities/rawalpindi).

Explore local businesses, schools and services on our city pages for [Karachi](/cities/karachi), [Lahore](/cities/lahore) and [Islamabad](/cities/islamabad) to see what each area offers.

## Final word: budget by neighbourhood, not by city

Comparing the cost of living in Pakistan city by city is a useful start, but your real budget is set street by street. Shortlist two or three localities, visit at different times of day, and talk to people who already live there.

Your next step: write down your current monthly costs using the checklist above, then compare them with two neighbourhoods you are considering.

:::faq
### Which is the most expensive city in Pakistan to live in?
Islamabad is generally the most expensive of the big cities for housing, especially in its central F and E sectors. Karachi and Lahore have premium areas that are just as costly, but they also have far more affordable neighbourhoods. For most families, the choice of locality and school affects monthly costs more than the city itself.

### What is a good salary to live in Karachi, Lahore or Islamabad?
It depends on family size, housing and schooling. A single person sharing accommodation can manage on much less than a family renting a 2-bedroom flat and paying private school fees. Add up rent, utilities, school fees, transport and groceries for your chosen area, then add a buffer of about 10 to 20 percent for price rises.

### Is Lahore cheaper than Karachi?
For rent, Lahore and Karachi are broadly comparable, and both have cheap and expensive areas. Food and fuel cost about the same nationally. Karachi households may spend more on cooling and, in some areas, on water tankers. Lahore households may spend more on winter heating. Compare specific localities rather than whole cities.

### How much is rent for a 2-bedroom flat in Pakistan?
Indicatively, a 2-bedroom flat in a mid-range area of Karachi, Lahore or Islamabad's G-sectors often rents for roughly Rs 20,000 to Rs 50,000 a month. Premium areas such as Lahore's Gulberg or Islamabad's F-sectors can cost two to three times more. Landlords commonly ask for an advance and security deposit as well.

### How is the electricity bill calculated in Pakistan?
Your bill is based on units used, priced in slabs set by NEPRA and the federal government. Protected households using 200 units or less a month for six months pay lower rates. On top of the unit charge, bills include fuel price adjustments, fixed charges, taxes and the electricity duty. Check the breakdown on your bill each month.

### What is the minimum wage in Pakistan in 2026?
The federal budget for 2026-27 raised the minimum wage to roughly Rs 40,000 a month or slightly more, with reports differing on the exact figure. Provinces set their own minimum wages, and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa announced Rs 45,000. Check your provincial labour department's notification for the rate that applies to you.

### Is it cheaper to live in Rawalpindi than Islamabad?
Generally yes. Rents in many Rawalpindi neighbourhoods are lower than in Islamabad's sectors, which is why many people working in Islamabad live in Rawalpindi and commute. The Metrobus connects the two cities. Factor in commute time and fuel before deciding, especially if you work on the far side of Islamabad.

### How can I reduce my household expenses in Pakistan?
Start with the biggest costs. Compare rents in nearby localities, review school fees, and check whether rooftop solar makes sense for your electricity use. Buy staples in bulk, compare grocery prices monthly and become a tax filer to avoid higher withholding taxes. Small changes to transport, such as using BRT, also add up over a year.

### Where can I check the current inflation rate in Pakistan?
The Pakistan Bureau of Statistics publishes the Consumer Price Index every month, usually in the first few days of the following month. It reports national, urban and rural inflation and price changes for key items. The State Bank of Pakistan also publishes inflation analysis in its monetary policy statements and reports.

### Are food prices different in Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad?
Only slightly. Staples follow national and regional supply, so prices move together. Differences come mainly from where you shop: wholesale markets and mandis are usually cheaper than supermarkets for fresh produce, while supermarkets may be cheaper for some packaged goods during promotions. Comparing a fixed basket monthly is the best way to save.
