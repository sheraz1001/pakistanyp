If you are buying or selling a house or plot this year, the **property tax Pakistan filer non-filer** gap is the single biggest number in your deal. The 2026-27 federal budget cut the advance taxes that filers pay when they buy (Section 236K) and sell (Section 236C) property, and turned them into flat rates. Non-filers, people who are not on the FBR's Active Taxpayer List (ATL), continue to pay far higher rates.

In short: filers now pay a low single-digit percentage on each side of a deal, while non-filer buyers can pay 10.5% or more. The budget also removed Section 7E, the annual tax on deemed income from property. This explainer sets out what changed, where official documents and press reports differ, and what to check before you sign.

:::takeaways
- The FBR's 2026-27 salient features said advance taxes on property sales and purchases were reduced and converted to flat rates for filers.
- Reported filer rates are 2.75% for sellers (236C) and 1.25% for buyers (236K); the salient features summary stated 1.5% for buyers, so confirm the operative rate.
- Non-filer buyers remain on value bands reported at 10.5% to 18.5%.
- Section 7E, the deemed income tax on certain properties, was omitted, according to the FBR's salient features.
- Provincial charges such as stamp duty, capital value tax and registration fees are separate and set by each province.
:::

## Property tax in Pakistan: which taxes apply to a deal

A typical sale involves federal and provincial taxes. The federal taxes are collected by the FBR (Federal Board of Revenue), usually at the time of transfer by the registrar or housing authority.

| Tax | Who pays | Level | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Advance tax on purchase (Section 236K) | Buyer | Federal | Much lower for filers |
| Advance tax on sale (Section 236C) | Seller | Federal | Much lower for filers |
| Capital gains tax | Seller | Federal | Depends on acquisition date and the rules for the tax year |
| Stamp duty and registration fee | Usually buyer | Provincial | Rates set by each province |
| Capital value tax (CVT) | Usually buyer | Provincial | Applies in some provinces and areas |
| Annual property tax | Owner | Provincial | Collected by the provincial excise department |

> [!NOTE]
> Housing societies and development authorities also charge their own transfer fees. These are not taxes, but they add to your costs. Ask the society for its fee schedule before agreeing a price.

## Filer and non-filer property tax rates after the 2026-27 budget

The federal budget was presented in June 2026. In its salient features, the FBR said the advance tax on sale and purchase of property was reduced and converted into lower flat rates. Several tax practitioners and calculators then reported the operative rates in the amended Income Tax Ordinance.

:::bars Reported advance tax rates on property, 2026-27 (%, indicative)
- Filer buyer (236K) | 1.25 | 1.25%
- Filer seller (236C) | 2.75 | 2.75%
- Non-filer seller (236C) | 11.5 | about 11–11.5%
- Non-filer buyer, lowest band | 10.5 | 10.5%
- Non-filer buyer, highest band | 18.5 | 18.5%
:::

There is a known inconsistency. The salient features summary gave 1.5% for filer buyers, while the consolidated law reported by practitioners states 1.25%. For non-filer sellers, sources give either 11% or 11.5%. Before the budget, filers paid banded rates, reported at 1.5–2.5% for buyers and 4.5–5.5% for sellers, depending on value.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not rely on social media rate charts. Ask the registrar, society or your tax adviser for the rate they will apply, and check the FBR's current withholding tax rate card. Tax is generally calculated on the property's fair market value, which can be higher than the price you agree.

### A worked example (indicative)

For a property valued at Rs20 million, using the reported rates:

- A filer buyer pays about Rs250,000 (1.25%).
- A non-filer buyer pays at least Rs2.1 million (10.5%, the lowest non-filer band).
- A filer seller pays about Rs550,000 (2.75%).

The difference between being a filer and a non-filer can be larger than a property dealer's commission. Our guide on [how to become a filer in Pakistan](/blogs/how-to-become-a-filer-in-pakistan) explains how to get onto the ATL.

## Section 7E removed: the end of tax on deemed property income

Section 7E, introduced in 2022, taxed a deemed income from certain immovable properties, even if they earned no rent. Many owners of second homes and plots disliked it. The FBR's 2026-27 salient features stated that section 7E had been omitted. Some reports also mention a court ruling on its validity, which we could not verify from a primary source.

If you paid 7E for earlier years, the omission does not automatically refund those payments. Ask a tax adviser how it affects your return for the current tax year.

## Late filers and timing

Several practitioners report that the separate, higher property rates for people who appear on the ATL only after filing late were removed in the Finance Act 2026. Even so, timing matters. Your ATL status is checked when the tax is collected, so file your return and confirm you appear on the list before the transfer date.

:::steps Tax steps in a typical property transfer
- Check ATL status | Buyer and seller each confirm they appear on the FBR's Active Taxpayer List.
- Agree value | Check the FBR valuation and the price, since tax is based on fair market value.
- Calculate taxes | Work out 236K, 236C, provincial duties and society fees in writing.
- Pay through PSID | Generate payment slips and pay through a bank or online banking.
- Complete transfer | Present the receipts at the registrar or society to complete the transfer.
- Keep records | Keep receipts, as you will need them for your tax return and any future sale.
:::

## Provincial taxes: stamp duty, CVT and annual property tax

Provinces set stamp duty, registration fees and, where applicable, capital value tax. The annual property tax on buildings in cities such as [Lahore](/cities/lahore), [Karachi](/cities/karachi) and [Islamabad](/cities/islamabad) is collected by the provincial or territorial excise and taxation department, not the FBR. Rates and rebates differ, so check with the relevant department.

:::checklist
- Copies of both parties' CNICs (Computerised National Identity Cards) and ATL status
- Title documents, such as the registry, mutation (intiqal) or society allotment letter
- Latest annual property tax receipt and no-dues certificates for utilities
- Society or authority transfer fee schedule and NOC (no objection certificate)
- Written breakdown of federal taxes, provincial duties and fees
- A lawyer's check of the title chain before any payment
:::

A property lawyer from [lawyers and legal services](/categories/lawyers-legal-services) can verify the title, and [legal documentation](/categories/legal-documentation) services can prepare agreements. If you are renting rather than buying, our guide to [renting a house in Pakistan](/blogs/renting-a-house-in-pakistan-guide) covers rent agreements and verification.

> [!TIP]
> Get the dealer, the seller and yourself to agree in writing who pays which tax and fee. Disputes about who pays CVT or society fees are common on transfer day.

## What the changes mean for the property market

Lower filer rates reduce the cost of moving between properties and give buyers a strong reason to file returns. For non-filers, buying stays expensive, which the government intends as an incentive to register. Whether the cuts revive transaction volumes will depend on interest rates, prices and the wider economy.

## Final word

For filers, the 2026-27 budget made property deals noticeably cheaper on the federal side, and Section 7E has gone. For non-filers, the cost of buying remains very high. Provincial charges and society fees still apply, and rates in different official documents do not fully agree, so confirm them before you pay.

Your next step: check your ATL status today, then ask your adviser for a written tax breakdown before you sign. Current rates are published on the [FBR website](https://www.fbr.gov.pk).

:::faq
### What is the property tax rate for filers in Pakistan in 2026?
After the 2026-27 budget, reported rates for filers are 1.25% advance tax for buyers under Section 236K and 2.75% for sellers under Section 236C, as flat rates. The FBR's salient features summary stated 1.5% for buyers, so confirm the operative rate with the registrar or a tax adviser before paying.

### What property tax do non-filers pay?
Non-filers pay much higher advance tax. Non-filer buyers are reported to remain on value bands of 10.5% to 18.5% under Section 236K, and non-filer sellers pay around 11% to 11.5% under Section 236C. The exact non-filer seller rate differs between sources, so check the FBR rate card for your transaction.

### What is Section 236K?
Section 236K of the Income Tax Ordinance requires advance tax to be collected from the buyer when immovable property is purchased or transferred. It is usually collected by the registrar or housing authority before the transfer. Filers can adjust it against their income tax liability, and the rate is far lower for people on the Active Taxpayer List.

### What is Section 236C?
Section 236C requires advance tax to be collected from the seller when immovable property is sold or transferred. Since the 2026-27 budget, filers are reported to pay a flat 2.75%, replacing banded rates of 4.5% to 5.5%. Non-filers pay considerably more. Sellers may also have capital gains tax to consider on their return.

### Was Section 7E abolished?
Yes, according to the FBR's 2026-27 salient features, Section 7E, which taxed deemed income from certain immovable properties, was omitted. The omission does not automatically refund amounts paid in earlier years. If 7E applied to you previously, ask a tax adviser how the change affects your current and past returns.

### How is property tax calculated in Pakistan?
Federal advance taxes are generally calculated on the property's fair market value, which may be the FBR's notified valuation or the declared price if higher. Rates depend on whether you are a filer or non-filer. Provincial duties, such as stamp duty and CVT, are calculated separately under provincial rules. Get a written breakdown before you pay.

### Who pays stamp duty and CVT on property?
Stamp duty, registration fees and capital value tax are provincial charges, and they are usually paid by the buyer, although parties can agree otherwise. Rates differ between Punjab, Sindh, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Balochistan and Islamabad. Check with the provincial registration or excise department, and write the agreed arrangement into your sale agreement.

### How can I become a filer to pay lower property tax?
Register on the FBR's IRIS portal with your CNIC, mobile number and email to get an NTN, then file your income tax return. Once processed, you appear on the Active Taxpayer List, which the registrar checks at transfer. File and confirm your ATL status well before the transfer date, as it is checked when tax is collected.

### Do overseas Pakistanis pay non-filer property rates?
Overseas Pakistanis have had special treatment under some rules, but conditions apply and have changed over time. Many overseas buyers choose to file returns to secure filer rates. Check the current position in the Income Tax Ordinance or with a tax adviser before buying, and keep proof of your status and the source of funds.

### Is annual property tax paid to the FBR?
No. The annual property tax on buildings in cities is a provincial or territorial tax, collected by the excise and taxation department of Punjab, Sindh, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Balochistan or the Islamabad Capital Territory. The FBR collects federal taxes on purchases and sales. Pay the annual tax on time to avoid surcharges and transfer delays.
:::
