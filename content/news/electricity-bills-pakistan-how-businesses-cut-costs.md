If your **electricity bill in Pakistan** feels impossible to control, you are not imagining it. In 2026 the bill is shaped by a national tariff set in January, new fixed monthly charges per kilowatt of sanctioned load approved in February, monthly fuel adjustments and a long list of taxes. Only part of it depends on how many units you use.

The good news is that the part you control is still the largest for most homes and shops. Shifting usage, fixing your sanctioned load, replacing old appliances and sizing solar correctly can cut bills significantly. This explainer sets out what changed in 2026, who announced it, and what households and businesses are doing about it.

:::takeaways
- NEPRA set a national average tariff of Rs33.38 per unit for calendar year 2026, determined on 7 January 2026, and tariff rebasing now happens every 1 January.
- Protected domestic consumers pay base rates of Rs10.54 (up to 100 units) and Rs13.01 (101–200 units), as reported; non-protected rates are much higher.
- Fixed monthly charges of Rs200–675 per kW of sanctioned load, approved on 11 February 2026, apply to most domestic consumers.
- Officials told a National Assembly panel in June 2026 that Rs620 billion in taxes had been collected through electricity bills.
- The next rebasing is due from 1 January 2027; NEPRA held a hearing on cost projections on 3 November 2026.
:::

## What makes up an electricity bill in Pakistan in 2026

A typical bill from LESCO, IESCO, MEPCO, K-Electric or another distribution company has several layers:

| Component | What it is | Can you reduce it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Energy charge | Units used × your slab rate | Yes, by using fewer units |
| Fixed charge | Rs per kW of sanctioned load, each month | Yes, by reviewing sanctioned load |
| Fuel charges adjustment (FCA) | Monthly correction for fuel costs | No |
| Quarterly adjustment | Correction for other power purchase costs | No |
| Taxes and duties | Sales tax, electricity duty, income tax for some, TV fee | Partly, for example by being on the Active Taxpayer List |

## The 2026 tariff: rebasing moved to January

On 7 January 2026, NEPRA determined distribution company tariffs for calendar year 2026, setting a national average of Rs33.38 per unit, compared with Rs34.00 for 2025-26, Business Recorder reported. Dawn reported that rebasing would now take place every 1 January instead of at the start of the fiscal year, to avoid tariff increases landing in high-consumption summer months.

The federal government notified a uniform tariff on 12 January, effective from 1 January. Dawn reported that consumers did not receive the roughly 62 paisa per unit reduction the regulator had determined.

:::bars Base domestic rates from January 2026 (Rs per unit, as reported)
- Protected, 1–100 units | 10.54 | Rs10.54
- Protected, 101–200 units | 13.01 | Rs13.01
- Non-protected, 1–100 units | 22.44 | Rs22.44
- Non-protected, 101–200 units | 28.91 | Rs28.91
:::

Higher slabs cost more, with the top domestic rate reported at around Rs47–48 per unit. These are base rates before fixed charges, adjustments and taxes.

> [!NOTE]
> "Protected" status generally applies to households that stayed at or below 200 units in each of the previous six months. One month above 200 units can move you to non-protected rates for the following months, so watch summer usage closely.

## Fixed charges per kilowatt: the February 2026 change

On 11 February 2026, NEPRA approved fixed monthly charges for domestic consumers based on sanctioned load. Dawn reported that the charge would be Rs200–675 per kW per month, depending on the consumer's category, and would affect about 28.5 million domestic consumers. Lifeline consumers using up to 100 units are exempt. NEPRA later received a review petition against the determination.

At the same time, the government notified a cut in industrial tariffs. Dawn described the package as relief for industry while shifting some of the burden to domestic bills.

## Taxes on electricity bills

Taxes add a large share to many bills. In June 2026, officials told a National Assembly committee that about Rs620 billion had been collected in taxes through electricity bills, as Profit and Business Recorder reported. Commercial users who are not on the Active Taxpayer List can face extra withholding tax. Getting onto the list by filing returns, explained in our guide on [how to become a filer in Pakistan](/blogs/how-to-become-a-filer-in-pakistan), can reduce this for businesses.

## What comes next: the January 2027 rebasing

The next rebasing for government-owned distribution companies is due to take effect from 1 January 2027. NEPRA scheduled a public hearing on 3 November 2026 on the projections. The Central Power Purchasing Agency (CPPA-G) projected that the power purchase price could reach about Rs27.31 per unit in 2027, but said this should not be read as the final retail tariff, Geo News reported. Watch for NEPRA's determination and the government's notification in December and January.

## How households are cutting electricity bills

Households are combining small habits with larger investments.

- **Stay under 200 units where possible.** Keeping protected status is worth more than almost any single appliance upgrade.
- **Switch to inverter ACs and set them at 26°C.** Cooling is the largest summer load for most homes. Compare [AC and appliance dealers](/categories/ac-appliances) on energy ratings.
- **Replace old fridges, motors and bulbs.** LED lights and efficient fans use a fraction of older models.
- **Check your sanctioned load.** With fixed charges per kW, an oversized sanctioned load costs money every month.
- **Fix wiring faults.** Earth leakage and faulty wiring waste power. Our guide to [finding a reliable electrician](/blogs/how-to-find-a-reliable-electrician-plumber-pakistan) explains what to look for.

For a wider view of household spending, see our [cost of living in Pakistan comparison](/blogs/cost-of-living-in-pakistan-city-comparison).

## How businesses are cutting costs

Shops, offices and factories face higher rates and taxes than homes, so savings add up quickly.

:::checklist
- Read three months of bills and note peak and off-peak use, if you have a time-of-use meter
- Move heavy loads, such as pumps, compressors and laundry, to off-peak hours
- Ask your distribution company whether your sanctioned load and tariff category are correct
- Get an energy audit of motors, compressors and lighting from a qualified engineer
- Compare solar quotes based on daytime self-use, not exports
- File tax returns to avoid extra withholding on commercial bills
:::

Solar remains the most common large investment, but the rules changed in 2026. NEPRA moved new prosumers to net billing with a much lower buyback rate, as explained in our [net metering policy update](/news/solar-net-metering-pakistan-policy-update). Size systems to cover daytime load, and read our [solar panel installation guide](/blogs/solar-panel-installation-guide-pakistan) before buying. Compare [solar energy companies](/categories/solar-energy) and [power backup suppliers](/categories/power-backup) on PakistanYP.

> [!TIP]
> If a bill looks wrong, take a photo of your meter reading on the billing date and compare it with the bill. Complain to your distribution company first, and escalate to NEPRA's complaint system if it is not resolved.

> [!WARNING]
> Be wary of devices sold as "electricity savers" that plug into a socket. Real savings come from efficient appliances, correct load and solar, not from gadgets with unverified claims.

## Final word

The electricity bill in Pakistan is now built from a national tariff set each January, a fixed charge tied to your sanctioned load, monthly adjustments and taxes. You cannot change the tariff, but you can control units, load, timing and tax status.

Your next step: pull out your last three bills, check your sanctioned load and slab, and list your three biggest loads. Then compare local electricians and solar installers in your city, such as [Faisalabad](/cities/faisalabad) or [Karachi](/cities/karachi). Official tariff decisions are published by [NEPRA](https://nepra.org.pk).

### Why is my electricity bill so high in Pakistan?
Bills combine your energy charge with fixed charges per kilowatt of sanctioned load, monthly fuel cost adjustments, quarterly adjustments and taxes. In 2026, NEPRA set a national average tariff of Rs33.38 per unit, and fixed charges of Rs200–675 per kW were approved in February. Crossing 200 units can also move you to much higher non-protected rates.

### What is the per unit electricity price in Pakistan in 2026?
Rates depend on your slab. As reported for January 2026, protected domestic consumers pay Rs10.54 per unit for up to 100 units and Rs13.01 for 101–200 units, while non-protected consumers pay Rs22.44 and Rs28.91 for the same slabs. Higher slabs cost more. Taxes and adjustments are added on top.

### What is a protected consumer?
A protected consumer is a domestic user whose consumption has generally stayed at or below 200 units in each of the previous six months. Protected consumers pay much lower slab rates. Using more than 200 units in a single month can remove protected status for the following months, so many households manage summer usage carefully.

### What are fixed charges on electricity bills?
Fixed charges are a monthly amount based on your sanctioned load in kilowatts, regardless of how many units you use. NEPRA approved them on 11 February 2026 at a reported Rs200–675 per kW per month, depending on category. Lifeline consumers using up to 100 units are exempt. Reviewing an oversized sanctioned load can lower this charge.

### How can I reduce my sanctioned load?
Apply to your distribution company to reduce the sanctioned load on your connection, usually with a form, your CNIC and a recent bill. The company may inspect your connected load. Make sure the new figure still covers your real peak demand, because exceeding sanctioned load can lead to penalties or tripping.

### When will electricity tariffs change next?
Rebasing now happens on 1 January each year. The next rebasing for government-owned distribution companies is due from 1 January 2027, and NEPRA held a hearing on cost projections on 3 November 2026. Monthly fuel adjustments and quarterly adjustments can also change your bill between rebasings.

### How much tax is collected through electricity bills?
Officials told a National Assembly committee in June 2026 that about Rs620 billion had been collected in taxes through electricity bills, as Profit and Business Recorder reported. Bills can include sales tax, electricity duty, income tax for some commercial users and other charges. Filing tax returns can reduce some withholding for businesses.

### Does solar still reduce electricity bills?
Yes, mainly by replacing grid units you would otherwise buy during the day. Since February 2026, new solar users are on net billing, where exported units earn a much lower rate than grid units cost. Systems sized for daytime self-consumption give the best savings. Fixed charges still apply regardless of solar output.

### How do I complain about overbilling?
Start with your distribution company's complaint centre or helpline, with a photo of your meter reading and a copy of the bill. Keep the complaint number. If the problem is not resolved, you can escalate to NEPRA through its consumer complaint system. Pay any undisputed amount on time to avoid disconnection.

### What uses the most electricity in a Pakistani home?
In summer, air conditioners are usually the biggest load, followed by water pumps, refrigerators, irons and older ceiling fans. Inverter ACs set around 26°C, efficient fans and LED lights can cut use noticeably. Running pumps and washing machines during daylight hours also helps if you have solar.
