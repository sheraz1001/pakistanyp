**Net metering in Pakistan** changed fundamentally in February 2026. The National Electric Power Regulatory Authority (NEPRA) notified the Prosumer Regulations, 2026, which move rooftop solar users from net metering, where exported units offset imported units one for one, to **net billing**, where exported units are bought at a much lower rate than you pay for grid power.

For buyers, the direct answer is this: solar still cuts bills, but the payback now depends far more on using your own solar power during the day than on selling units back to the grid. Protection for people who already had net metering contracts was promised after the rules were issued, but the final legal position should be confirmed with NEPRA and your distribution company before you rely on it.

:::takeaways
- NEPRA notified the Prosumer Regulations, 2026 around 9–10 February 2026, replacing net metering with net billing for prosumers.
- Reported buyback rates fell from roughly Rs26 per unit to about Rs10–11 per unit, and new agreements run for five years rather than seven.
- After the Prime Minister's intervention, NEPRA issued a draft amendment in February 2026 to keep existing contracts on the old terms until they expire.
- We could not confirm a final, notified version of that amendment; check NEPRA's website before relying on it.
- New fixed monthly charges per kilowatt of sanctioned load, approved in February 2026, also affect solar households.
:::

## How net metering in Pakistan worked before 2026

Under the framework NEPRA introduced in 2015, a home or business with rooftop solar could install a bidirectional meter and export surplus units to its distribution company (DISCO), such as LESCO, IESCO or K-Electric. Exported units were netted against imported units, and any surplus was credited at a buyback rate tied to the national average power purchase price, reported at around Rs26–27 per unit before 2026.

That made a large system attractive: daytime exports earned credit that offset expensive evening imports. Solar adoption grew rapidly, especially in cities such as [Lahore](/cities/lahore), [Faisalabad](/cities/faisalabad) and [Multan](/cities/multan).

## NEPRA's Prosumer Regulations 2026: what changed

Dawn reported that the regulations were notified without changes after a public hearing. The main differences, as reported in the press, are below.

| Feature | Old net metering (pre-2026) | Net billing under Prosumer Regulations 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How exports are treated | Netted against imports unit for unit | Bought at a separate, lower rate |
| Buyback rate | About Rs26–27 per unit, as reported | About Rs10–11 per unit, as reported |
| Imports | Billed after netting | Billed at the normal tariff |
| Agreement term | Seven years | Five years for new contracts |
| Export credit period | Three months, as reported | One month, as reported |

:::bars What a solar unit is worth (Rs per unit, indicative, as reported)
- Old net metering buyback | 26 | about Rs26
- New net billing buyback | 11 | about Rs10–11
- Grid import, low end | 37 | about Rs37
- Grid import, high end | 55 | about Rs55
:::

> [!NOTE]
> Buyback rates, tariffs and settlement rules are set in NEPRA determinations and can be revised. The figures above come from press reports of the regulations, not from your bill. Check the current rate with your DISCO.

## What happens to existing net metering users

This is the most contested part. As first notified, the regulations kept existing licences and agreements valid, but reports said billing would move to the net billing method from the next billing cycle.

The government then stepped in:

:::steps How the 2026 net metering dispute unfolded
- 9–10 February 2026 | NEPRA notified the Prosumer Regulations, 2026, moving prosumers to net billing.
- Mid-February 2026 | Geo News reported the Prime Minister had ordered that net billing not apply to existing consumers for now.
- February 2026 | Business Recorder reported the Power Division had asked NEPRA to review the regulations.
- About 16 February 2026 | NEPRA issued a draft amendment keeping existing agreements on the old rate until expiry, open for 30 days of comments.
- Late August 2026 | NEPRA directed PITC to update billing for net metering applications pending before 9 February 2026.
:::

Under the draft amendment, as reported by Express Tribune and ProPakistani, distributed generators with valid agreements would continue to be billed under the earlier rate and mechanism until their contracts expire. We have not found the final notified amendment, so its exact wording and status remain unconfirmed at the time of writing.

> [!WARNING]
> If you already have net metering, keep a copy of your agreement and its expiry date. When it ends, any renewal is expected to follow the new net billing terms. If your bill suddenly shows exports credited at a lower rate, raise a complaint with your DISCO and, if unresolved, with NEPRA.

## Fixed charges: the other change on solar bills

On 11 February 2026, NEPRA approved fixed monthly charges for domestic consumers, billed per kilowatt of sanctioned load rather than per unit used. Dawn and other outlets reported a range of Rs200 to Rs675 per kW a month depending on the consumer's slab, with lifeline users (up to 100 units) exempt. A review petition was later filed against the determination.

For solar households this matters because the charge does not fall when your solar output rises. A home with a 5 kW sanctioned load pays the fixed charge even in a month when solar covers most of its use. Review your sanctioned load: if it is far higher than you need, ask your DISCO whether it can be reduced.

## What the net metering Pakistan changes mean for solar buyers

Net billing does not make solar pointless. It changes how you size a system.

- **Size for self-consumption.** Match panels to your daytime load, such as air conditioners, pumps, fridges and office equipment, rather than oversizing to export.
- **Consider batteries carefully.** Storage lets you use daytime solar after sunset, when grid power is expensive, but adds cost and needs replacement. Compare [power backup and battery suppliers](/categories/power-backup).
- **Shift heavy loads to daytime.** Running a water pump or washing machine at midday is worth more than exporting the same units.
- **Check payback with today's numbers.** A quote built on the old Rs26 buyback rate is out of date.

## Questions to ask a solar installer now

A good installer should explain net metering Pakistan rules as they stand today, not as they were in 2025.

:::checklist
- Which NEPRA rules will my new connection fall under, and what buyback rate are you assuming?
- How many units will I use myself versus export, by season?
- Is the installer registered with the relevant authority to handle net metering or net billing applications?
- What inverter, panel and battery warranties apply, and who honours them in Pakistan?
- What does the quote include: structure, wiring, earthing, meter application and DISCO fees?
- How long will the DISCO application take, and who follows it up?

## Final word

The era of selling solar units back at near-retail prices is over for new connections. Existing users were promised protection until their contracts expire, but you should confirm the final amendment with NEPRA. For new buyers, solar remains worth considering, provided the system is sized for daytime use and the quote reflects current rates.

Your next step: check your sanctioned load and current bill, then get two or three quotes from local installers. The official regulations and determinations are published on the [NEPRA website](https://nepra.org.pk).

:::faq
### Is net metering in Pakistan still available?
Not for new applicants in its old form. NEPRA's Prosumer Regulations, 2026, notified in February 2026, replaced net metering with net billing, under which exports are bought at a lower rate than grid imports. Existing agreements were promised protection until expiry through a draft amendment. Check with your distribution company which terms apply to your connection.

### What is net billing?
Net billing means the units you import from the grid and the units you export are priced separately. Imports are charged at your normal tariff, while exports are bought at a lower rate set by NEPRA, reported at about Rs10–11 per unit in 2026. Under old net metering, exported units offset imported units one for one.

### What is the new solar buyback rate in Pakistan?
Press reports of the Prosumer Regulations, 2026 put the buyback rate for new net billing users at roughly Rs10–11 per unit, down from about Rs26 under net metering. The rate is linked to a NEPRA-determined benchmark and can change. Ask your DISCO for the current figure, as some unofficial sites quote different numbers.

### Will existing net metering users be moved to net billing?
After the regulations were notified, the Prime Minister ordered that existing consumers not be moved for now, and NEPRA issued a draft amendment in February 2026 keeping valid agreements on old terms until they expire. We could not confirm the final notified amendment. Keep your agreement and check NEPRA's website for the current text.

### How long is a net billing agreement?
New agreements under the Prosumer Regulations, 2026 are reported to run for five years, compared with seven years under the earlier net metering regulations. Renewal terms will depend on the rules in force at the time. Check the term and expiry date written on your own agreement with the distribution company.

### Is solar still worth it in Pakistan after net billing?
For many homes and businesses it can be, but the payback is longer if you rely on exports. Systems sized to cover daytime use, such as cooling, pumps and office loads, still save money at grid prices reported between about Rs37 and Rs55 per unit. Get quotes based on current buyback rates, not 2025 assumptions.

### Do solar users pay fixed charges on electricity bills?
Yes. In February 2026 NEPRA approved fixed monthly charges for most domestic consumers, billed per kilowatt of sanctioned load, reportedly between Rs200 and Rs675 per kW depending on the slab. Lifeline consumers using up to 100 units are exempt. The charge applies regardless of how much solar you generate.

### How do I apply for net billing?
Applications go through your distribution company, usually with help from your installer. You will typically need your CNIC, a recent electricity bill, system specifications and equipment details. The DISCO inspects the system and installs a bidirectional or green meter. Ask the installer to confirm the current process and fees before you sign.

### Does K-Electric follow the same net metering rules?
K-Electric is regulated by NEPRA, and the Prosumer Regulations, 2026 apply to distribution licensees including K-Electric. Its application process and billing systems may differ in practice from those of government-owned DISCOs. Karachi customers should check K-Electric's own guidance and current buyback rate before installing.

### Where can I check the official net metering rules?
NEPRA publishes its regulations, determinations and notices on its official website. Your distribution company can confirm how they apply to your meter. Treat installer brochures and unofficial websites with care, because many still quote the old net metering buyback rates or unconfirmed figures.
:::
